# 10. Robots: AI, routines and crowds

One of the [system guides](../Systems.md); read [How to use this document](../Systems.md#how-to-use-this-document) first.

**Status:** Hob's body built 2026-11-26 (designed 2026-11-25) ([Roadmap.md, 4.4](../Roadmap.md#44-the-caretaker-robot)), on the game designer's recommended options until @DunderGG answers: `AVestigeNpcCharacter` (`BP_Hob`), standing at its spot all day, turning to face the player during a talk, with a placeholder body. Schedules, walking, Smart Objects and StateTree come with Hob's schedule; the town is milestone 3.

**References:** Epic's "StateTree", "Behavior Trees", "Smart Objects", "AI Perception", "Mass Entity", "Significance Manager" and "AI Controllers" pages (`AAIController::SetFocus` and `SetFocalPoint`, which set the controller's rotation toward a target in its tick, and `UCharacterMovementComponent::bUseControllerDesiredRotation` with `RotationRate`, which turns the body toward it smoothly). Lyra's bots use behavior trees with an `AAIController` (`ALyraPlayerBotController`); StateTree is Epic's newer general-purpose state machine, built for Unreal Engine 5, and the one we choose for new AI.

**Take, skip, change from Lyra and Epic:**

- **Take:** the body is a character with components and an engine AI controller possesses it (`AutoPossessAI`), so walking with `MoveTo` and a StateTree later need no change of class; facing through the controller's focus, Epic's way of making an AI look at something.
- **Skip for now:** StateTree, behavior trees, perception, navigation, Smart Objects and a controller class of our own. Hob stands at one spot and only turns, which the engine's `AAIController` does as it is. StateTree (the `StateTreeModule` and `GameplayStateTreeModule` dependencies, the plugin and its decision log entry) comes with the first behaviour that has states: Hob's schedule (where they are at what hour) or the tutorial's guiding.
- **Change:** the robot's spot is where the level places it, not a marker of its own: the placed `BP_Hob` is the spot, and its placed rotation is the facing it turns back to. With the schedule, the spot becomes a Smart Object or a target named in the schedule data ([Levels/HomeFarm/README.md](../Levels/HomeFarm/README.md), item 2), and the placed actor becomes a spawn.

**`AVestigeNpcCharacter : ACharacter, IVestigeInteractable`** (`Characters/`), the body of every named character, robot or human, that the player can talk to. One job: being that character in the world. `BP_Hob` (`Content/Vestige/Characters/`) is Hob.

- **Which character:** `CharacterId`, an `FPrimaryAssetId` (`AllowedTypes = "VestigeCharacter"`, `EditDefaultsOnly`, checked in data validation), `VestigeCharacter:Base.Hob` on `BP_Hob`. `BeginPlay` finds its `UVestigeCharacterData` in the catalogue ([section 11](11-Dialogue.md), the characters' data) and keeps it; a missing one logs a warning (`LogVestigeDialogue`) and the body offers nothing.
- **Talking:** an `InteractionBox` (the `VestigeInteractable` profile, overlap events off, no navigation effect, as the bed's), a little larger than the body, so the player's facing ray finds it ([section 7](07-Interaction.md)). `GetInteractionOptions` offers one option, `{Text "Talk to Hob", Action "Talk"}`, when the interactor has a conversation component and no talk is running. The text is `FText::Format(LOCTEXT("TalkTo", "Talk to {Name}"), DisplayName)`, built once in `BeginPlay`, so the five-times-a-second ask copies an `FText` and nothing more. `Interact` with `Talk` asks the interactor's `UVestigeConversationComponent::StartConversation(*this, Data)`; when it starts, the body faces the interactor and listens to that component's `OnConversationEnded` once, keeping the component as a weak pointer; the end handler unbinds it, and `EndPlay` unbinds it too if the body goes first.
- **Facing:** `bUseControllerRotationYaw` false; the movement component's `bOrientRotationToMovement` false, `bUseControllerDesiredRotation` true and `RotationRate.Yaw` `TurnDegreesPerSecond` (600, so a half turn takes 0.3 s, the designer's value). A talk's start calls `SetFocus(Interactor)` on its `AAIController`; its end calls `SetFocalPoint` a metre ahead along the placed facing (`HomeRotation`, kept in `BeginPlay`), so the body turns back. Both use the default priority, `EAIFocusPriority::Gameplay`, so the focal point replaces the focus actor; no `ClearFocus` is needed (in 5.8 it takes a priority and has no default). `BeginPlay` sets the same focal point, so Hob holds the placed facing from the start. Checked in the 5.8 source: `AAIController::SetFocus`, `SetFocalPoint`, `UpdateControlRotation` (run in the controller's tick), `UCharacterMovementComponent::bUseControllerDesiredRotation` and `RotationRate`, `EAutoPossessAI::PlacedInWorldOrSpawned`. Without a controller (a test that doesn't possess it) nothing turns, and nothing fails.
- **Possession:** `AIControllerClass` is the engine's `AAIController`, `AutoPossessAI` is `PlacedInWorldOrSpawned`. `AIModule` joins the private dependencies in `Vestige.Build.cs`.
- **Solid:** the capsule blocks pawns as a character's does, so the player walks round. Capsule 35 by 75 cm half-height for a 1.5 m body.
- **Idle:** presentation only, no C++: the face screen's eyes blink in its material (a time-driven mask, no tick), and any sway on the placeholder is the artist's choice within the content rules ([Architecture.md, section 10](../Architecture.md#10-performance-by-design)); with the Blender model it becomes the idle animation ([section 13](13-Animation.md)).
- **The placeholder body:** in `BP_Hob`, from engine basic shapes: a body block and a boxy head about 1.5 m tall in all, cream paint with olive panels, a dark face screen with two cyan eye bars as in the concept art (emissive, so Hob is found after dark), a cyan visibly greener than water's azure `58AEFF`: @DunderGG's choice (2026-11-25), an approved exception to the colour cue rule, recorded in [GameDesign.md, section 15](../GameDesign.md#15-open-questions), material instances of `M_Prop` ([Art/README.md](../../Art/README.md#importing-into-unreal)). The Blender model replaces the shapes in the same Blueprint.
- **Saving:** nothing. Hob's state is the level's (where it stands) and the relationship component's (how the player stands with Hob).

**Placing Hob** in `L_Farm`: one `BP_Hob`, at (500, -350), outside the field's 1.5 m apron, about 6 m from the Player Start and 35 degrees off its facing, turned toward the Player Start, in view from it, with room for the farmer to stand facing Hob. This is not yet the farmhouse side of the plan: the map has no house, and the Player Start sits at the field's west edge facing +X (east), so any spot west of the field would be behind the camera. The home farm layout item moves the start and the actor beside the door, and the test below then checks west of the field.

**Performance:** one character's movement component and one AI controller tick, which is negligible for Hob. When the town has many robots, these ticks are what significance turns down first (tick intervals, movement off while standing).

**Tests** (`Vestige.Robots.*`, with the real `BP_Hob` and the catalogue):

- `HobIsTheCharacterBaseHob` (the Blueprint's `CharacterId`), `HobOffersTalkToHob` ("Talk to Hob", from the display name), `HobOffersNothingDuringATalk`, `HobOffersNothingWithoutAConversationComponent`, `TalkingToHobStartsTheConversation`, `HobTurnsToTheFarmerAndBack` (the controller's focus actor during the talk, the focal point ahead of the placed facing after), `HobBlocksThePlayer` (the capsule blocks `Pawn`), `AnUnknownCharacterOffersNothing`.
- `Vestige.Level.HobStandsOffTheFieldInViewOfTheStart`: `L_Farm` has exactly one `BP_Hob`; it stands outside the field's bounds grown by 150 cm, within 15 m of the Player Start and within 45 degrees of its facing.

**Rules:**

- **AI controller plus StateTree** for robot behaviour with states. The robot pawn is a body with components; the controller and its StateTree decide what to do. Log the choice of StateTree over behavior trees when the first such behaviour is built.
- **Daily routines are data.** A robot's schedule (where it is at what hour, by season and weather) lives in its character data, not in the tree. The tree runs "go to the next activity" generically.
- **Smart Objects for places robots use** (a bench, a pump to repair, a shop counter, Hob's spot once Hob has a schedule): the place says what can be done there, and robots claim slots. This suits robots repeating pre-war routines and lets mods add places without touching AI code.
- **Robot definitions are content** (`UVestigeCharacterData`, primary asset type `VestigeCharacter`, for humans and robots alike), with the ID (`Base.Hob`, no category segment: [Architecture.md, section 5](../Architecture.md#5-data-and-content)) and the name; schedule and relationship data are added with their items, and conversations name their character ([section 11](11-Dialogue.md)). Whether a character is a robot or a human is a gameplay tag, not a separate type.
- **Facing through the controller's focus** with the movement component's desired rotation, never by setting the actor's rotation each frame.
- **Scale by significance.** Robots far away or out of sight update less often (tick interval, perception off, animation update rate optimization). When the town has many simple background robots, consider Mass for them and keep full actors for the named characters.
- Perception (sight, hearing) only for robots that need it, and not every frame.

**Pitfalls:**

- Behaviour logic in the robot's Blueprint event graph or `Tick`.
- Pathfinding on every frame: move with `MoveTo` requests and react to completion events.
- `ACharacter`'s default `bUseControllerRotationYaw` (true) snaps the body to the controller's rotation at once; turn it off and let the movement component turn it at its rate.
- A placed character with no controller: `AutoPossessAI` defaults to placed-in-world only, so a spawned one gets none unless it is `PlacedInWorldOrSpawned`.
