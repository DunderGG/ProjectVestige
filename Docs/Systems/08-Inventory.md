# 8. Inventory and items

One of the [system guides](../Systems.md); read [How to use this document](../Systems.md#how-to-use-this-document) first.

**Status:** built in three steps: item definitions and their fragments (approved by David on 2026-10-30), the inventory on the player state (approved by David on 2026-10-31), and the hotbar with the item in hand ([CodeReview.md, H2](../CodeReview.md#h2--ufarmingcomponent-does-seven-jobs-and-can-plant-only-one-seed), 2026-11-07, approved by a senior Unreal C++ reviewer agent after three rounds of review, whose approval counts as David's). Four items so far: `Base.TurnipSeeds`, `Base.Turnip`, `Base.Hoe` and `Base.WateringCan`; the player starts with a hoe, 10 turnip seeds and a watering can. Money waits for the shipping bin.

**References:** Epic's "Data Assets" and "Asset Management" pages, and `UAssetManager::InitializeAssetBundlesFromMetadata`, which also follows instanced subobjects, so a soft reference inside a fragment joins its item's asset bundles. Lyra's inventory (`Source/LyraGame/Inventory`): `ULyraInventoryItemDefinition` (an item made of **fragments**, small `DefaultToInstanced, EditInlineNew` objects each adding one aspect such as a pickup icon, a quick bar icon, stats or equipment, found with `FindFragmentByClass`), `ULyraInventoryItemInstance` (one item the player has, with stats), `ULyraInventoryManagerComponent` (the list) and a separate equipment manager. For the hotbar, Lyra's `ULyraQuickBarComponent` (`Source/LyraGame/Equipment`): a controller component with `NumSlots` (3) slots that point at inventory item instances and an `ActiveSlotIndex`; `SetActiveSlotIndex` unequips the old slot's item and equips the new one when it has a `UInventoryFragment_EquippableItem`, whose `EquipmentDefinition` the pawn's `ULyraEquipmentManagerComponent` equips, granting its ability sets with the item instance as their source. Epic's `APawn::ReceiveControllerChangedDelegate`, and `UAbilitySystemComponent::GiveAbility`, `ClearAbility` and `FindAbilitySpecFromHandle` while the ability list is locked (below). Engine behaviour below was checked in the 5.8 source.

**What we take from Lyra and what we skip:**

- **Take:** fragments for what only some items have; `FindFragmentByClass` as the one way to ask for one; definitions without runtime state; the inventory as a component apart from equipment (the tool in hand). For the hotbar: an active slot whose item is what the player holds; an item that is used through a fragment naming a separate definition of how it is used (Lyra's equippable fragment names an equipment definition, ours names a tool asset), so every seed item shares the one Seeds tool; equipping on the pawn by granting the definition's ability while the item is held and taking it back when it isn't.
- **Skip:** definitions as Blueprint classes (Lyra's items are `TSubclassOf<ULyraInventoryItemDefinition>`). Ours are data assets of a primary asset type, found by `ContentId` like crops and tools, so saves and mods name them the same way ([section 2](02-Content.md)). A quick bar with slots of its own that point into the inventory: ours is the inventory's first slots, as in farming games, so the hotbar and the bag can't disagree and moving an item is one change. The controller component: the selection lives with the slots on the player state, so it is saved with them and outlives the pawn. Replication and gameplay messages; C++ delegates as everywhere else.
- **Skip until needed:** item instance objects and `OnInstanceCreated` (only for items with their own state, such as a tool's durability); stats as tag stacks (Lyra counts ammo that way; our stacks are an ID and a count); the replicated `FFastArraySerializer` list (single-player).

**Rules for item definitions:**

- **One asset per item:** `UVestigeItemData`, a `UVestigeContentData` subclass of primary asset type `VestigeItem`, named `DA_Item_<Name>` in `Content/Vestige/Items`, always cooked. Seeds, harvested crops, materials, tools and later food are items.
- **What every item has** sits on `UVestigeItemData`: the ID and display name from the base class, `MaxStackSize` (how many fit in one inventory slot; 1 for items that don't stack) and `SellPrice` (what selling one pays; unused until there is currency). Nothing else goes on the base class.
- **What only some items have is a fragment:** a subclass of the abstract `UVestigeItemFragment` (`DefaultToInstanced, EditInlineNew`), in the item's `Fragments` list (`UPROPERTY(EditDefaultsOnly, Instanced)`). Code asks with `FindFragment<T>()`, a linear search over the few fragments an item has. A new kind of item is a new fragment, never a subclass of `UVestigeItemData` and never a `switch` over item types.
- **The seed fragment, `UFarmSeedFragment`:** its `CropId` (`FPrimaryAssetId`, limited to `FarmCrop`) is the crop that planting the item grows.
- **The tool fragment, `UVestigeToolFragment`:** its `ToolId` (`FPrimaryAssetId`, limited to `VestigeTool`) is the tool holding the item uses. The hoe and the watering can are items with one (`DA_Item_Hoe`, `DA_Item_WateringCan`, stack size 1), and every seed item has one naming `VestigeTool:Base.Seeds` next to its seed fragment; a seed without it can't be planted. Item IDs may reuse a tool's name (`VestigeItem:Base.Hoe`): IDs are unique per content type.
- **Crops name the item they yield:** `UFarmCropData::HarvestItemId` (`FPrimaryAssetId`, limited to `VestigeItem`). `Yield` stays on the crop, because how many grow is the crop's rule.
- **Prices belong to items, not crops:** the sell price is on the harvested item and on the seed item, and crops have none. What a shop charges is the shop's stock data, decided when shops are built, so two shops can price the same item differently.
- **Content refers to content by ID,** never by a hard pointer ([section 2](02-Content.md), pitfalls), so loading a seed item doesn't load its crop's meshes, and a missing crop is a skipped ID rather than a broken asset.
- **Fragments come with the system that reads them:** a food fragment with stamina, a placeable fragment with placement ([section 16](16-PlacingAndBuilding.md)), an icon (a soft texture in the `Icons` bundle, which the content catalogue never loads; [section 2](02-Content.md)) with the hotbar row, and a description with the inventory screen. Item category tags wait for the shipping bin or gifts.
- **Data validation** (`IsDataValid`) on top of the ID check: `MaxStackSize` at least 1, no empty fragment entries, at most one fragment of each class, and each fragment checks its own fields (a seed's `CropId` and a tool fragment's `ToolId` are set and name content the Asset Manager knows). `HarvestItemId` on a crop is checked the same way.
- **Loading:** items have no meshes or textures yet, so a definition is a few hundred bytes. Every definition is loaded once per game by the content catalogue ([section 2](02-Content.md)), and the rule from [CodingGuidelines.md, section 12](../CodingGuidelines.md#12-performance) stands: an item that can enter the inventory during play (a harvest) has its definition loaded beforehand, never synchronously at that moment.

**Rules for the inventory:**

- **The inventory is a component on the player state,** so it outlives the pawn ([Architecture.md, section 2](../Architecture.md#2-unreals-gameplay-framework-where-things-go)). `AVestigePlayerState`, in `Player/`, holds `UVestigeInventoryComponent`, the ability system component ([section 9](09-AbilitySystem.md)) and the rest component, each created in its constructor. `AVestigeGameMode` sets it as the player state class, and `BP_PlayerState`, a Blueprint subclass in `Content/Vestige/Blueprints`, holds the values (the starting items), as the farmer Blueprint does for the farmer. `BP_GameMode` names it.
- **The rules sit in a plain struct, `FVestigeInventory`,** as the tile's rules sit in `FFarmTileState`, so they are tested without a world. The component wraps it with events and saving. Both live in the layer folder `Inventory/`, between `Time/` and `Farming/`: the farming component uses the inventory, and the inventory knows nothing about farming or tools. Nothing below `Player/` knows `AVestigePlayerState`; the farming component and the save subsystem find the inventory with `FindComponentByClass` on the player's `APlayerState`.
- **A fixed number of slots** (`SlotCount`, 24, on the component's defaults). A slot is empty or holds one item definition and a count from 1 to the item's `MaxStackSize`. A bigger bag later raises the count.
- **Adding and removing are all or nothing.** `TryAddItem(ItemId, Count)` first tops up stacks of the same item in slot order, then fills empty slots in order, and changes nothing if the whole count doesn't fit. `TryRemoveItem(ItemId, Count)` takes from the last stacks of that item first and changes nothing if there aren't enough. `TryRemoveItemFromSlot(Slot, Count)` (over `FVestigeInventory::TryRemoveFromSlot`) removes from one slot only, all or nothing. `GetItemCount(ItemId)` sums the stacks, and `CanAddItem` asks without changing anything. Callers that turn one thing into another (a ripe crop into items) ask first, so a full bag leaves the crop in the ground instead of destroying it. Partial adds wait for a caller that needs them (picking up a pile).
- **The API takes item IDs** (`FPrimaryAssetId` of type `VestigeItem`), the form content uses for references (`HarvestItemId`). An ID that names no item is refused with a warning.
- **Loading:** the component loads nothing. It finds item definitions in the content catalogue ([section 2](02-Content.md)), which loaded them all before play, so adding an item during play never waits on a load ([CodingGuidelines.md, section 12](../CodingGuidelines.md#12-performance)), and a chest or the shipping bin shares the same definitions. Definitions have no meshes or textures (an icon will be in the `Icons` bundle, loaded by the UI that shows it), so all of them together stay small. Revisit, with a measurement, if items ever get heavy or number in the thousands.
- **Starting items** (`StartingItems`, a list of `FVestigeItemStack` item IDs and counts on the component) are added at `BeginPlay`. On `BP_PlayerState` they are a hoe, 10 turnip seeds and a watering can, in that order, so keys 1, 2 and 3 are the hoe, the seeds and the can. Loading a save replaces them.
- **One event, `OnInventoryChanged`** (`FOnVestigeInventoryChanged`, a C++ multicast delegate with no parameters), broadcast after every change, including a restore. The HUD listens to it on the player state, not on the pawn.
- **Seeds and the harvest are items.** `UFarmingComponent` uses what is in hand and has no counts of its own. Planting plants the seed item in hand and removes one from its slot (the hotbar, below); harvesting adds the crop's `Yield` of its `HarvestItemId`, and if the bag can't take them, the crop stays and the player is told ("No room in your bag."). The abilities ask for the inventory on each use, never at `BeginPlay`.
- **Money is not built yet.** Nothing earns or spends it until the shipping bin and shops. It arrives with the first of them, as its own decision (a count on the inventory component or a small wallet component on the player state), rather than as an unused field now.
- **Saving:** the component returns an `FVestigeInventorySaveData` (in `Inventory/`) holding one entry per non-empty slot (the slot index, the item's `ContentId` and the count) and the selected hotbar slot. Restoring clears the slots and puts each entry back in its slot. An entry naming an unknown item is skipped with a warning and counted as missing content. A count above the item's stack size (its `MaxStackSize` was lowered, or a mod changed it) fills its slot and the rest goes in as a normal add; what doesn't fit is dropped with a warning and counted as missing. A bad slot index goes in as a normal add, and a saved selection outside the hotbar selects slot 0, with a warning. The layout is version 2: a version 1 bag held no hoe or watering can, so the registry leaves it out and the player gets the starting items.
- **The HUD** shows the non-empty slots as "Bag: Turnip Seeds 9   Turnip 2", by display name, rebuilt on `OnInventoryChanged`, until the UMG inventory screen replaces the line.
- **Performance:** no tick. Adding, removing and counting walk the 24 slots, only on a player action.
- **Tests:** `Vestige.Inventory.*`, `Vestige.Item.*` and the planting and harvesting tests in `Vestige.Farming`. `ThePlayerStartsWithAHoeSeedsAndAWateringCan` checks the game mode's player state class has those items in slots 0 to 2 with slot 0 in hand, and `Vestige.Content.EverySeedItemNamesATool` checks every seed item's tool fragment, so a missing editor step fails a test.

**Rules for the hotbar and the item in hand:**

- **The hotbar is the first slots of the bag:** the first `GetHotbarSlotCount()` slots, which is `HotbarSlotCount` (9 on the component's defaults) but never more than the slots there are (tests reset to small inventories). Adding items fills empty slots in order, so a new item goes to the hotbar while it has room. `SelectHotbarSlot(Slot)` selects a hotbar slot, and returns false and changes nothing for a slot outside the hotbar. `GetSelectedHotbarSlot()` and `GetItemInHand()` (the selected slot's item definition, or nullptr when the slot is empty) read it. A new game, a reset and a restore start from slot 0. An inventory that isn't the player's (a chest, later) sets `HotbarSlotCount` to 0: it has no hand, its selected slot is `INDEX_NONE` and it never broadcasts the hand's event, which is simpler than a separate hotbar component for the one inventory that has one.
- **Stepping.** `StepHotbarSlot(Direction)` selects the next or previous hotbar slot by the sign of `Direction`, wrapping around and counting empty slots, through `SelectHotbarSlot`; from no selection it starts at the first slot (next) or the last (previous). It returns false, changing nothing, for a `Direction` of 0 or an inventory without a hotbar. Which slot is selected is the inventory's rule: keys 1 to 9 call the character's `SelectHotbarSlot`, and the mouse wheel and the gamepad shoulders its `StepHotbarSlot` ([section 5](05-Input.md)), each forwarding to the inventory on its player state and doing nothing without one. Slots past the ninth are reached by stepping.
- **One event for the hand.** `OnItemInHandChanged` (a C++ multicast delegate without parameters, like `OnInventoryChanged`) fires when the selected slot changes or the item in it changes: the slot empties (the last seed planted), fills (a harvest lands in the empty selected slot), or a restore, a reset or `StartNewGame` replaces it. A change of count alone doesn't fire it. The component remembers the slot and item of its last broadcast and compares them after every change, and broadcasts after `OnInventoryChanged`, so a listener to both sees the slots already updated. It updates what it remembers before it broadcasts, so a listener that changes the inventory again (selects another slot from the handler) is compared against the latest state and the component never broadcasts a change twice. That doesn't order what listeners see: a multicast `Broadcast` calls a copy of its listener list from last to first (`MulticastDelegateBase.h`), and the list is compacted with `RemoveAtSwap`, so during a nested broadcast a listener the outer one hasn't reached yet is called for the newer change first and the older one after. So the event carries no value, and every listener reads `GetSelectedHotbarSlot()` and `GetItemInHand()` when it is called, which is always the latest state. The farming component's `OnToolChanged` has no parameter for the same reason: the character's handler shows `Farming->GetCurrentTool()`'s held mesh instead of trusting a passed tool. The comparison is one index and one pointer.
- **Which tool is in hand.** `UFarmingComponent::GetCurrentTool()` asks the inventory each time: the tool its item in hand's tool fragment names, found in the content catalogue. An empty slot, an item without a tool fragment, or one whose tool doesn't exist is no tool; pressing E then says "Nothing in hand to use.", with a warning in the log for a tool that doesn't exist. Harvesting is F's, through the harvest tool the farming component always grants ([section 7](07-Interaction.md)). The farming component reaches the inventory through its pawn's player state (`FindComponentByClass`, as the HUD does) and keeps only a weak pointer to the inventory it listens to, never a copy of the item.
- **Following the inventory.** One function, `FollowInventory()`, finds the inventory on the pawn's player state now and, if it isn't the one in the weak pointer, unbinds `OnItemInHandChanged` from the old one and binds it on the new one; then it runs `HandleItemInHandChanged`. It is called at the end of `BeginPlay`, from `FollowController` (bound to the pawn's `ReceiveControllerChangedDelegate`, which fires after `PossessedBy` sets and `UnPossessed` clears the player state, `Pawn.cpp`), and at the top of `StartUsingTool` and `UseTool`; `EndPlay` unbinds. `BeginPlay` matters because the pawn a map starts with is possessed before the world begins play (`UGameInstance::StartPlayInEditorGameInstance` spawns the play actor before `BeginPlay`, and so does `UEngine::LoadMap`), so its controller-changed event fires before the component binds to it. The player state's inventory may not have restored its slots yet when the pawn's `BeginPlay` runs; it then broadcasts the hand when it restores, and the component is already bound. The press calls cover a pawn given a player state without a controller. `HandleItemInHandChanged` runs `UpdateGrantedTool` and broadcasts `OnToolChanged` when the tool in hand differs from the last one it broadcast, which it remembers apart from `GrantedTool` (empty while there is no ability system), so a pawn without a player state broadcasts no tool and its held mesh goes blank. The farming test fixture possesses the farmer with a player controller, so the tests run the event path.
- **Granting** ([section 9](09-AbilitySystem.md), tools as abilities). The spec's source object is the tool asset. A switch to another tool while a use is under way waits for the use to finish, and selecting a slot whose item uses the same tool (another stack of seeds, or a second kind of seed) changes nothing about the grant, so a held key goes on with the new item. The selected slot can also empty inside a use: planting the last seed removes it inside `UseOn`, the inventory broadcasts, and the farming component takes the plant ability back. That happens two ways:
   - **The first use** runs inside `TryActivateAbility`, which holds the ability system's ability list lock (`ABILITYLIST_SCOPE_LOCK` in `InternalTryActivateAbility`). A `GiveAbility` in that moment queues the new spec in `AbilityPendingAdds` and returns its handle, and it is added when the lock is released. `FindAbilitySpecFromHandle` with its default (`EConsiderPending::PendingRemove`) returns null for a queued spec, so a press in that moment finds no spec and does nothing. Never ask with `EConsiderPending::PendingAdd`: its loop over the queued specs in 5.8 doesn't compare handles and returns the first one.
   - **A repeat** runs from the `WaitDelay` callback, without the lock, so `GiveAbility` adds the spec at once and may reallocate the ability list; nothing keeps a spec pointer across it.

   Taking a grant back must also work for a spec that is still queued: a second hand change inside the same locked first use (a Blueprint handler of `On Tool Used` or a listener that selects another slot) releases a grant that is still queued, and `SetRemoveAbilityOnEnd` looks the spec up with the default and finds nothing, so it would stay granted forever. So `ReleaseGrantedTool` releases the input and calls `SetRemoveAbilityOnEnd` only when it finds the spec active, and otherwise calls `ClearAbility(GrantedSpec)`, which removes a queued spec from `AbilityPendingAdds`, marks an inactive one for removal while the list is locked, removes it at once otherwise, and does nothing for a handle it doesn't know. The plant ability, its input released, finishes its use's time and ends, so the repeat stops, as switching tools does.
- **Planting from the item in hand.** The plant ability reads the selected slot and the item in hand from the inventory on the ability system's owner (`GetInventory()`), the item's `UFarmSeedFragment`, and the crop it names from the content catalogue. It removes one from the selected slot with `TryRemoveItemFromSlot`, so the seeds leave the stack the player holds and a second stack elsewhere stays as it is. When the item in hand has no seed fragment or its crop doesn't exist, `CanUseOn` is false and the player is told there is nothing to plant. Two seed items in two slots each plant their own crop.
- **Targeting is `UFarmTargetingComponent`'s.** The target highlight follows its `OnTargetTileChanged`, the character's `DoMove` calls its `AimAt` and `GetAimDirection`, and the use-tool ability finds it on the avatar when it activates, as it finds the farming component, reads the target tile and the grid from it and listens to its event. Without a targeting component the ability ends at once.
- **The HUD.** The status line shows the item in hand's display name ("Day 3   18:40   |   Turnip Seeds"), or "Empty hand", rebuilt on `OnItemInHandChanged`, read with `GetItemInHand()` in the handler. The key hints name the items in the hotbar slots ("1 Hoe   2 Turnip Seeds   3 Watering Can"), empty slots left out, rebuilt on `OnInventoryChanged`, on `OnItemInHandChanged` and when the pawn changes, from the character's `GetHotbarKeyHints`. The held mesh follows the farming component's `OnToolChanged` (read with `GetCurrentTool()`), so seeds show the Seeds tool's mesh and an empty hand shows none.
- **Saving.** The selected slot is saved with the bag (above). The farming component saves nothing; `Base.Farming`, its old key, is retired and never reused ([section 1](01-Saving.md)), since a new owner of that key would read the old bytes.
- **Layers.** `UVestigeToolFragment` sits in `Content/` with the seed fragment; `Inventory/` knows nothing about tools; `Farming/` reads the inventory and the fragments; the character (`Player/`) reaches the inventory.
- **Performance.** No tick. Selecting a slot is a key press. After each inventory change the component compares one index and one pointer. Finding the tool in hand is a search over the item's few fragments and a map find, on a press or a hand change. The HUD rebuilds the hotbar hints on every inventory change, at most about three times a second while a held key plants or harvests, and the status line only when the item in hand changes.
- **Tests:** `Vestige.Inventory.*` for the hotbar, the hand's event (including a handler that selects another slot) and the saved selection; `Vestige.Item.AToolFragmentNeedsAKnownTool`; `Vestige.Farming` for the tool in hand, planting from it, the last seed on a first use and on a repeat, and `ASecondHandChangeDuringTheFirstUseLeavesOneToolAbility` for the queued grant above.

**Pitfalls:**

- A field on `UVestigeItemData` that only some items use (a crop ID, a stamina value). It belongs in a fragment.
- Two fragments of the same class on one item: `FindFragment` would only ever see the first. Validation rejects it.
- One `UObject` per item in the inventory when a stack (ID and count) would do; instances only for items with their own state.
- Skip Lyra's replicated `FFastArraySerializer` list until multiplayer is real.
- Caching the inventory or the player state on the pawn at `BeginPlay`: a pawn spawned later begins play before it is possessed and has no player state yet. Ask the pawn for its player state when needed (an action), or follow the player state, and bind at `BeginPlay` too, since the first pawn of a map is possessed before it (the farming component's `FollowInventory`).
- Reading item definitions by loading them at the moment an item is added. Every definition is in the content catalogue, loaded before play ([section 2](02-Content.md)).
- An inventory, or any other system, loading and keeping content of its own: find it in the content catalogue instead.
