## 1. The game in one paragraph

A 3D life and farming sim set in a retro-futuristic, post-apocalyptic world. You wake from decades of cryosleep under an overgrown pre-war homestead. Humans have almost vanished. Robots carry on with the jobs people gave them, often for nobody. You bring the farm back to life, from hand tools to automated machines, help rebuild the struggling robot town next door, form friendships and romances with robots and with the rare humans you find, and slowly push out into the wasteland. The tone is mostly serious, with comic relief throughout.

## 2. Pillars

Every feature should serve at least one pillar. Features that serve none are cut or postponed.

1. **Bring the land back.** Farming is the heart of the game. Poisoned soil, scarce water and storms make growth feel earned, and a thriving farm in a dead world is the main reward. Healing the land also clears the sky above it and powers the farm.
2. **Old hands, new machines.** Progress runs from rusty hand tools to pre-war tech to high-tech automation. The game mixes old-fashioned farm life with futuristic technology, and automation is a goal in itself.
3. **A world of robots, and rare people.** Robots fill every role people used to: neighbours, friends, love interests, helpers and enemies. Each one has its own history. Humans are rare, and finding one should feel special.
4. **Serious world, light touch.** The setting is grim and the stakes are real, but the game is warm and funny in its details: robots stuck in pre-war routines, absurd old advertising, dry humour.
5. **Your pace.** No forced sleep, no failing out of the game. Days have limits and late nights have costs, but the player decides how to spend their time. Survival needs can be switched off.

**Design goal: moddable.** Content (crops, items, tools, robots, recipes, tech tree entries) is data found by the game, never a hard-coded list, so a mod can add more of it the same way the base game does. See [Architecture.md section 12](Architecture.md#12-designing-for-mods). Beyond that, a system gets mod-specific extension points only when there is a concrete use for them; we don't build speculative abstractions ahead of the gameplay. Mod loading isn't built yet, so the game doesn't claim mod support until we have decided what mods may change and what compatibility we promise between versions.

**Design target: single-player.** The game is designed and built for one player, as [Architecture.md](Architecture.md#16-decision-log) says. Co-op is wanted some day, but no system needs to be ready for networking now. Keeping ordinary separation of concerns (state owned by one class, no needless global assumptions) is enough. When co-op becomes real work, it gets its own design milestone that decides authority, the shared clock and sleep, farm ownership and saves (see [open questions](#15-open-questions)).

## 3. Premise and setting

### Premise

- The player wakes in a failing cryo pod in a hidden room under an old farmhouse. They don't know exactly how long they've been asleep.
- The farm's **caretaker robot** has kept the place barely alive for decades, waiting for "the family" to wake up. They are the player's first relationship and the tutorial guide.
- Robots now run most of society, and they don't see themselves as lesser than humans. More and more of them consider themselves clearly superior. Humans are at best a curiosity, and some robots are openly hostile to them.
- Next to the farm lies a small **robot town**: a settlement of robots still running their pre-war jobs, falling apart because nobody fixes the water pump, the power line or the clinic. Many robots there won't admit that they need a human's help.
- **Main goal:** restore the farm and rebuild the town. The town's broken facilities are restored with crops, materials and machines the player produces, and each restored facility changes the town and unlocks something new.
- **Lore hook:** how robots came to run society and why so many feel superior to humans; how and why the player ended up in the pod, what the war was, what happened to the family, why the caretaker robot stayed.

### Setting

- A post-apocalyptic countryside decades after a war. Ruins, overgrowth, radiation, scrap, pre-war tech that sometimes still works.
- The world is our own. Don't borrow names, iconography or signature ideas from other games or franchises. The cryo pod, the robot-run world and the rarity of humans are our identity.
- **Lore hook:** the world's history, factions, the currency, the companies that built the robots.

## 4. Game loops

### Core loop (minutes)

Prepare the soil, plant, water, protect, harvest, then use or sell the harvest to improve the farm. Each step is a tool action on a tile.

### Daily loop (one in-game day)

1. Wake at 06:00 with full stamina (less if exhausted, see [section 5](#5-time-days-and-seasons)).
2. Tend the farm: water, harvest, clear radiation, refill water stores.
3. Spend the rest of the day on a choice: visit robots in town, work on machines and the tech tree, explore a wasteland zone, gather materials.
4. Go home and sleep. The day ends, crops grow, the game saves.

The day should always offer more to do than there's time for, so choosing is the game.

### Seasonal loop (28 days)

- Each season has its own crops, weather and town events.
- Unripe crops that don't fit the new season wither at 06:00 on its first day, so planning matters. The deadline is always visible: seeds show their days to harvest, the HUD shows the day of the season (of 28), each morning's message tells the days left, planting a crop that can't ripen in time gives a warning, and the caretaker robot mentions crops at risk a few days before the change.
- A season ends with a festival or event in town (**Lore hook:** pre-war holidays the robots still celebrate, without remembering why).

### Long loop (years)

- Restore the town facility by facility.
- Climb the tech tree from hand tools to a fully automated farm.
- Deepen relationships, find the humans, and possibly marry.
- Open up wasteland zones further from home.

## 5. Time, days and seasons

- **Clock:** 24-hour display (06:40, 21:30). A day runs from 06:00 onwards.
- **Seasons:** four seasons (spring, summer, autumn, winter) of 28 days each. The wasteland twist comes from weather events, not from strange seasons.
  - **The calendar** is one count of days: a new game starts on spring 1 at 06:00, and the seasons follow in order, spring, summer, autumn, winter, then a new year starts with spring. A season changes when its 28th day ends and the next morning starts.
  - **The date on the HUD** reads "Spring, Day 3": the season and the day of the season, counting up from 1 to 28 (@DunderGG, 2026-11-14). The HUD doesn't show the days left. The year is added to the date once a game can reach a second year. As a label on the HUD "Day" is capitalised; in a message's sentence it is "day".
  - **The days left are told on waking,** in the one morning message, after the date: "You slept. Spring, day 3 begins. 25 days left.", "1 day left." on day 27, and "It's the last day of spring." on day 28. Staying up all night reads "You stayed up all night. Spring, day 3 begins. 25 days left.", and loading a save "Welcome back. Spring, day 3. 25 days left." (@DunderGG, 2026-11-14, that the morning message tells them; the texts are the game designer's.)
  - **A new season is announced by one message** on its first morning, in place of the usual day message, still saying how the night ended, and with the days left: "You slept. Spring is over, and summer has begun. 27 days left." or "You stayed up all night. Spring is over, and summer has begun. 27 days left." Each season has two names in the calendar's data, one that starts a sentence ("Summer") and one for the middle of a sentence ("summer"), so the text never lowercases a name while the game runs, which would break translations. The seeds' season message can use the second form too. Loading a save never starts a season, so "Welcome back." needs no variant. **Later:** an end-of-season recap screen after the last night and a title card showing the new season (@DunderGG, 2026-11-14); until they exist, the morning message is the announcement.
  - **Days left** are the days after today: 28 minus the day of the season, so spring 3 has 25 left. A crop planted today fits the season when its days to harvest are no more than the days left: a 3-day turnip planted with 3 days left is ripe on the last day (the last planting days in [section 6](#crops)).
- **Weather:** clear, rain (waters crops), dust storms, and radiation storms (raise tile radiation, dangerous outdoors). Weather is announced a day ahead (**Lore hook:** a robot weather broadcaster).
- **Stamina:** tool actions cost stamina. At zero, every action still works but takes twice as long, and costs nothing more. Stamina never goes below zero, so the faster drain after midnight only matters while some is left. Sleep refills it fully, or to 75% when exhausted.
- **No forced sleep.** The player can stay up as long as they like, but late nights cost:
  - After midnight, **hostile robots** roam the farm and its surroundings. They attack the player, never crops or buildings.
  - Stamina drains faster: from midnight it drains steadily, about 10 an hour, even when the player does nothing, until they sleep. (There is no drain by day, so "faster" means it starts.)
  - Sleeping late makes the player **exhausted**: the next day starts with less stamina.
  - **What counts as late:** going to bed at 00:00 or later. Going to bed before midnight is a good night.
  - **Staying up all night:** the night ends at 06:00. A player who never went to bed starts the next day where they stand, as if they had gone to bed at the latest possible hour: exhausted.
  - With survival needs on, cold and night radiation also build up (see [section 7](#7-survival-needs)).
- **Late-night guardrails.** The pressure is gentle: a late night costs the next morning, never the player's progress.
  - The HUD warns at 22:00 ("getting late") and at 00:00 ("it's dangerous out now"). Until hostile robots exist, the midnight warning names the stamina drain instead.
  - Exhaustion has one level and never stacks: several late nights in a row are no worse than one. One night in bed before midnight clears it, and the next day starts with full stamina.
  - Being knocked out by night robots sends the player home exhausted, with no item or currency loss until combat and its penalties exist.
- This replaces the prototype's "pass out at 02:00" rule.

## 6. Farming

### Tiles

**Which tile you work on.** Tools act on a tile next to the one the farmer stands on, in one of the eight directions (diagonals included), chosen by the direction of the movement input. Walking eases in over a moment, so a quick tap of a movement key or the stick turns the farmer to face that neighbour with no more than a nudge of a few centimetres, and holding walks without a pause; the target follows the direction of travel. The farmer always turns smoothly, and after he stops he finishes turning to face the targeted tile. With no input the target stays where it was. The targeted tile is outlined; past the edge of the field there is no target. Mouse and gamepad work the same way.

A farm tile has a state (grass, tilled, planted), and on top of that:

- **Water level:** watered tiles grow. Water is a resource (see below), not free. Decided by @DunderGG on 2026-11-20, after his play-test ([section 14](#14-decision-log)); the cap, the step per use and the nightly fall are tuning in the data, changed without a build:
  - Water on a tile is a level in whole steps from 0 (dry) to 2 (soaked). One use of the watering can adds one step. Each night a crop on a tile with at least one step grows, then the tile loses one step. So a tile watered twice today also covers tomorrow night.
  - Soaking costs the same stamina and water per night grown (2 stamina and one water a night), so it never pays to soak everything; it only frees tomorrow (*Your pace*). Soaking all 16 first-planting tiles takes 64 stamina and 32 water, more than the well's assumed 24 a day ([section 15](#15-open-questions)), so a day away is where clean versus dirty water becomes a real choice.
  - Holding E waters each tile it reaches once; a second step on the same tile takes a new press, so a held press lingering on one tile doesn't soak it unevenly and empty the can.
  - Grass takes no water ("Nothing to water here."). Empty tilled soil and ripe crops do, so soil can be readied before planting, and the water stays on the tile when a crop is harvested. A soaked tile takes no more ("The soil is already soaked."), using no water or stamina.
  - A crop grows on any night its tile has water: no crop needs more than one step, and the crops have no water need of their own. Thirstier crops are content for a drier season, designed with weather (Later).
  - A dry crop doesn't grow, and nothing else happens to it: it never dies of dryness, since a missed day mustn't cost the crop (*Your pace*) and the season's withering is loss enough. A thirsty look while its soil is dry (paler, a little drooped, normal again once watered) is its own item in [roadmap 4.2](Roadmap.md#42-farming-depth). **Lore hook:** the caretaker robot noticing thirsty crops, and suggesting soaking the soil before a day away (the writer's).
  - The player sees the water in the soil alone: one step darker and a little glossier per step, so light soil is dry, dark covers tonight, and darkest covers tomorrow too. Darkness is a difference in value, not hue, so it reads for colour-blind players. A HUD line for the faced tile waits for contamination, which has no natural look, and the HUD's layout pass ([roadmap 4.6](Roadmap.md#46-interface-and-input)); water's azure cue stays on the well and the sprinkler.
  - *For later items:* the sprinkler adds one step at dawn, after the night's fall, drawing water only for tiles it raises; rain soaks every tile to the cap; dirty water adds a step like clean water, and contamination as well.
- **Tile contamination** (the lore's radiation; the glossary's name, [section 16](#16-mechanics-glossary), so the interface says "contaminated", not "radiation"): contaminated soil grows crops slowly or not at all, and can cause mutations. Reduce contamination with tools and machines that capture or remove contaminated material (soil filters, careful removal, or special crops harvested and safely contained); fertilizer improves soil but does not remove contamination.
  - *In the vertical slice* ([roadmap 4.2](Roadmap.md#42-farming-depth)): how many levels there are and what each does to growth, which tiles start contaminated (dirty water and storms come later), how the player sees it, how it is cleaned, what the mutated crop does and how often normal crops mutate are open in [section 15](#15-open-questions), each with the option assumed until @DunderGG decides. Contamination stays on a tile whatever its state: grass, tilled or planted, and tilling, harvesting or clearing a withered crop doesn't change it; only cleaning does.
  - **The texts,** decided by the game designer within its limits ([section 14](#14-decision-log)); they apply under the assumed options and follow them if another is chosen:
    - The levels are named, wherever the game names them: "Clean", "Lightly contaminated", "Heavily contaminated".
    - The HUD names the faced tile's level only when it is contaminated: a clean tile, like no target, shows no line.
    - Normal seeds on soil where they can't grow (heavily contaminated for the spring crops) are refused: "This soil is too contaminated for these seeds to grow." No seed is used, nothing is remembered, and a repeat press gives the same answer, as "Seeds need empty tilled soil." does. It comes after the mutated crop's refusal below and before the too-late warning, so a tile that is both says this. A crop there would never grow or die, and nothing pulls up an unripe crop, so planting anyway would only lose the seed and block the tile from the cleaning crop until the season ends (the design review, 2026-11-22). *Assumed until @DunderGG decides* ([section 15](#15-open-questions)); it replaces the ask-once warning first recorded here.
    - The mutated crop's seeds on clean soil are refused: "These seeds only grow in contaminated soil." It comes after "Seeds need empty tilled soil." and before the too-late warning, as a reason about the tile.
    - Harvesting a crop that cleans its tile says "The soil here is cleaner now.", and "The soil here is clean now." when it reaches clean, so the player learns that the harvest did it. A harvest has no message of its own, so the line stands alone.
  - **A save from before contamination** keeps every tile clean, as a save from before limited water starts with a full can: crops already in the ground don't stall from one load to the next. A new game starts with the field's own contamination.

### Crops

- Each crop is a data asset with a namespaced ID (`Base.Carrot`). It defines its seasons, growth stages, radiation tolerance, yield and value.
- **Mutated crops** are a special kind: they grow in irradiated soil and can be useful (more yield, special effects, rare ingredients) or risky (unpredictable). Mutation alone does not make food unsafe; each crop's content defines whether it is harmful to eat. Contaminated soil alone does not make an otherwise safe crop harmful.
- **Food safety:** A crop that can expose the player when eaten must be clearly identified as unsafe before consumption. The exact detection and warning mechanic is open. Crops grown to remove contamination are not food; their harvest must be contained for disposal or controlled handling, never eaten, composted or burned.
- Crops that span several seasons and regrowing crops come later.

**Spring crops in the vertical slice.** Spring is the slice's season. Three ordinary crops, each with its own role, and the mutated crop ([roadmap 4.2](Roadmap.md#42-farming-depth)) makes four. Decided by @DunderGG on 2026-11-13; the numbers are the current tuning, changed in each crop's data asset without a build.

| Crop | Role | Days to ripe | Yield | Seed price | Sell price (each) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `Base.Turnip` | Quick: the first harvest, and the crop that fills gaps | 3 | 1 | 10 | 25 |
| `Base.Potato` | Mid-length bulk: several per plant | 6 | 3 | 20 | 18 |
| `Base.Cabbage` | Slow and valuable: plan it early | 10 | 1 | 40 | 110 |

- **Days to ripe** count watered nights: a crop grows one day each night its tile has water ([tiles](#tiles)), and each crop lists in its data the night each of its stage meshes starts (see "Growth looks"), so a turnip planted and watered on day 1 is ripe on the morning of day 4.
- **Longer crops pay a little more per day.** At these prices a tile earns about 5 a day with turnips, 5.7 with potatoes and 7 with cabbages, and replanted all season about 135, 136 and 140. None is the right answer: turnips pay the same for more trips to the shop and the field, cabbages for planning and a tile tied up for ten days. The last day to plant a crop and still harvest it in the season is day 25 for turnips, day 22 for potatoes and day 18 for cabbages.
- The sell prices go into the harvest items' data with the shipping bin ([roadmap 4.5](Roadmap.md#45-automation-and-selling)), and the seed prices into the data with the seed shop; until then a seed price is recorded here only. The prices were checked against the season budget in [section 9](#economy).
- **Growth looks.** Each crop has its own meshes, from seedling to ripe. The ripe look appears only when the crop can be harvested, so a ten-day cabbage never looks ready early.
  - Each crop's data lists the watered night each stage starts, front-loaded so the first change comes after the first night (current tuning, in the data):
    - Turnip, 4 looks: changes every night, on nights 1 and 2, ripe on 3.
    - Potato, 4 looks: changes on nights 1 and 3 (flowers), ripe on 6.
    - Cabbage, 6 looks (5 growing stages and ripe): changes on nights 1, 3, 5 and 8, ripe on 10.
  - Every stage is a new shape (more leaves, flowers, a head forming, a change of colour), not only a bigger copy of the one before: from the game camera a new silhouette reads, a few centimetres of size doesn't.
  - Seedlings are at least about 20 cm across on the day they are planted, sized to be seen from the game camera (about 4 m away). No look is wider than about 60 cm, so neighbouring crops on the 1 m tiles keep a gap (current tuning).
  - A ripe potato shows yellowing foliage with 2 or 3 potatoes at the soil.
- **Item names:** "Potato", "Cabbage", "Potato Seeds" and "Cabbage Seeds", beside the turnip's.
- **Every night it grew shows.** On top of the stage meshes, a crop gets slightly bigger each night it grew, so the player sees the next morning that a watered crop grew and a dry (later: irradiated) one didn't. Decided by @DunderGG on 2026-11-13. Current tuning, in the data:
  - Within a stage the crop scales evenly, as a whole from its base, from **80%** on the stage's first day to **100%** on its last: a three-day stage shows 80%, 90%, 100%, a two-day stage 80%, 100%. A one-day stage, such as each of the turnip's, shows at 100%, since its mesh already changes every night.
  - The ripe stage is always shown at 100%; a ripe crop doesn't grow.
  - So that a new stage never looks smaller than the end of the one before, each stage's mesh is at least 1.25 times the previous one (1 / 0.8), in both spread and height. Spread is the limit that matters, because of the 1 m tile and the camera; checking height catches a crop that would look shorter overnight. This is a rule for the crop art, checked when a crop's meshes are made. For the cabbage the spreads are 20, 25.6, 33, 42.5 and 54.5 cm, then about 58 cm ripe (each at least 1.28 times the one before).
  - The size step is the small extra between shape changes; on its own it doesn't read on a small plant.

**Seeds and the end of the season** ([roadmap 4.2](Roadmap.md#42-farming-depth)). Decided by @DunderGG on 2026-11-20, after his play-test ([section 14](#14-decision-log)):

- **Seeds out of their season are refused:** nothing is planted and no seed is used, and the message names when to plant them. Allowing them would let a spring crop grow on until the next season change, so the seasons would stop meaning anything. In the slice the player meets this only in summer, which has no crops.
- **A too-late planting asks once:** the first press of the day that would plant a crop too late plants nothing and warns, ending with "Press again to plant anyway."; every later press that day plants that crop as usual, without the warning. The memory is per crop and per day and isn't saved, so a load warns again once. It costs one press per crop per day, only late in the season, loses no seed to an unread message, and leaves the choice to the player (*Your pace*).
- **A crop that lists no season** grows in every season, is never refused and never too late, and data validation reports it, so a crop meant for every season lists all four and a forgotten field is caught in the editor without blocking play. Every season a crop lists must be one of the calendar's, so the refusal always has a season to name.

- **Seeds show their days to ripe** beside the item in hand on the status line: "Spring, Day 20   14:10   |   Cabbage Seeds (10 days to ripen)", and "(1 day to ripen)" for a one-day crop. Other items show their name only. It is the same count as the days left in [section 5](#5-time-days-and-seasons), so the two compare directly. The hotbar, once drawn ([roadmap 4.6](Roadmap.md#46-interface-and-input)), may show it on the slot as well.
- **A crop is too late** when its days to ripe are more than the days it has before it would wither: the days left in this season, plus the days of each following season in a row that the crop also lists. Every slice crop lists spring only, so this is the days left; a crop that lists every season is never too late. The count assumes a watering every night: a dry night can still make a crop that fit miss the season, and that gets no second warning.
- **What planting reports,** the first that applies: no seeds in hand ("Nothing to plant in hand."), seeds out of their season, no tile ("Stand facing a farm tile."), a tile that can't take seeds ("Seeds need empty tilled soil."), too late. The season comes before the tile, because no tile would take those seeds and tilling first would be wasted work; too late comes last, so it shows only when a seed would really go in. Off the field, with no tile targeted at all, "Stand facing a farm tile." comes first, before any of these, as it already does before "Nothing to plant in hand."; the order above is for a targeted tile. Like every reason, each shows once per press, never once per tile of a held press. A held press stays quiet after its first tile: on a new tile or with other seeds, it plants no seeds that still need their warning and shows no reason, so a new press is the way on. @DunderGG play-tested this silence and kept it (2026-11-20).
- **The texts.** They say "These seeds" rather than the item's name, so no name has to agree with a verb in a translation; the status line already names the seeds in hand.
  - Out of season: "These seeds don't grow in summer. Plant them in spring." (`These seeds don't grow in {SeasonInSentence}. Plant them in {PlantSeasonInSentence}.`): today's season, then the first of the crop's seasons to come after it in the calendar's order, so a crop of several seasons names the next chance to plant it.
  - Too late: "These seeds need 10 days to ripen, but only 8 days are left before summer. Press again to plant anyway." (`These seeds need {DaysToRipe} {DaysToRipe}|plural(one=day,other=days) to ripen, but only {DaysToDeadline} {DaysToDeadline}|plural(one=day is,other=days are) left before {DeadlineSeasonInSentence}. Press again to plant anyway.`), where the deadline season is the first coming season the crop doesn't list. With no days left: "These seeds need 3 days to ripen, but summer begins tomorrow. Press again to plant anyway." (its own text, as for the morning's last day: English can't turn "0 days" into "tomorrow" by plural forms). "Before summer" counts the same days as "8 days left" in the morning message; "summer begins in 8 days" would be off by one.

**Withering at the season change** ([roadmap 4.2](Roadmap.md#42-farming-depth); the rule is the 2026-10-28 entry in [section 14](#14-decision-log)):

- At 06:00 on a season's first day, every unripe crop on the farm whose crop doesn't list the new season withers; a crop that lists it grows on. A ripe crop never withers: it stays ripe and harvestable until picked, since the planting warning promises ripe by the deadline, not harvested by it, and a day spent elsewhere on the last day mustn't cost a planting (*Your pace*). It happens once, on the morning the season starts: never on a load or a developer jump to a date.
- It happens before that night's growth, so a crop one watered night short of ripe withers rather than ripening on the new season's first morning. This is what the planting warning already promises: a crop fits when it is ripe by the last day ([section 5](#5-time-days-and-seasons)), and on the last day every crop is too late.
- **The player hears it in one line right after the season's day message,** only on a morning when something withered: "3 crops withered. They don't grow in summer." and "1 crop withered. It doesn't grow in summer." (`{Count} {Count}|plural(one=crop,other=crops) withered. {Count}|plural(one=It doesn't,other=They don't) grow in {SeasonInSentence}.`), counting every withered crop of every field. A count, not the crops' names, since lists of names don't translate, and the field shows which they were. Its own line rather than a clause on the season message, because it is news of its own, as the rest messages already are, and the season message keeps its two forms instead of four.
- **A withered crop stays on its tile as a dead plant until the player clears it.** It keeps the shape of the stage it reached, slumped and coloured a dry straw-brown (sRGB about `8A7650`; the grey-brown first built, about `958B7C`, was nearly the well's stone and could read as rubble; both are clear of the water and contamination cues, [section 12](#12-presentation)), so the player sees which crop it was and that it died. Facing it offers **Clear** on F, as a ripe crop offers Harvest, for the hand's stamina (1, as harvesting); clearing leaves tilled soil that keeps its water. A withered crop never grows and gives nothing, and takes water like empty soil; seeds and the hoe on it say "Clear the withered crop first." So a withered tile costs no more to replant than grass (clear 1 and plant 1, against till 2 and plant 1), and clearing the first planting's 16 tiles takes 16 of the 100 stamina.
- **Nothing reminds the player before the change for now.** The caretaker robot's reminder ([section 4](#seasonal-loop-28-days)) is a roadmap item of its own, and until then the withering line explains a loss afterwards. With ripe crops kept, only crops already short of time are lost. The rule for that item: a crop is **at risk** when the watered nights it still needs equal the days left, so one dry night loses it, and **lost** when they are more. **Lore hook:** the caretaker's reminder is the writer's to word.

### Water

- Clean water is stored and has to be secured: a well, rain collectors, a water purifier. The watering can fills from these sources, not from thin air.
- Dirty water is easy to get but raises tile radiation.
- Automation (sprinklers, robot farmhands) uses the same water stores.
- **How much clean water there is** (decided by @DunderGG, 2026-11-21, [section 14](#14-decision-log)). Both numbers are data, tuned without a build:
  - **The watering can holds 8 waterings;** one watering is one step of water on one tile. The first planting of 16 tiles takes two fills, so the player meets the well on the first day.
  - **The well gives 24 a day,** back in full each morning; what isn't drawn is not carried over. A dry well blocks nothing: it says so and refills overnight.
  - **The farm has one clean-water store,** which the well draws from; the sprinkler and the rain collectors and purifier to come draw from and add to the same store, whichever map is open.
  - **Filling is free and instant:** F at the well tops up the can wherever it is in the bag, with no stamina, no clock time and no tool swap, and says "Can filled." on each fill with a splash sound, which also plays when the well runs dry part-way through a fill; the text stays with the sound (@DunderGG, 2026-11-21) and is reconsidered when a visual splash comes.
  - **The well's prompt shows what is left today:** "Fill can (16 left)", and "(0 left)" when dry, so F still says why.
  - **A new game, and a save from before limited water, start with a full can and a full well.**
  - **The well stands in the service yard,** on the far side of the field from the farmhouse, about 9 m from the nearest field edge: a short walk that makes a refill a small choice, not a trek.
  - **The can holds one kind of water at a time.** When dirty water arrives, pouring dirty water into a can with clean water makes all of it dirty. The dirty source itself is still open ([section 15](#15-open-questions)).
- **The watering can's texts.** Decided by the game designer within its limits ([section 14](#14-decision-log)):
  - The item in hand shows what the can holds: "Watering Can (5/8)", as seeds show their days to ripen; the bag line keeps "Watering Can 1".
  - E with an empty can waters nothing, costs no stamina and says "Your can is empty. Fill it at the well." It comes after "Stand facing a farm tile." and before the tile's own reasons ("Nothing to water here.", "The soil is already soaked."), as "Nothing to plant in hand." does for seeds. A held press waters each new tile until the can is empty, then says it once and stays quiet, like every reason.
  - F at the well tops the can up, drawing only what it lacks. A full can says "Your can is already full." and draws nothing; a dry well says "The well is dry. It refills overnight."; a well that runs out partway fills what it has and says "The well ran dry before your can was full." A full can comes first: it is the player's reason, whatever the well holds.

### Buildings and machines

- The player places buildings and machines on the farm grid: sprinkler, greenhouse, water purifier, rain collector, robot charging station, storage, crafting stations.
- **The vertical slice's first three machines** are the sprinkler, the shipping bin and the well (roadmap 4.2 and 4.5). The sprinkler is placed on the farm grid, waters nearby tiles each morning from the farm's water store and needs no power. The shipping bin sells the crops put in it overnight, each at its sell price ([crops](#crops)). The well gives clean water that the watering can fills from, and needs no power. The [home farm plan](Levels/HomeFarm/README.md) puts the bin and the well together in a service yard beside the shared route, outside the field's work apron. Their size and look, the sprinkler's range, how much water there is and how the bin is used are not decided yet: [section 15](#15-open-questions) has each with a recommendation, and the meshes are built to those until the maintainer answers. Until the robot town exists, seeds are bought from seed dispensers in the same service yard ([section 9](#economy)); a store building to visit replaces them later.
- The house can be decorated.
- **Later:** animals, as mutated livestock (**Lore hook:** what they mutated from).

### Energy

- Solar is the main energy source. **Solar panels** charge **power cells**, and power cells run the farm's machines and automation: water purifiers, robot farmhands, the robot charging station, later machines. Power cells are a resource, not a currency.
- A panel's daily output depends on the time of day (nothing at night), the weather (dust storms block most of it) and **the haze** over the farm (see below).
- Machines draw a set amount of charge to run. When the stored charge runs out, they stop until it's recharged. Early tools and simple sprinklers don't need power, so a new player isn't blocked by energy.
- Energy sources (panels, later other generators) and consumers are data assets, so mods can add both.
- **Lore hook:** Cinderium dust and the haze, see [Lore.md section 4](Spoilers/Lore.md#energy-after-the-war).

### Land health and the haze

The player's actions slowly improve or damage the local environment, and healthier land gives more solar energy nearby. A restored farm is visibly a brighter place. Regional weather determines whether radiation storms occur; the farm's land health cannot stop or schedule them.

- Each map has a **land health** value, computed from its tiles: clean, planted and healthy tiles raise it, and irradiated, polluted or bare tiles lower it. It changes slowly, over days rather than minutes.
- **Improves land health:** growing healthy crops, containing removed contamination, using clean water, leaving ground planted or covered, and growing certain mutated crops that take up Cinderium compounds especially well.
- **Damages land health:** dirty water, burning scrap, stripping soil without replanting, and "dirty" machines that are fast or cheap but pollute.
- **Effects:** land health affects how much contaminated dust local winds can lift and how much dust settles during a storm. Healthier, covered soil means less local resuspension and exposure, a thinner near-ground haze, and more solar output. Regional haze and storm occurrence change slowly and are not cleared by one farm.
- **Radiation storms:** weather sets when a storm happens; its effects on the map depend partly on local land health. A storm can deposit contamination, while healthy ground cover limits resuspension and reduces local dust exposure. Restoring land does not make storms less frequent.
- **The trade-off:** dirty shortcuts (dirty water, polluting machines) make the farm faster now and weaker later. The game never forbids them; the player chooses.
- Land health should be easy to read: a simple meter or sky indicator, and haze you can see.

**Bounds.** Land health is strategic feedback, but gentle: it shapes how well the farm runs, never whether it can. These limits keep the loop from running away in either direction (starting values, to tune):

- Land health runs from 0 to 100 for each map.
- A low-tech way back always exists: the well and hand tools need no power, so even a badly damaged farm can be cleaned up by hand.
- The haze never cuts solar output below 40% of a clear day, so powered machines never stop entirely because of it.
- One storm adds at most a set amount of contamination to each tile.
- With clean play, going from poor (20) to fair (50) takes about one season.
- Before the energy system is built, simulate a best-case and a worst-case season (on paper or in a test) to check these limits.

### Implementation order

1. Radiation on tiles and mutated crops.
2. Clean water as a resource.
3. Weather events.
4. Solar energy and power cells, then land health and the haze.
5. Scavenging (later, together with exploration).

## 7. Survival needs

- **Needs:** hunger, thirst and radiation sickness. Eating crops and drinking clean water keep them in check, which feeds back into farming.
- **Optional:** a setting turns survival needs off for casual players. With them off, the game is a pure farming and life sim:
  - Off removes the player's own meters (hunger, thirst, radiation exposure, cold) together with their HUD and penalties.
  - Everything that belongs to farming stays the same in both modes: tile contamination, clean water for crops, and storms.
  - The setting can be changed at any time. The save keeps the meters, and they start full when needs are switched back on.
- Needs never kill the player outright. Neglecting them lowers stamina and work speed, and at worst the player wakes at home with a penalty (the same as being knocked out in combat).

## 8. Robots and people

### Robots

Robots fill every role a human would. They don't see themselves as servants: most start out wary of or dismissive towards the player, and some are hostile. That attitude is what makes winning a robot's friendship, and later its love, feel special.

- **Townsfolk and friends:** each robot has a name, a personality, a daily schedule, things it likes and dislikes, and its own lore and backstory.
- **Love interests:** romance is an important part of the game, with both robots and humans.
- **Farm help:** automation robots that water, harvest and haul, unlocked through the tech tree. Robots befriended in town can also be recruited to the farm.
- **Companion:** the caretaker robot, and later others, can follow the player and help in the field.
- **Enemies:** feral or hostile robots in the wasteland and on the farm at night.

### Humans

- Very rare. Each human the player finds is a story moment and a special relationship.
- Found through exploration and story progress, not standing around in town.
- **Lore hook:** who they are, how they survived, why they are alone.

### Relationships

- Robots start at a low or even negative opinion of the player, because the player is human. Humans start neutral or friendly.
- The caretaker robot is the exception: they are loyal to the family, so they start as a dutiful servant rather than a sceptic. Their arc is about choosing a purpose of its own (**Lore hook:** see its character sheet).
- Every change in trust has a reason the player can see: the character says or does something different.
- Friendship grows by talking, giving gifts, helping with requests and taking part in events.
- Higher friendship unlocks the character's backstory and personal events, gifts and optional recipes, recruiting them to the farm, and romance with eligible characters. It does not unlock robot, farm, or player performance upgrades; robot upgrades belong to the tech tree. Relationship rewards do not grant system-wide bonuses to farm production, power, durability, weather protection, survival, or tool efficiency.
- Romance leads to dating and marriage. A committed partner may move to the farm only by a separate choice; commitment does not force relocation.
- Romance is always opt-in through an explicit dialogue choice, and the character can decline. Gifts alone never lead to romance.
- **Vertical slice:** one complete arc with the caretaker robot. Trust grows when the player works alongside them and asks for their opinion instead of giving orders. They respond visibly by helping on their own initiative, and the payoff is a story moment, not an item.
- Characters are data assets so mods can add new robots and humans.

### Talking

The first version, with Hob, the caretaker robot (working name; "they", as the character sheet says). How a talk feels (time, line reveal, choices, the box, camera, facing, what Hob says, idle) is open in [section 15](#15-open-questions), built on the recommended options.

- **Starting a talk:** face Hob and press the interact key (F, gamepad B), with the same range and targeting as every prop. The prompt is "Talk to Hob", built from the character's display name ("Talk to {Name}"), so day 1 teaches the name before the first word and later characters are told apart. Talking costs no stamina. Moving and the use key are off while the box is open.
- **Going on:** F, Space, Enter or a left click; on a gamepad A, and B while B is the interact key, since the press that opened a talk must also continue it. Escape ends a talk early. When interact moves to A (the template's replacement), B becomes "end the talk", the platform's convention.
- **Replies:** up and down (keys, D-pad, left stick) or the mouse move the highlight, which starts on the first reply; the go-on key or a click picks one.
- **The box's key hint** follows the device, like the HUD's: "F Next" on a line, "F Close" on the last line, "F Choose" on a list of replies.
- **No double press:** the press that ends a talk doesn't start it again, and a press that finishes a line being typed doesn't also skip to the next.
- **Time of day for lines** (the writer's bands): morning 06:00 to 11:59, afternoon 12:00 to 17:59, evening 18:00 to 23:59, night 00:00 to 05:59.
- **Hob's placeholder body,** until the Blender model: about 1.5 m tall, a little shorter than the farmer; a boxy head with a dark face screen and two glowing eye bars; a body block in cream paint with olive-green panels and some rust, after the concept art. The eyes are **cyan, as in the concept art** (@DunderGG, 2026-11-25), greener than the water cue's azure `58AEFF`: an approved exception to the colour rule ([section 15](#15-open-questions), the colour cues). The face makes turning readable, and the glowing eyes help find Hob after dark.

## 9. Progression

The player grows along several tracks at once. Each track has one job, so no reward is gated by two tracks and no track has to be ground to reach the main loop:

| Track | Its job | What it gates |
|---|---|---|
| Town restoration | The main story | New shops, robots, areas and mechanics |
| Tech tree | Engineering | Machines, automation, tool tiers and robot upgrades |
| Relationships | Characters | That character's story, gifts, optional recipes, recruiting and romance; never robot upgrades, core machines or system-wide performance bonuses |
| Skills | Getting better by doing | Small bonuses only, never a gate |

- **Skills:** farming, engineering, scavenging and combat. They level up by doing, and each level gives a small bonus.
- **Tool tiers:** rusty, then pre-war, then high-tech, unlocked through the tech tree's tools branch. Better tools cost less stamina, work on more tiles at once, or do new things (for example a tool that cleans radiation).
- **Tech tree:** the backbone of engineering progress. Branches include automation (sprinklers, then robot farmhands, then full automation), tools, water and soil machines, energy (solar panels and power cells), and robot upgrades. Nodes are unlocked with materials, currency and engineering levels. Tech tree entries are data assets so mods can add branches.
- **Town restoration:** the main story progress. Each restored facility (water pump, power, clinic, market...) opens new shops, robots, areas or mechanics.
- **Relationships:** see [section 8](#8-robots-and-people).

In the vertical slice, farming is the primary loop and the sprinkler is the one tech unlock. The caretaker robot's relationship is optional but rewarding. By the end of the season the player has the sprinkler, one step of trust with the caretaker robot, and money for the next season's seeds.

### Economy

- There is one currency from the start (**Lore hook:** what it is).
- Selling crops and goods gives currency, which buys seeds, tools, tech tree unlocks and buildings.
- The economy is a supporting system, not a focus early on. Balancing and depth (shops, prices, trading) come later.
- **First-season guarantees:**
  - The player starts with seeds for a first planting and a small purse. In the vertical slice the first planting is 10 turnip, 4 potato and 2 cabbage seeds: 16 tiles, which tilling, planting and watering on day 1 takes 80 of the 100 stamina ([section 6](#crops)). Until a shop exists, these are all the seeds there are. The new seeds come after the watering can in the hotbar: keys 1 to 3 stay the hoe, the turnip seeds and the can, 4 is the potato seeds and 5 the cabbage seeds.
  - One cheap basic crop is always for sale and always grows in clean, watered soil, so a bad harvest is a setback, never a dead end.
  - A basic crop sells for at least twice its seed price, counted per seed planted (yield times the sell price): the turnip pays 25 for a 10 seed, 2.5 times; the potato 54 for 20 and the cabbage 110 for 40 pass too.
  - Town restoration asks for specific items in fixed amounts, separate from selling, so donating versus selling is a real choice.
  - Before setting prices, plan a simple budget for one ordinary and one poor season (below).

**The slice's season budget** (2026-11-22, the game designer, for roadmap 4.5). Prices as in [section 6](#crops) (seeds 10, 20, 40; harvest 25, 18, 110 each), stamina 2 to till, 1 to plant, 2 to water, 1 to harvest, 100 a day, and the well's 24 waterings a day; a seed shop from about day 4 is assumed. Rounded:

| | Ordinary season | Poor season |
|---|---|---|
| Tiles growing | 16 on days 1 to 3, then 24 (the well's limit) | 16 all season |
| Watered nights | about 85% | about 55% (dry days, late replanting) |
| Stamina on a usual day | about 60 (48 watering, 10 harvest and replant) | about 30 |
| The bin pays, all season | about 4,300 | about 1,900 |
| Spent on seeds (the first 16 are free) | about 1,400 | about 500 |
| Coins on day 28, purse included | about 3,000 | about 1,500 |

- **Clean water, not stamina, caps the farm:** 24 tiles, about 40 stamina spare a day for the town, the caretaker and, once it exists, a dirty-water field, which is the slice's clean versus dirty choice. The sprinkler draws from the same store, so it saves stamina (16 a day), not water.
- **Both seasons leave money for the next one:** re-sowing 24 tiles costs 240 to 960. A sprinkler at about 500 would be in reach around day 10 in an ordinary season and day 15 in a poor one, close to the "halfway" the sprinkler question asks for ([section 15](#15-open-questions)); its price is set with the shop.
- **No dead end even with nothing:** a dry crop never dies, and the starting purse buys a re-sowing of turnips.
- **Play-test check:** by day 14 an ordinary first-time player should have about 1,000 to 1,500 coins; far less means the crops or the walk to the bin cost more than planned, far more that the prices are too generous.

**Selling at the shipping bin** ([section 6](#buildings-and-machines); how the bin is used is the recommended option in [section 15](#15-open-questions), assumed until decided). Decided by the game designer within its limits (2026-11-22, [section 14](#14-decision-log)):

- **F at the bin with a sellable stack in hand** puts the whole stack in and says "Shipped: Turnip ×10. The bin pays in the morning.", so the first sale teaches that the money comes overnight. One press ships one stack; holding F doesn't repeat. The prompt stays "Ship".
- **F with nothing sellable in hand** (a tool, seeds, an empty hand) ships nothing and says "Hold a crop to ship it." In the slice only crops sell; the text follows when other goods do.
- **The sale runs at every new day, however the night ended** (slept, stayed up all night), before the morning message, at each item's sell price that night. Loading a save sells nothing; the bin keeps what is in it until the next night.
- **The morning message ends with "The bin paid 250."** (the night's total) on mornings something sold, the season's first morning included, where the withering line still follows it; "Welcome back." never carries it. One message per morning, as @DunderGG asked (2026-11-14).
- **The purse is on the HUD** on its own line under the stamina: "Coins 250", "Coins" being the working name until the writer names the currency (**Lore hook**); the HUD's layout pass ([roadmap 4.6](Roadmap.md#46-interface-and-input)) may move it. It shows from the first moment of a new game.
- **A save from before coins** starts with the starting purse, as a save from before limited water starts with a full can.
- **Decided by @DunderGG (2026-11-22), the recommended options:** the starting purse is 100 coins (ten turnip seeds: money becomes real with the first sale on day 4, with a safety net from the start); what goes in the bin is sold and can't be taken back before night (every crop in the slice is for sale, so a wrong press costs little; a bin screen can come with town donations); seeds don't sell at the bin (it is plainly for the harvest, with no buy-and-sell loop against the shop).

**Buying seeds** ([roadmap 4.5](Roadmap.md#45-automation-and-selling)). Decided by @DunderGG for the slice (2026-11-22, [section 14](#14-decision-log)), on the recommended options:

- **A row of three seed dispensers** in the service yard beside the bin, one per seed, each on a 1 m tile of its own, so facing one picks the seed with the targeting the player already knows. Each is a machine of the bin's family, about 0.9 by 0.9 m and 1.5 m high, a teal vending cabinet with a window showing its seed packets so the three tell apart at a glance. A fourth dispenser (the sprinkler, a mod's seed) is the same thing again. Rejected: one dispenser with a new key to step through its offers (a control used nowhere else, a hidden choice), a shop screen (needs the first menu, [roadmap 4.6](Roadmap.md#46-interface-and-input)).
- **All three seeds are always for sale, without limit,** at the seed prices in [section 6](#crops); the turnip is the basic seed that is always for sale. In the slice's summer the dispensers still sell spring seeds and planting refuses them; seasonal stock comes with crops for other seasons. Rejected: the turnip only (the crop choice is gone after the starting seeds), a daily limit (a pressure the slice doesn't ask for, and a value to save).
- **One seed per press.** A usual replant is 2 to 10 seeds. Rejected: a pack of 5 (coarse: 40 coins buys no pack of potatoes); holding F to repeat, kept as the fix if the play-test finds tapping tedious.
- **Later, a proper store building to visit replaces the dispensers** (@DunderGG: "later on we should have a proper store building to visit, not just three dispensers with seeds"), with the robot town's shops ([roadmap, milestone 3](Roadmap.md#the-robot-town)).

The details, decided by the game designer within its limits (2026-11-22, [section 14](#14-decision-log)):

- **The prompt names the seed and its price:** "Buy Turnip Seeds (10)", on F like every prop. The price is per seed, so it is the number the purse loses.
- **F buys one seed** into the bag and says "Bought: Turnip Seeds ×1 for 10.", in the form of the bin's "Shipped:" line; the purse on the HUD and the bag line show the new totals. One press buys one; holding F doesn't repeat, as at the bin.
- **A buy that can't happen buys nothing and costs nothing,** and says why, the first that applies: a full bag, "No room in your bag." (the harvest's text); a short purse, "Not enough coins. These seeds cost 10." (`Not enough coins. These seeds cost {Price}.`, "coins" the HUD's working name until the writer names the currency, **Lore hook**). The bag comes first: with no room, money wouldn't help. There is no credit or debt.
- **The shop doesn't warn about the season** (it follows from the stock above). Out of season or too late, seeds are still sold; planting refuses or warns ([section 6](#crops)), and the status line shows their days to ripen once they are in hand. Warning twice would teach the player to skip both.
- **Nothing about the shop is saved:** the stock is unlimited and the prices fixed, so only the purse and the bag change, and both are saved already.
- **Not for sale in the slice:** the sprinkler until it exists (its sale from the same shop is assumed in section 15), and anything but seeds. The shop's stock is data, so the sprinkler and a mod's seeds join it without a new machine type.
- **On the first day** the player sees the dispensers beside the bin and the well, and facing one shows the price, with 100 coins in the purse. Nothing pushes a purchase: the starting seeds fill the day's stamina, so the first buy usually follows the first sale on day 4. **Lore hook:** the dispensers as old Civitas vending machines that still take coins, and the caretaker robot pointing them out (the writer's).

## 10. World and exploration

- The world is a set of **small separate maps** with loading between them. This is far easier to build and run than one open world.
- **The farm:** the player's home and the biggest map.
- **The robot town:** next to the farm. Shops, robots, events, facilities to restore.
- **Wasteland zones:** further out and unlocked over time, for example a ruined city, an old research facility, a dam. Zones are where enemies, scavenging, rare materials and the rare humans are.
- **Faction hubs:** separate maps unlocked through character stories. The engineer's report opens the route to the Bastion; an Index archivist's exchange opens the route to the Archive.
- Zones are data driven, so mods can add new ones.

## 11. Combat

- Simple real-time combat: melee (a wrench to start) and ranged (improvised guns later).
- Never the focus. The farm and town are mostly safe by day. Combat gets harder further out in the wasteland and on the farm at night.
- Losing a fight never ends the game: the player wakes at home with a penalty (some lost items or currency, exhaustion).

## 12. Presentation

- **Camera:** third-person, as now. Other camera angles (for example a higher, angled view) may be added later, so camera code should not be hard-wired into gameplay.
- **Art:** one stylized look, the same in the repository and in sold builds, all of it in the repository. Chunky, slightly exaggerated silhouettes that read from the game camera (crop stages, tile states, robots); a few materials with flat or lightly textured colour, where wear, rust and overgrowth are big shapes and roughness, not fine detail; colour that tells the land's story, a washed-out wasteland and a farm that grows more saturated as it heals, with contamination and water state each given a colour cue nothing else uses. Lumen lighting carries the mood. The current low-poly assets are the first pass of this style and are improved in place (same IDs, sizes and pivots), never replaced by a second set.
- **Art sources:** made for Project Vestige, plus third-party assets only under the licenses [CONTRIBUTING.md section 11](../CONTRIBUTING.md#third-party-material) accepts, wherever they're hosted (a Fab listing under CC BY 4.0 is fine). Assets under a store's own license (the Fab Standard License, which covers Megascans) aren't used. CC0 material is used mainly for textures and HDRIs, re-materialled through our own master materials, so recognisable CC0 model kits don't make the game look assembled from stock. Epic's engine and template content stays under the Unreal Engine EULA, as [LICENSING.md](../LICENSING.md#which-license-applies-where) lists, until it is replaced. If better art is paid for before release, it raises this same style; that needs @DunderGG's yes on the money and a contract, checked by a lawyer, that gives him the copyright (or an exclusive license) and accepts that the sources are published in the repository.
- **Mood:** properly dark nights, overgrown ruins, warm light around the farm and the town.
- **UI:** 24-hour clock. Clear and light, with pre-war flavour (**Lore hook:** the style of pre-war tech interfaces).

## 13. Scope and milestones

### Done: prototype

Third-person farmer, a grid of tiles, tilling, planting, watering, harvesting, a day and night cycle with a clock, a HUD, and saving and loading.

### Next: vertical slice

One full 28-day season that proves the farming core. It covers three pillars in a small form (*Bring the land back*, *Old hands, new machines* and *Your pace*) and starts a fourth with one relationship: the caretaker robot. It does not test the robot town, humans, exploration, combat or survival needs. Those get their own proof in later milestones, and the slice should not be read as validating them.

Content:

- 3 to 4 crops, including one mutated crop.
- Radiation and clean water on tiles.
- Stamina with the late-night penalties (faster drain, exhaustion; hostile robots may wait until combat exists).
- The caretaker robot with a little dialogue and one relationship track.
- One automation unlock: a sprinkler.
- Selling crops for currency.
- Crops, tools and items as data assets (needed for moddability before content grows).

The slice is done when the content works and playtests show that a first-time player, without outside help:

- Notices why a crop grows slowly or not at all (dry soil or radiation) and fixes it.
- Makes at least one real choice, such as clean versus dirty water or which crop goes in which soil.
- Sees the caretaker robot's opinion change because of something they did.
- Spends less time watering once the sprinkler is in place, and notices it.
- Stays up late at least once and understands what it cost them the next day.
- Finishes the season wanting to start the next one.

### First release

The minimum complete game. Everything beyond these limits is a stretch goal. The numbers are starting limits, to revisit after the vertical slice.

Roughly in order, to be planned when we get there. Cut candidates in this list are stretch goals beyond the first release:

- **A pet** ([RoadmapSuggestions.md, item 18](RoadmapSuggestions.md#18-decide-in-or-out-on-common-genre-features)). One creature on the farm that greets the player, is petted with the interact key and has a name the player gives it: no products, no needs, no way to lose it. It serves *Serious world, light touch*: something simply glad to see the human, where most robots aren't. Nothing here plans one: the companions of [section 8](#robots) are robots that help in the field, and the animals of [section 6](#buildings-and-machines), a cut candidate in [section 13](#first-release), are livestock; whether that cut covers a pet is part of this question. *Options:* (a) none in the first release, with the caretaker robot as the player's company; (b) a small robot critter: hard-surface parts are the cheapest to model and animate (rigid parts on simple joints, and wheels or a hover need no walk cycle), and a robot in a pet's old role fits *A world of robots*, but it must read as a pet, wordless, or it competes with the caretaker; (c) a mutated stray animal: the strongest hook, life in a dead world nearly as rare as a human, but it needs organic modelling and four-legged animation made for the game ([section 12](#12-presentation) keeps store-licensed assets and recognisable stock model kits out). Two choices weigh more than the kind. Staying on the farm costs far less than following the player between maps, through town and zones, and a follower must keep off the tile the farmer targets. Petting should give nothing but the pet's reaction: a daily reward turns it into a chore, and needs that can be neglected go against *Your pace*. A name needs a text box that works with a gamepad, as a character creator's would. *Recommended:* (b), on the farm only and petted for its own sake, in the first release after the slice, and the first thing cut if the release runs late. Not in the slice, where a second follower would blur the caretaker's arc, and not on the first day, which is the caretaker's. (c) stays an idea for later. *Assumed until decided:* (a); nothing is built for a pet. **Lore hook:** what the creature is and where it came from: for a robot, why it is a pet and not a person in a world where robots are people; for an animal, whether a living one fits the world. The writer decides.
- **Fishing** ([RoadmapSuggestions.md, item 18](RoadmapSuggestions.md#18-decide-in-or-out-on-common-genre-features)). Casting a line and waiting for a bite is a calm pastime many players of the genre expect. Nothing here plans it, and it runs into *Bring the land back*: water is scarce, and dirty water poisons the soil ([section 6](#water)). The suggestions propose one gathering loop for the first release, foraging or scavenging ([item 7](RoadmapSuggestions.md#7-add-one-first-release-gathering-loop)), and defer fishing until after milestone 3 ([item 15](RoadmapSuggestions.md#15-defer-or-cut-the-following)). *Options:* (a) out: this world's open water is poisoned, and scavenging ([section 10](#10-world-and-exploration)) is its gathering; (b) later, after the first release, as a second gathering loop once the first has proved itself; (c) in the first release, as part of the one gathering loop. *How it would fit, under (b) or (c):* only in poisoned water, never the farm's clean water; the catch mostly scrap and pre-war parts, which feed what scavenging feeds, and mutated fish, each safe to eat or not by its own data, as crops are ([decision of 2026-10-30](#14-decision-log)); on the farm, catches getting cleaner as its land heals would tie it to the first pillar. It costs a rod, casting and reeling with their animations, a catch game kept simple (a hard one shuts players out, so it needs an easier setting), catch tables by season, time, weather and water, and fish items with meshes and icons. *Recommended:* (b): it is a whole loop, the first release's systems ([section 13](#first-release)) don't yet name its gathering loop beyond scavenging in the two zones, and scavenging already gives the reason to leave the farm. If the gathering loop's design finds water its best source, it becomes (c) in the shape above. *Assumed until decided:* no fishing in the first release, and no map keeps water for it. **Lore hook:** what still lives in the water, and where.
- **A handheld target** ([RoadmapSuggestions.md, item 19](RoadmapSuggestions.md#19-make-hard-to-undo-choices-early)). Many players of the genre play on handheld PCs such as the Steam Deck. The target is a desktop PC with an RTX 2060 at 1920x1080 and High ([Architecture.md, section 10](Architecture.md#budget), 2026-10-29); nothing here says whether handhelds count. On one, everything has to work on the built-in controls with no setup, typing a name through an on-screen keyboard included; text has to read at arm's length on a screen of about 7 inches at 1280x800 (Valve's compatibility review asks for the smallest character to be at least 9 pixels tall there, and recommends 12 and a text size setting); and the game has to hold 30 fps there with its default settings, on a chip with about a quarter of the target GPU's power. That means a lower scalability profile, where the mood that [section 12](#12-presentation) gives to Lumen has to hold with less of it, or none. *Options:* (a) not a target: desktop only, with gamepad play as already planned ([DecisionLog.md](DecisionLog.md), 2026-11-07) and nothing promised or tested; a later yes reworks the HUD and the lighting; (b) handheld-ready, measured later: from the HUD layout pass ([roadmap 4.6](Roadmap.md#46-interface-and-input)) on, every screen is laid out to read at 1280x800 and 16:10, with the smallest text at least 12 pixels tall there, every menu works with a gamepad alone, and each milestone's play-test looks at the game at Low (dark nights, the farm's warm light, the colour cues), while a handheld budget is set and measured on a device before release, at the risk of finding only then that the frame rate needs more than settings; (c) a full second target now: (b), plus a handheld row in the budget, measured on a device at the end of every milestone. *Recommended:* (b). The parts that are hard to undo, the text and the look, cost little to design in now and help players on a TV, on a laptop or with poor sight too; gamepad play is planned already; the frame rate is mostly scalability settings, best tuned on the device; and with no handheld or gamepad at hand yet ([PlayTest.md](PlayTest.md)), nobody could measure (c)'s budget. At 1280x800 the interface scales to about three quarters of its 1080p size, which puts today's smallest text, the key hints, at or below the minimum. *Assumed until decided:* (b)'s interface rules in the HUD layout pass, since larger text costs a desktop player nothing and a desktop-only layout costs a rework; no performance work.
- **Languages of the first release** ([RoadmapSuggestions.md, item 19](RoadmapSuggestions.md#19-make-hard-to-undo-choices-early)). The texts are already written to translate (two names per season in [section 5](#5-time-days-and-seasons), counts with plural forms and no lists of names in [section 6](#crops)), but no language besides English is chosen, and the only roadmap item is the text gather ([roadmap, section 6](Roadmap.md#6-before-a-public-release)). The layout depends on the choice: German runs up to a third longer than English, short labels often more, and Chinese and Japanese need a font set with fallbacks (the HUD's Roboto has none of their characters) and more pixels per character to stay readable, which meets the handheld question at the smallest text size. English and Simplified Chinese are together more than half of Steam's users in its 2026 surveys. *Options:* (a) English only, laid out for English, each later translation costing a layout rework; (b) English first, with the interface built for translation from the HUD layout pass on: room for text a third longer, no text in fixed-size boxes or in images, a font set for Latin, Cyrillic, Chinese, Japanese and Korean that suits the pre-war style ([section 12](#12-presentation)), a check at each milestone with fake, lengthened text (pseudo-localization), and no right-to-left languages, which mirror the whole layout; the translations to ship are chosen when the text is nearly final, since every rewrite after a translation is paid again in each language; (c) a set of languages chosen now and translated for the first release, such as English, Simplified Chinese, Russian, Spanish, Brazilian Portuguese, German, French and Japanese: the widest reach, but paid translation of every line, a play-test in each language, and text that keeps changing until release. *Recommended:* (b), with Simplified Chinese first in line for translation, for its share of players, and lettering in the world (signs, pre-war advertising) left as drawn, as part of the art, so anything the player has to read to play is interface text. *Assumed until decided:* (b)'s layout rules, which cost little and keep both other options open.
- **Pollution and exposure:** whether pollution is its own tile value or lowers land health directly, and whether standing on contaminated tiles adds player exposure ([section 16](#16-mechanics-glossary)).
- **Land health:** exactly how fast it changes, and whether damage spreads to neighbouring tiles.
- **Energy balance:** how much charge panels give and machines use, and whether the vertical slice needs any of it (probably not).
- **Mutated crops:** exactly which effects are good and which are risky, and how often normal crops mutate. For the vertical slice, the four questions below.
- **Contamination in the slice, 1: levels and growth** ([section 6](#tiles)). *Options:* (a) three levels, clean, lightly contaminated (a normal crop grows on every second watered night, so half speed) and heavily contaminated (a normal crop doesn't grow at all, and doesn't die); (b) four levels, each slower than the last; (c) clean or contaminated only, where contaminated stops growth. *Recommended, and assumed until decided:* (a), two steps like the water level, deterministic so the player can plan: a chance-based slowdown would read as a bug. The mutated crop grows at full speed at any level. Each crop's tolerance is data, so later crops can differ; the three spring crops share one.
- **Contamination in the slice, 1b: normal seeds on soil where they can't grow** ([section 6](#tiles)). *Options:* (a) refused, "This soil is too contaminated for these seeds to grow.", nothing planted; (b) ask once and plant on the second press, as a too-late planting does ("... Press again to plant anyway."), which only makes sense with a way to pull up an unripe crop (an uproot action on F, for the hand's stamina, leaving tilled soil), or the seed is lost and the tile blocked from the cleaning crop until the season ends. *Recommended, and assumed until decided:* (a): a crop that can never grow or die is no choice worth asking about, and an uproot action is a new control for one case. (b) without uprooting is rejected. If later crops tolerate heavy soil, they simply plant there.
- **Contamination in the slice, 2: where it is and how the player sees it.** Without dirty water and storms, the field starts contaminated in a new game. *Options:* (a) bands across the 8 by 8 field, getting worse away from the farmhouse: the two rows nearest it clean (16 tiles, the first planting; until the farmhouse exists, the bed's side, the field's near edge), four rows lightly contaminated (32) and the two far rows heavily (16), so growing past 16 tiles, which the season budget assumes from about day 4 ([section 9](#economy)), means using or cleaning contaminated soil; (b) one contaminated patch of about a third of the field, leaving 44 clean tiles, more than the well can water, so it is a side project the player can ignore; (c) random tiles each game. *Recommended, and assumed until decided:* (a); with (b) a player can finish the slice without meeting contamination, and the slice goal of noticing why a crop doesn't grow fails. *Seeing it:* the soil (and the grass, before tilling) shows contamination in the contamination cue ([section 15](#15-open-questions), the colour cues), as speckled cracks that are denser on heavy soil, so it reads by shape as well as hue; facing a contaminated tile names its level on the HUD. Options: the soil alone (subtle on a dark, watered tile), or a scanner tool (hides the cause the slice wants noticed). **Lore hook:** what lies past the field's far edge that poisoned it.
- **Contamination in the slice, 3: cleaning it, and what the mutated crop does.** *Options:* (a) the mutated crop cleans: it grows only in contaminated soil, and each harvest lowers its tile one level; its harvest isn't food, goes in the shipping bin and sells for little (the buyer contains it, which keeps the rule that cleanup crops are never eaten, composted or burned); (b) a cleaning tool, a shovel that scrapes off one level for stamina and gives a sack of soil to contain, with the mutated crop a valuable food crop that thrives on contaminated soil; (c) both. *Recommended, and assumed until decided:* (a): cleaning is farming, needs no new tool or key, and the slice's one mutated crop has a job no other crop has. A cleaning tool comes with the tool tiers ([section 9](#9-progression)). Its seeds come from a fourth seed dispenser. Proposed numbers, within the siblings' range: 5 nights to ripe, yield 1, seed 15, sells 30 (about 3 a tile a day against 5 to 7), and a heavy tile takes two plantings. **Lore hook:** its name, look and description are the writer's.
- **Contamination in the slice, 4: how often normal crops mutate.** *Options:* (a) once, when a normal crop ripens on lightly contaminated soil, a 1 in 4 chance it ripens mutated: its ripe look in the contamination cue with a shape of its own, and its harvest a mutated version that is safe to eat and sells for twice as much; (b) a stalled crop on heavy soil can turn into the mutated crop, which then grows and cleans the tile; (c) no mutation of normal crops in the slice. *Recommended, and assumed until decided:* (a): a pleasant surprise that gives light soil a reason to try, without paying better than cleaning (half speed at a quarter's chance of double is about 30% less a tile a day than clean soil). It costs one mutated harvest item and ripe look per crop; (b) costs no new items but turns a crop into a non-food one, which reads as a loss.
- **Talking with Hob, the first version** ([section 8](#talking)). Eight questions, each *assumed until decided* on the recommended option, which is what is built:
  1. *Time during a talk.* Recommended: the clock stops while the box is open, and the world keeps moving (idles, wind, light); talking is free (*Your pace*). Options: the clock runs (a slow reader loses minutes, and the night drain ticks); the clock stops but each talk costs 10 minutes (a rule to explain for no gain in the slice).
  2. *How a line appears.* Recommended: typed out at about 40 characters a second (a 120-character line in 3 s); a press shows the whole line, the next goes on; an instant-text setting comes with the settings menu. Options: the whole line at once (flatter; a long line lands as a wall).
  3. *Replies.* Recommended: the box shows up to 3 replies, and this first talk has one harmless choice that changes only Hob's answer, so the choice list is built and play-tested before the trust track, which is about asking Hob rather than ordering them. Options: lines only until the trust item (the box's focus handling is reworked then); choices with trust outcomes now (that is the next roadmap item).
  4. *The box.* Recommended: a bottom-centre panel about two thirds of the screen wide, room for 3 lines larger than the HUD's text (at least 12 px at 1280x800), the speaker's name on a tab at its top-left, the key hint at its bottom-right, no portrait; the interact prompt and the key hints hide while it is open, the clock and date stay. Options: a portrait with eye-display expressions (art per expression and character); speech bubbles in the world (small, hard to read at 1280x800, no room for replies).
  5. *The camera.* Recommended: it stays where it is, since the player already faces Hob; if the play-test finds Hob behind the box or off-screen, it eases behind the player instead. Options: that easing from the start; a framed two-shot (most work, jarring for a short greeting).
  6. *Facing.* Recommended: on the first line Hob and the farmer turn to face each other (about 0.3 s), and Hob turns back to their spot's facing at the end. Options: only Hob turns; nobody turns (reads as talking to a sign).
  7. *What Hob says.* Recommended: a first meeting of about 3 to 5 lines, once ("has met Hob" is saved); then the first talk of each day gives a greeting for the time of day (2 or 3 per band, not yesterday's), and later talks that day a short line ("talked today" isn't saved). Nothing on trust, the tutorial or crops at risk. Options: one fixed talk (a broken record on day 2); greetings by weather and day of the season too (later, with the reminders). **Lore hook:** every line is the writer's, and how the first meeting fits an outdoor start before the cryo-pod opening exists.
  8. *Hob when not talking.* Recommended: standing at their spot day and night with a slow sway and an occasional blink, solid. Options: perfectly still (reads as a prop); small chores at the spot (needs animation, with the Blender model).

- **The colour cues for water and contamination** ([section 12](#12-presentation) gives each a colour that nothing else uses; the first props are the first to show water). Checked by hand against the machines' palette (teal paint `2F7F7A`, brass, dark metal, rust, wood), the crop and soil colours, the tile frame's yellow and the warm oranges of sunrise and the haze. *Water:* azure `58AEFF` (nothing in the game is blue; the nearest colour is the teal paint, about 33 degrees of hue away and much darker); aqua `4FD8E8` (about 10 degrees from the teal paint, so a teal sprinkler would read as water); pale ice-blue (reads as sky or moonlight and is weak against brown soil). *Contamination:* raspberry-magenta `D63A9C` (about 35 degrees from the turnip's purple `7A3B8F` and 45 or more from the red-browns and the red stamina text; no crop, soil or machine is this hue); ultraviolet `8A4DFF` (about 50 degrees from the water blue and 25 from the turnip's purple); toxic lime `B8F020` (the same hue as the potato sprout and the cabbage head, and next to the tile frame's yellow, so crops would read as contaminated). *Recommended, and assumed until decided:* azure for clean water and magenta for contamination, each used for nothing else (no paint, trim, crop or light); dirty water shows in the contamination colour, not in a third; wet soil stays the darker brown it is, and the blue can tint it when the soil material that reads water arrives (roadmap 4.2). A colour-blind player can't rely on hue alone, so each cue also gets a second signal, a shape or an icon, with the HUD. The first meshes use only the water cue (the well and the sprinkler, in a material slot of its own so the colour changes in one place); the contamination cue first shows on the dirty water source and the soil. The one exception, approved by @DunderGG on 2026-11-22: the cleaning crop's ripe pods are in the contamination colour, since they hold the contamination the crop took up and the colour shows it is ripe; a mutated crop is tinted a much paler pink over the whole plant, so the two don't read alike. A second exception, approved by @DunderGG on 2026-11-25: Hob's eyes are cyan, as in the concept art, greener than the water's azure ([section 8](#talking)).

The well is 0.9 m across and about 1.3 m high: a round stone ring about 0.55 m high with a teal-and-brass lever pump, a brass dial whose band is the water cue, and the water inside the ring in the water cue. ## 16. Mechanics glossary

The gameplay values behind the setting's words. Use these names in code, UI text and content assets; the lore names are flavour for the same thing. Each value is separate unless the table says one feeds another. Anything marked *open* is still to be decided (see [open questions](#15-open-questions)).

| Gameplay value | Where it lives | Lore name | What changes it | What it affects |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Tile contamination** | Each farm tile | Radiation, Cinderium compounds in the soil | Raised by dirty water and by radiation storms depositing dust. Lowered by soil filters, careful removal, harvesting and containing absorbing mutated crops, and slow decay | Crop growth speed, whether a crop grows at all, the chance a crop mutates. Feeds **land health** |
| **Tile water** | Each farm tile | | Raised by the watering can, sprinklers and rain. A level from 0 to 2 that falls one step each night ([section 6](#tiles)) | Whether the crop grows that night |
| **Water store** | The farm, one store for every map, drawn from at the well (rain collectors and purifiers later) | Clean water | Back in full each morning from the well's daily supply (24, nothing carried over); later also filled by rain and purifiers. Drawn by the watering can at the well and by sprinklers ([section 6](#water)) | How much clean watering the player can do each day |
| **Land health** | Each map, 0 to 100 | | Computed from the map's tiles, and changes over days: planted, clean, covered tiles raise it; contaminated, polluted or bare tiles lower it | **Local haze**, dust exposure during storms |
| **Pollution** | *Open:* its own tile value, or a direct cut to land health | | Dirty machines, burning scrap | Land health |
| **Local haze** | Each map | The haze | Follows land health slowly, on top of a regional haze that barely changes | Solar output, how bright the sky looks |
| **Weather** | The region, set each day and announced a day ahead | | Regional forecast only; the farm can't change it | Rain waters tiles. Dust storms cut solar output. Radiation storms add tile contamination and **player exposure** outdoors |
| **Charge** | Power cells | | Solar panels add it; machines draw a set amount to run | Whether a powered machine runs |
| **Date and days left** | The game, worked out from the day count | | Each new day; 28 days a season, spring to winter, then a new year | Which crops fit the season and when out-of-season crops wither. The HUD shows the day of the season, counting up; the morning message tells the days left, 28 minus the day of the season; a crop fits when its days to harvest are no more than the days left |
| **Stamina** | The player | | Tool actions spend it; it refills on sleep. Drains faster after midnight | How much work the player can do, and how fast at zero |
| **Exhaustion** | The player, for one day | | Going to bed at 00:00 or later, or staying up all night | Starts the next morning at 75% stamina; one night in bed before midnight clears it |
| **Player exposure** | The player, only with survival needs on | Radiation sickness | Radiation storms outdoors, night radiation, eating harmful crops (*open:* standing on contaminated tiles) | Stamina and work speed ([section 7](#7-survival-needs)) |

Tile contamination and player exposure are different values: dirty soil hurts crops, and only weather, night and food hurt the player directly.
