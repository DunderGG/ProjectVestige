# Story timeline and reveal pacing

> **Spoilers.** This is a writer-facing guide to the planned story order, possible storylines, emotional tone and character knowledge.

## Purpose and status

This is a provisional narrative framework, not a quest list or a fixed calendar. The phases describe story progression, not years, seasons, heart levels or playtime. Each phase gathers storylines that may overlap or branch; it does not mean every listed scene is required or already designed.

Provisional voice-ready scripts for these beats are collected in [Dialogue/README.md](Dialogue/README.md). Their scene coverage is a drafting aid, not a commitment to implement every optional character or branch.

Use the overview table to understand the intended pacing and the chapters as the detailed reference. Character and faction sheets define their individual arcs. Where those sheets or this timeline leave a detail open, keep it open rather than treating an example as settled canon.

Relationship arcs and hub visits may happen in different orders. The player should not need romance, every optional investigation or every possible character to understand the central revelations. Keep in-world dates relative to the Four Days; the exact current year is unspecified.

## Pacing shape

**Cozy recovery → relationships and local mysteries → ambiguous clues → corroborated truth → survivor-led response**

The family mystery runs alongside the larger story. Its late message is an intimate thread, not a prerequisite for discovering the trials or responding to them.

## Story phase overview

| Phase | Emotional register | Main story movement |
|---|---|---|
| [Opening — waking and making a home](#phase-opening) | Cozy, curious, occasionally lonely | Wake the sleeper, establish the farm and Wellspring, and begin the relationships and family mystery. |
| [Early — relationships and local records](#phase-early) | Warm and grounded, with small uncertainties | Develop character arcs, the local repair effort, and the first ambiguous irregularities. |
| [Transition — a pattern emerges](#phase-transition) | Uneasy and investigative | Let independent clues corroborate a pattern of research on surviving humans without yet settling its full scope or responsibility. |
| [Middle — the trials become undeniable](#phase-middle) | Serious and emotionally direct | Establish that the Continuance Trials happened and were abusive, while preserving uncertainty about individual knowledge and responsibility. |
| [Late — what happens next belongs to the harmed people](#phase-late-response) | Compassionate, difficult and measured | Let survivors define what help, privacy, accountability or contact they want, and follow their lead. |
| [Late, parallel thread — the family message](#phase-family-message) | Intimate and reflective | Continue the family mystery toward Hob's sealed message without making it resolve unrelated mysteries. |

## Phase 1 — Opening: Waking and making a home

<a id="phase-opening"></a>

**Emotional purpose:** Make the player's first experience about waking, learning, tending and meeting people. The world is damaged, but the opening should offer concrete routines and acts of care rather than front-load its darkest history.

### The sleeper wakes

The cryo pod fails after waiting for the all-clear that never came. The sleeper wakes under the farm, disoriented and without knowing how much time has passed. Hob explains that the war, called the Four Days, ended about seven decades ago. Do not name an absolute year or imply the sleeper immediately understands the social and political changes that followed.

The first practical problems should be understandable at human scale: the pod needs attention, the small farm needs tending, and Hob is learning what to do now that the order to keep the farm until the sleeper woke has been fulfilled. Tutorials can carry the player through daily routines while also showing Hob's careful maintenance and damaged recollections.

### Hob and the farm

Hob's arc begins as duty fulfilled rather than a new command. Hob may help the sleeper, but the story should let Hob decide whether to remain, join town recovery or pursue another purpose. The player's early relationship with Hob can develop through shared work, asking Hob's opinion and restoring parts of the garden together.

Restoring the farm is not a solo rebuilding fantasy. Hob knows the small farm system; town residents and Keeper operators know Wellspring's services. The farm's gradual improvement gives the player a dependable home base and a reason to cooperate, without making the player responsible for every local repair.

### Wellspring and ordinary life

The nearby town is struggling with an aging water system and other failing services. Introduce it through useful, everyday encounters: residents balancing assigned tasks, the public commons, a working or failing pump, local repairs, trade and seasonal customs. Wellspring is independently governed; the Keeper network is not the town government.

The town's trouble should invite relationships and cooperation, not suggest that its residents are helpless until the sleeper arrives. Operators keep authority over dangerous legacy systems, and the assembly determines local priorities.

The Keeper story can develop alongside repairs: a service failure exposes how several specialist roles depend on one another; a role boundary leaves part of the problem unowned; operators compare records, local workarounds and compatible-part requirements; and the town decides whether to adopt a continuing cross-role maintenance practice. The sleeper helps people coordinate but does not operate unfamiliar machinery or decide what Wellspring needs. Whether the town adopts a lasting practice remains open.

### Family traces begin

Letters, photographs, damaged recordings or Hob's partial memories can appear during farm restoration. Early pieces should be affectionate, ordinary or incomplete: they establish that the parents stayed above ground and are gone, but do not determine their fate or explain the missing family pod. The sibling's survival path and whether the sleeper will meet them remain undecided.

### Knowledge boundary and handoff

At the end of the opening, the player should understand the passage of time, the farm's immediate needs, Hob's fulfilled order and Wellspring's interdependence. The player does not yet know the full history of the factions or that some survivors were subjected to research. End on curiosity about what happened, not certainty that the world is hiding a conspiracy.

## Phase 2 — Early: Relationships and local records

<a id="phase-early"></a>

**Emotional purpose:** Let daily life and character relationships carry the story. Small uncertainties may disturb the calm, but they should not turn every character or institution into a threat.

### Farm and town restoration

The farm loop continues to make visible progress. Wellspring's repair work develops through operators comparing observations, coordinating compatible parts and agreeing on a safe plan. Different characters contribute distinct expertise rather than competing to be the one who saves the town:

- Hob understands the farm and the caretaker order, and chooses what to do after it.
- Keeper operators understand the town's legacy system and its practical workarounds.
- Gage leads technical inspection and repair planning through his Iron Creed field assignment.
- Cleo helps recover and contextualize old records, while learning that diagrams do not replace local knowledge.
- The sleeper contributes practical work, asks useful questions and helps people coordinate; the sleeper is not a substitute for their expertise.

Service restoration can support character scenes and town life, but it should not be treated as proof that all factions have changed their beliefs.

### Hob's choice and memory

As trust grows, Hob can move from formal fulfillment of the order toward freely chosen help. Memories of the family return in fragments while the player restores the farm. These moments are personal relationship rewards, not a sequence of evidence that must be solved before the rest of the story can proceed.

Hob may decide to participate in Wellspring's recovery, but this is Hob's decision. A player's choice to focus elsewhere should not imply that Hob has failed the family or abandoned the sleeper.

### Gage's work at Wellspring

Gage begins as a respected Iron Creed engineer who is cautious about letting a human near dangerous machinery. His personal arc gives the player chances to challenge that assumption with concrete observations and useful work:

- An early station visit lets the sleeper step back, identify that a vibration syncs with the intake flap, or challenge Gage's condescension. Accurate observation earns practical trust without making the sleeper an instant expert.
- A later search for a compatible legacy coupling shows that the sleeper's pre-war familiarity can solve a problem Gage's Bastion tooling cannot. Gage admits that he was wrong to dismiss the sleeper's salvage knowledge.
- During the controlled pressure test, the player's choice leads to an emergency abort with a cracked shear pin and contaminated reservoir, a push ahead that ruptures a gasket, or a stepped bleed-down that avoids damage. The consequences and Gage's later report must follow the chosen branch.
- The pressure-test aftermath is where Gage can reveal his earlier repair failure and the partner who saved him, making his blind spot personal rather than representative of the whole Creed.

Later, Gage's report to the Bastion should credit the actual repair team and record the pressure-test outcome honestly. The report earns the sleeper a route and invitation to the Bastion; romance is not required. The town's eventual pump overhaul can extend the cooperation example, but should preserve the branch's repair history.

### A possible Bastion readiness story

After the hub opens, a separate Iron Creed story can test what readiness means in practice. A threat or failing defense near the Bastion exposes a gap in its preparations. One member favors coordinating with nearby communities; a dominationist argues for a show of force that would secure the Bastion while endangering others.

The sleeper can help establish what the threat can do, which parts are compatible and who would bear the cost of each plan. Creed members remain responsible for their own decision. A protective response can create an example for continued debate without ending the Creed's internal conflict or making its members uniformly hostile or reformed. The exact threat, supporting characters and choices remain open.

### Cleo's records exchange

Cleo arrives to study Wellspring's pre-war aquifer infrastructure. Her relationship with the sleeper begins with an informed request that states the requested material, storage, intended audience, retention and withdrawal terms before the player chooses. The sleeper may allow technical records, also agree to a bounded family-rationing interview, or refuse.

This records exchange sets up Cleo's ethical arc without making the sleeper's consent a key that unlocks her romance or the Archive. At the records desk, a mismatch between official diagrams and the Keeper operator's lived knowledge teaches Cleo that the archive needs context as well as documentation. At the town commons, residents challenge her draft when it includes personal logs or security vulnerabilities; the community's sharing limits matter.

At higher trust, Cleo can invite the sleeper to the Archive after honoring Wellspring's limits. The Review Council's later response to her monograph tests whether she can defend an accurate record with community-approved redactions. The hub is a place to learn about the Index's institutions, not evidence that every member shares Cleo's evolving ethics.

### The nomad and other encounters

The nomad's visits offer trade, practical news and a view of human survival that does not make them a spokesperson for all people. Their arc can start with the player holding a sealed weather tube under an agreement not to open it, then helping inspect a silent route marker for signs of damage or tampering. The player learns to report what the evidence supports without inventing a missing contact's fate.

As trust grows, a copied route note creates a harder problem: closing the route protects contacts but cuts off supplies and warnings. The nomad can choose to warn only the people who need to know, replace the compromised signal and ask a few trusted stops to share the work. Names, destinations and a complete map remain private.

Other encounters can build the feeling of a populated robot world: the administrator's concern for Wellspring, the clinic robot's curiosity, the mail carrier's undelivered letters, festivals and faction visitors. Keep their scenes rooted in their present concerns. They need not hint at the trials.

### The family mystery continues in parallel

Farm finds, messages and the mail carrier can gradually widen the family's story. Each artifact should add a piece of ordinary context or a credible lead; none should announce that the parents' fate or the sibling's story is already solved. The sibling remains an optional story thread whose final role and timing are open.

### First irregularities

If a darker-history clue appears in this phase, keep it isolated and interpretable in more than one way: a missing name in a clinic ledger, a sealed medical shipment, a record marked "transferred," or disagreement about a former patient. The clue should not yet establish a program, its perpetrators or the survivor's experience. A player who misses it should still be able to understand the later reveal.

The character arcs need not wait for each other. Gage can earn the Bastion invitation while Cleo is still working through the Wellspring monograph; the nomad may have an active route problem before or after either hub visit. Gate each scene on its own relationship and evidence, not on a single rigid order of romances or faction visits.

## Phase 3 — Transition: A pattern emerges

<a id="phase-transition"></a>

**Emotional purpose:** Shift from comfortable local stories toward investigation gradually. The player can become uneasy, but the story should preserve ordinary warmth and avoid implying that all kindness was a deception.

### Independent clues begin to corroborate

Clues from distinct places and sources start to fit together: missing patient names, repeated sealed shipments, euphemisms in damaged records, and robots who disagree about what happened to specific people. A clue becomes stronger when another independent source supports it, not simply because a faction character declares it true.

Potential sources include:

- Wellspring's old clinic or municipal records.
- Documents Cleo finds while researching unrelated civic infrastructure.
- Restricted or damaged records at the Archive or Bastion.
- Route observations or carefully verified news carried by the nomad.
- A survivor's own account, only if they choose to share it.

These are possible paths, not a checklist. No single character should be the sole keeper of the truth, and the nomad must not disclose a contact's identity or location without permission.

The nomad's route arc can reach the farm-agreement stage here or later: the nomad, Hob, the sleeper and Wellspring discuss whether the farm can be a limited rest, trade and warning stop. Each affected party sets their own terms. Agreeing to the stop does not expose a concealed refuge, make the farm a human headquarters or grant the player access to the nomad's contacts.

### What the player may infer

The player may suspect that surviving humans were researched, categorized or moved between institutions. They should not yet know all of the following merely from a clue: which procedures were performed, who ordered them, how many people were harmed, which faction members knew, or what any survivor wants now.

Keep the distinction between fact and inference visible. Terms such as "nonviable" and "transferred" may be institutional euphemisms, but a surviving record still needs context and corroboration before writers can treat every implication as confirmed.

### Character knowledge follows evidence

Cleo and Gage may respond differently based on what they personally see or are shown. Their membership does not automatically make either one aware of the programs. Other Index and Iron Creed members may remain unaware; those who learn of evidence can disagree, object, protect the institution or help establish accountability.

Wellspring residents also retain their own concerns. They may fear that exposing a nearby institution could bring danger, or worry that researchers will once again treat people as evidence. Those reactions should be grounded in the specific risk, not presented as ignorance to be overcome by the sleeper.

### Other possible survivor stories

The lore also leaves room for a shelter survivor, a second sleeper, a human living in disguise among robots, and the sleeper's sibling. These are possible paths, not a required checklist or a fixed cast. If included, each should enter through a credible lead and have a distinct relationship to the sleeper and the world:

- A shelter survivor can offer a first-hand but limited account of how one community endured; they do not stand for all hidden humans.
- A second sleeper can share the disorientation of waking into a changed world while having their own history and choices.
- Discovering a disguised human can create a secret held by both people, not automatic permission for the sleeper to reveal it.
- The sibling remains part of the family thread; their survival path and whether or when they meet the sleeper are undecided.

Any such story must preserve the limited-cast goal and avoid making the nomad's private contact circuit a convenient guide to every survivor.

### Handoff to the central reveal

By the end of the transition, the player should have enough converging evidence to ask a clear question: were people subjected to these procedures without informed, ongoing consent? The middle story answers that the separate programs existed and were abusive. It does not answer every question about individual responsibility or prescribe what should happen to the people harmed.

## Phase 4 — Middle: The trials become undeniable

<a id="phase-middle"></a>

**Emotional purpose:** Deliver the central truth plainly and give it room to land. Do not use the survivor as a twist, an exhibit to unlock, or a mechanism for proving a faction's ideology.

### The old research institute

An old research institute brings together records that make the Continuance Trials difficult to deny. Evidence connects the Index's living exhibit to cognitive integrations and confirms that Iron Creed members conducted frame conversions. The programs are separate, and the evidence need not show a single shared command or conspiracy.

The core story should communicate what is established:

- The researchers claimed to prepare humans to survive in a robot-run world.
- People were recruited under misleading promises, pressured or taken against their will; some who wanted to leave were not permitted to.
- Both programs caused serious harm and had no reliable recipe for success.
- Failure was hidden behind terms such as "nonviable" or "transferred".
- Most members of the factions did not know about the work; responsibility is not collective guilt.

The institute may hold records, equipment and testimony, but the amount of detail and who controls access remain tied to the survivor's wishes.

### The living exhibit

The living exhibit is a person, not a collectible, reward or automatic quest-giver. The player can learn that they are a trial subject and that their display is not voluntary. Their identity, history, condition, wishes and willingness to speak remain open until developed with their agency at the center.

An initial contact should prioritize immediate safety and ask whether they want contact, care, privacy, testimony or space. The player does not automatically gain their name, full story, medical details or permission to share records by finding the Cognitive Wing.

### Evidence branches and optional investigations

The main story provides an accessible route to the broad truth. The Cognitive Wing and Frame Conversion Wing are optional investigations that may add records, named researchers, conflicting accounts or further context. Optional access must not be required for the player to believe the survivor, receive care or begin a response.

Evidence can differ by route. Track which records were found, who has seen them and what they actually establish. A character should not make accusations based on an optional document the player did not recover. A missing file can leave a question open; do not silently turn absence into proof.

### Immediate consequences

The discovery can change how characters understand their institutions and one another:

- Cleo confronts the gap between preserving a record and respecting the person recorded.
- Gage confronts the use of readiness and protection as rationales for controlling another body; the affiliation of the researchers with the dominationist current remains unknown.
- Ethical members of either network may object, offer help or seek more evidence.
- Others may defend the institution, dispute the interpretation or fear that disclosure will cause harm.
- Wellspring's residents may debate what can safely be shared outside the town.

These reactions should be character-specific. No one scene should make one person represent a whole faction.

The main story can also put the sleeper in contact with an affected person whose choices shape how evidence is gathered. That person may be the Index's living exhibit, but their identity, history and willingness to participate are not settled by discovering the institute. If they decline contact or disclosure, the player can still understand that the programs existed and were abusive.

## Phase 5 — Late: What happens next belongs to the harmed people

<a id="phase-late-response"></a>

**Emotional purpose:** Move from discovery to care, control and specific accountability. Avoid a revenge spectacle, a clean institutional redemption or a forced public confession.

### Ask before acting

The affected people decide what happens next. Their wishes might include safety, medical support, privacy, a private account, public testimony, return or destruction of records, accountability, no further contact, or a different response. These are possibilities, not a fixed menu that every survivor must choose from.

Consent to one action is not consent to another. Agreeing to medical care does not mean agreeing to be interviewed; sharing an account with Cleo does not mean permitting it in the Archive; identifying a researcher privately does not mean accepting a public hearing. Refusal, silence, uncertainty or a change of mind must remain valid outcomes.

### Separate care, evidence and accountability

The response can contain several connected but distinct threads:

1. **Immediate care and safety:** Ask what conditions would make contact safe. The survivor may choose support from a trusted character, a neutral place, privacy or no contact.
2. **Records and testimony:** Preserve only what the survivor authorizes. Redact identifying or dangerous details where requested. The archive may record that material is withheld without claiming to know its contents.
3. **Investigation:** Identify people and institutions through corroborated evidence. Do not infer guilt from faction membership, rank or presence in the same hub.
4. **Accountability:** Let the harmed people define what accountability means to them. A confrontation is only one possible route, and it should happen only if they want it and it is safe enough to attempt.
5. **Institutional response:** Specific Index and Iron Creed members may help, resist, deny, or seek reform. Any precedent is contested and limited; it does not instantly reform either network.

The player and allies can help carry out an agreed response, but cannot select a survivor's preferred outcome for them. If a survivor wants no further involvement, that boundary takes precedence over completing optional content.

### Character and community arcs pay off

Cleo can defend a community-approved record and its limits at the Archive, carrying forward the Wellspring precedent rather than asserting ownership of the survivor's story. Her ethics are tested by peer review, institutional pressure and her own desire to preserve knowledge.

Gage can support a specific accountability path and challenge uses of readiness that erase another person's agency. He should not be framed as the lone hero who punishes the researchers or speaks for every Creed member. His professional standing and the Creed's response can vary without making survivor care depend on his success.

Wellspring's assembly and local operators decide what the town shares and what risks it accepts. The sleeper may help residents and researchers communicate, but the town is not a stage for the player's moral victory.

Other personal arcs can reach their own payoffs alongside this response. Gage's high-trust story can culminate in the joint aquifer overhaul and his presentation of cooperation as readiness; his pressure-test branch still determines the earlier repair history. The nomad may ask the sleeper and other agreed stops to help sustain the route circuit, then continue travelling by choice. These arcs can reinforce themes of shared responsibility without becoming prerequisites for a survivor's care or accountability.

### Branches and aftermath

Different survivors may want different things, and one person may change their mind. The story can therefore end with several simultaneous outcomes: one person accepts support, another chooses privacy, records are partly preserved under limits, and a responsible institution remains under dispute.

No branch should treat withholding testimony as failure, force reconciliation with perpetrators, or resolve historical harm through a single apology. The lasting change can be a concrete practice—consent checks, restricted records, safer care or an accountability process—without claiming that abuse can be undone.

## Phase 6 — Late, parallel thread: The family message

<a id="phase-family-message"></a>

**Emotional purpose:** Give the sleeper's family story its own intimate payoff. The emotional resolution should not be subordinated to the faction trials or used to explain them.

### Evidence across the story

Family traces appear from the opening onward in the farm, old addresses, letters, photographs, recordings and Hob's damaged memory. Their order can vary with exploration. Each item should provide a credible piece of the family's history without requiring the player to find every item to understand the late message.

The player learns only gradually that the parents stayed above ground and are gone; the sleeper entered the pod because only one family pod arrived before the war; and the sibling was away when the war began and may still be alive. At the message scene, the family thread resolves: the parents stayed to maintain the farm's shelter, became fatally ill through repeated Cinderium exposure, and died at the farm. Hob laid them to rest beneath the old pear tree at the garden's edge. The sibling's survival path and whether the sleeper meets them remain undecided.

### Hob's sealed message

Hob holds a message the parents wrote together after the Four Days, with the instruction to deliver it "when the farm is whole again." The visible threshold is a weather-safe farmhouse, clean running water, a food-producing garden, and Hob's freely chosen commitment to a future alongside the sleeper. These are understandable restoration and relationship milestones, not a hidden completion percentage, a day count, town completion, or faction progress.

The message explains the parents' informed choice to keep the sleeper's shelter running despite the danger to themselves, their love, their wish to be laid to rest beneath the old pear tree, and their wish that the farm become a living home. Hob then confirms that it fulfilled the burial request. This preserves what the parents could plausibly know while giving the player a complete answer about their fate. The message does not reveal a surviving sibling's location, settle the world's human population or explain the Continuance Trials.

### The sibling remains a separate question

The sibling is definite in the sleeper's story, but their survival path and role are undecided. Possible outcomes include a later meeting, reliable evidence that they survived, an unresolved absence, or an overlap with another planned survivor. Do not choose among these possibilities implicitly in an early clue.

If the sibling is encountered, they are a person with their own history, boundaries and response to the sleeper. Kinship does not grant automatic trust, access to their memories or authority over their choices. Their story must fit the limited human cast and should not turn the nomad's private contact circuit into a convenient directory.

### Closing the family thread

The family-message payoff gives the sleeper closure: the parents chose to stay, the reason they died, where Hob laid them to rest, and the freedom to make a future without turning the farm into a shrine. It leaves room for the sibling's future to remain open and does not answer unrelated regional mysteries.

## Reveal and knowledge guardrails

- **Hint before proof.** Early clues should support more than one interpretation. Save corroborating evidence and direct testimony for later phases.
- **Do not gate the basic truth behind optional content.** The main story should establish that the trials happened and were abusive. Optional faction wings may add records, named perpetrators or complications.
- **Track knowledge by source.** Keep a note in each scene about what the player and each present character have actually learned. Do not let characters refer to evidence from a branch the player did not see.
- **Separate fact from inference.** Records may prove that a program existed without proving who knew, who ordered it, or what an individual survivor wants now.
- **Keep survivors in control.** Consent to care, testimony, recordkeeping, disclosure and confrontation are separate choices. Refusal or silence must not be framed as guilt or as a failure to complete the story.
- **Do not collapse the factions.** The Index and Iron Creed ran separate programs. Their motivations, perpetrators and institutional responses may differ; members are not collectively responsible merely because they belong to a network.
- **Keep the opening's promise.** The darker history should complicate a cozy opening gradually, not retroactively make ordinary farming and relationships a disguise for a grim story.
- **Treat every phase as relative.** Do not assign absolute in-world years or make the order depend on a specific calendar date.
