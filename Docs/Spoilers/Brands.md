# Brands and companies

> **Spoilers.** Some guardrails below name what a brand must never hint at.

The pre-war companies and brands that already appear in the game's texts, the devlog or the lore, with what a writer needs to give each one a printed voice: an ad, a label, a sign, a catalogue page, a jingle.

- **Only brands already in use.** No speculative companies. An entry is added when a brand first appears, and not before.
- **The rule:** a new brand gets its entry here before it appears in any piece, in the game or the devlog. Write the entry, then the piece.
- **One source of truth.** Where [Lore.md](Lore.md) or a character sheet already describes a company or a product, the entry links to it instead of repeating it, and that source wins if the two ever differ. This file adds only what the sources don't hold together: where the brand appears, how it sounds in print, and what it must never touch.
- **Names** marked *(working name)* can still change ([Lore.md, section 14](Lore.md#14-open-questions)). A name already printed in a public devlog piece stays in that piece if it is renamed later, so ask the maintainer before renaming one.
- **Room for more:** the three makers made most robots, not all of them ([Lore.md, section 12](Lore.md#12-lore-and-mods)). Mods can add brands of their own.
- **All pre-war ads** share the house style of [Lore.md, section 3](Lore.md#3-before-the-war-the-automation-age) (bright, confident, "of tomorrow", a lot of small print) and the tone rules of [section 15](Lore.md#15-tone-and-the-darkest-material): the joke is on the product, the promise and the paperwork, never on the people who bought it or what happened to them.

## Hearthwell Mechanical *(working name)*

- **What it made:** home and farm robots, appliances and home cryo pods; design language and company slogan in [Lore.md, section 7](Lore.md#7-the-robot-makers). Its home thinking machines also answered the household's questions ([section 3, "Thinking machines"](Lore.md#thinking-machines)).
- **Era and status:** pre-war. Gone as a company; its machines and its spring festival survive.
- **Product lines already named:**

| Product | What it is | Slogan | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| HB-7 "Homebody" | The home robot line Hob belongs to | none yet | [Hob.md](Characters/Hob.md), [Hob-Everyday.md](Dialogue/Hob-Everyday.md) (`Hob.Meet.Answer.Name`) |
| SnugSleep *(working name)* | The home cryo pod | "Shelter for the whole family!" (small print: additional pods sold separately, delivery times may vary) | [Lore.md, section 9](Lore.md#why-the-sleeper-was-in-the-pod) |
| Home thinking machines | Household advice and decisions | "Why think when Hearthwell thinks for you?" | [Lore.md, section 3](Lore.md#thinking-machines) |
| Engineered livestock | "Self-shearing" sheep, "low-maintenance" hens | none yet | [Lore.md, section 11](Lore.md#11-smaller-hooks) |

- **Where it appears:** Hob names the model in the slice's first meeting ([H-01](Dialogue/Hob-Everyday.md#h-01---first-meeting-slice)); the farm is "a small, isolated Hearthwell system" ([Hob.md](Characters/Hob.md)); the Hearthwell maintenance shed on the farm ([Engineer.md](Characters/Engineer.md), [E-04](Dialogue/Phase-02-Early.md)); Hearthwell Day, the spring festival ([Lore.md, section 11](Lore.md#11-smaller-hooks), and the festival lines in the [archivist](Characters/Archivist.md), [engineer](Characters/Engineer.md) and [nomad](Characters/Nomad.md) sheets). Not yet in a devlog piece.
- **Voice in print:** warm, homely and a little smug. Speaks to the family at the kitchen table, promises comfort and fewer worries, and hides its catches in small print. Exclamation marks are allowed.
- **Guardrails:**
  - The SnugSleep stays a product, never the family's story: no hint that only one pod came, of who was in it, or of the all-clear that never sounded. A pre-war pod ad may exist; the family's own paperwork is late-game material ([StoryTimeline.md](StoryTimeline.md)).
  - The thinking-machine slogan is about households leaning on machines, which is early-chapter knowledge. Never tie Hearthwell's home machines to the strategic systems that steered the war ([Lore.md, section 3](Lore.md#thinking-machines)).
  - Hob's own history, memory and the family stay out of any Hearthwell ad.

## Civitas Automata *(working name)*

- **What it made:** civic and service robots (mail, clinics, utilities, shops, traffic); design language and company slogan in [Lore.md, section 7](Lore.md#7-the-robot-makers). It made most of the robots in Wellspring and minted the pinions as company scrip ([section 8](Lore.md#8-the-currency)).
- **Era and status:** pre-war. Gone as a company; its machines still run the town's shops, clinic and water, and its coin is still the town's money.

- **Where it appears:** Hob's dispenser line in the slice; the town's robots, the administrator and Sunny the weather broadcaster ([Lore.md, sections 10 and 11](Lore.md#10-characters)); Sal and Cleo. Not yet in a devlog piece.
- **Voice in print:** civic, upright and numbered. Forms, schedules, notices "by order of", reference codes and polite instructions. The humour is bureaucratic: a rule kept perfectly long after its reason is gone.
- **Guardrails:**
  - The clinic register, its "transferred" lines and anything near the trials stay out of Civitas print; a Salus piece may be the slogan and the waiting room, nothing more ([Lore.md decision log, 2026-11-15](Lore.md#13-decision-log)). The trials' euphemisms are never used for jokes.
  - Sal and Cleo appear in a devlog piece only once a player has met them in the build ([Devlog.md](Devlog.md)).
  - "Pinions" is a working name; until it is final, a public piece says "coins".

## Ironside Dynamics *(working name)*

- **What it made:** war machines, security and curfew robots; design language and company slogan in [Lore.md, section 7](Lore.md#7-the-robot-makers). It made most of the hostile robots.
- **Era and status:** pre-war, built up in the years of the defence race. Gone as a company; its machines are among the hostile robots, and some, like Gage, chose other lives.
- **Product lines already named:**

| Product | What it is | Slogan | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pioneer PR-520 "Field Sapper" | Heavy sapper and infrastructure maintenance unit (Gage) | "Built to Endure. Engineered to Overcome." | [Engineer.md](Characters/Engineer.md) |

- **Where it appears:** Gage's sheet and the Iron Creed's ([IronCreed.md](Factions/IronCreed.md)); the hostile robots ([Lore.md, section 6](Lore.md#hostile-robots)). Not yet in the game's texts or a devlog piece.
- **Voice in print:** terse, armoured and certain. Short claims, few faces, safety sold as strength. The humour is in the overconfidence, never in what the machines did.
- **Guardrails:**
  - Only the pre-war catalogue: civil defence, security, maintenance. Never a machine at work in the Four Days, never the war's causes, and never who struck first.
  - Curfew units and the Iron Creed's frame conversions stay out of Ironside print.
  - Its ads are the darkest of the makers' and the easiest to tip into bad taste; give the joke to the product's promise, not to what it was for.

## Sure-Sprout *(working name)*

- **What it sold:** garden seed, by catalogue, to households with a home robot. Its promise was that the garden would look like it was growing.
- **Era and status:** pre-war. Who made it, and whether it belonged to one of the makers, is not settled; leave it open.
- **Where it appears:** the devlog post "Hello from the farm" (2026-11-27): a seed advertisement still pinned inside the barn door, voice `sure-sprout`, stamp "Spring catalogue, page 12." Source: `../VestigeDevlog/content/posts/2026-11-27-hello-from-the-farm/index.md` in the devlog repository; listed in [Devlog.md](Devlog.md#published-pieces). Not yet in the game.
- **Voice in print:** opens on a worry, then waves it away ("Stop worrying!"); a bright promise with an asterisk, and the footnote takes it back. The home robot does the work so the customer never has to. Tagline from that ad: "Sure-Sprout. Because a garden should look like it's trying." Its seeds are "grown to be seen".
- **Guardrails:**
  - Already public, so its name, tagline and footnote habit are fixed by that piece; a later piece keeps them.
  - The joke is cosmetic growth and small print. Never Cinderium, contamination, mutation or anything glowing: Sure-Sprout's promises are about looks, not safety.
  - Keep its crops to ordinary garden vegetables.
