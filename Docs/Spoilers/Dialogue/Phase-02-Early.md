# Phase 2 — Early: Relationships and local records

> **Spoilers.** These drafts cover the town's repair effort, character arcs, the first route visits and ambiguous early clues.

## E-01 — Repair huddle

**Placement:** The pump team compares a shift log, the Keeper operators' practical knowledge, and Cleo's diagrams.
**Speakers:** PLAYER, GAGE, CLEO, KEEPER OPERATOR.
**Knowledge:** Everyone is discussing the failing aquifer system. No one has evidence of the Continuance Trials.

**GAGE:** The old plan puts the bypass on the east wall. The pipe in front of me puts it somewhere else.

**CLEO:** The plan is dated before the Four Days. It may show an earlier revision.

**KEEPER OPERATOR:** It shows where the pipe used to be. We moved it after the first freeze cracked the housing.

**GAGE:** That would have been useful to know before I measured the wall.

**KEEPER OPERATOR:** It is in the shift book.

**CLEO:** The shift book is in a different room from the plan. Neither record points to the other.

**PLAYER:** Could we add the repair note to both?

**KEEPER OPERATOR:** We could. The operator on duty still needs to check the line before anyone touches it.

**GAGE:** Agreed. A note is not a safety check.

**CLEO:** And the diagram is not the whole system. I'll mark the revision and cite the operator who explained it.

**GAGE:** Good. Then we have a plan we can actually use.

*[At the next assembly, if the town agrees to try a shared handoff. This is a time-limited trial, not a permanent decision.]*

**TOWN ADMINISTRATOR:** The assembly agreed to try the shared log through the season. Then we'll ask the operators whether it's helping.

**KEEPER OPERATOR:** We'll keep the pump details with the operators. The public notice will say what's running and what isn't.

**PLAYER:** And if it makes things worse?

**KEEPER OPERATOR:** Then we change it. A trial only helps if we're willing to learn from it.

**TOWN ADMINISTRATOR:** You helped get the idea in the room. The town decides what to do with it.

## E-02 — Hob's next purpose

**Placement:** The player asks Hob whether they will stay at the farm or join the town's repair work.
**Knowledge:** Hob is free to decide; no choice is an extension of the old order.

**PLAYER:** The town could use another pair of hands. Would you want to go?

**HOB:** I might. I know this farm well. I don't know the town's pump.

**PLAYER:** You don't have to prove anything to me.

**HOB:** I know. That's part of why I'm thinking about it.

**PLAYER — CHOICES**

- **[Encourage Hob to decide later]** "Take your time. You can try a day and see how it feels."
  - **HOB:** I would like to try that. A day is not a life sentence.
- **[Offer the farm as an option]** "You can stay here, too. I don't want you to feel you have to leave."
  - **HOB:** Thank you. I want the choice to be real, not a test of whether I am useful.
- **[Ask what Hob wants]** "If nobody needed anything from you, what would you choose?"
  - **HOB:** I don't know yet. But I would like to find out.

## E-03 — Gage's inspection

**Placement:** The sleeper visits Gage at the aquifer station during an inspection.
**Knowledge:** Gage is cautious around a human at dangerous machinery. The sleeper is not an instant pump expert.

**GAGE:** Stop there. That flange is under pressure, and the grating is slick.

**GAGE:** I heard you were awake. No one told me what you knew.

**PLAYER:** You could have asked.

**GAGE:** I could have. I didn't. That's on me.

**PLAYER — CHOICES**

- **[Stay back]** "Understood. Tell me where it's safe to stand."
  - **GAGE:** Behind the yellow rail. Watch your footing on the second plate.
- **[Point out the vibration]** "That shudder comes and goes with the intake flap. It doesn't sound like the whole casing."
  - **GAGE:** Hold on. There. It does pulse with the flap. Good catch. Hand me the socket on the bench, if you can reach it without crossing the rail.
- **[Challenge his tone]** "You can warn me without talking as if I can't think."
  - **GAGE:** Fair. The warning stands; the assumption doesn't. The rail is safe. Stay there while I check the valve.

## E-04 — The coupling and pressure test

**Placement:** A later repair day combines the search for a compatible legacy coupling with the controlled pressure test. The tests can be split into separate quests in implementation.
**Knowledge:** Gage leads technical inspection. Operators control their equipment. The player contributes observations and helps coordinate; the operator makes no unsafe move without authorization.

**KEEPER OPERATOR:** The replacement coupling from the Bastion doesn't fit the old line. The thread pitch is wrong.

**GAGE:** I checked twice. It's the closest part we have.

**PLAYER:** There's a fitting in the farm shed with that mark. I can bring it over, if you're willing to inspect it.

**GAGE:** You recognize the maker's mark?

**PLAYER:** My family used the same kind of pipe. I can't promise it's right. I can show you where I found it.

**GAGE:** That's useful. I'll inspect it. No one installs it until the operator signs off.

*[After the fitting is verified and installed, the team begins a controlled pressure test. The test branch must match the player's choice.]*

**KEEPER OPERATOR:** Pressure is climbing, and the bypass is chattering. We need to stop or bleed it down.

**GAGE:** I want one more reading. The Bastion expects a certified test today.

**PLAYER — CHOICES**

- **[Emergency abort]** "Stop the test. The reading can wait; the people in this room can't."
  - **GAGE:** Operator, open the bleed. Everyone behind the marked line.
  - **KEEPER OPERATOR:** The pressure is down. The reservoir has silt in it, and the shear pin is cracked.
  - **GAGE:** Then we flush it, replace the pin and test tomorrow. The warning was right. I wanted the certificate more than I wanted another reading.
- **[Push ahead]** "Give it a few seconds. We came here to finish the test."
  - **GAGE:** Hold your stations. Five seconds.
  - **KEEPER OPERATOR:** The gasket's gone. Shutoff is closed.
  - **GAGE:** I heard the warning and kept going. That was my call. We replace the seal, clean the spill and test again when the operator clears it.
- **[Stepped bleed-down]** "Open the bypass a little at a time. Keep watching the lower gauge and stop if it moves the wrong way."
  - **GAGE:** Operator, take it in small turns. Call every change.
  - **KEEPER OPERATOR:** Pressure is settling. No silt in the reservoir. No new damage.
  - **GAGE:** Good. We stop here and record exactly what the test did—and did not—prove.

## E-05 — Gage's report

**Placement:** After the pressure-test outcome, Gage explains his own past failure and prepares a report for the Bastion.
**Knowledge:** This is Gage's experience, not a statement about all Iron Creed members or the pressure-test researchers.

**GAGE:** I used to think a failed repair was the worst thing I could report.

**PLAYER:** What changed?

**GAGE:** At the Bastion, I rushed a turbine repair because command wanted the line back that day. I missed a stress crack. The housing went, and the slag pinned my lower frame.

**PLAYER:** Someone got you out?

**GAGE:** Another engineer. They were ordered to pull back. They ignored it, cut me free, and we went back to rebuild the housing properly.

**PLAYER:** They came back for you.

**GAGE:** I hadn't thought a human could be a partner in that kind of work.

**PLAYER:** What will you put in the report?

**GAGE:** What we found. Who made each call. What failed, if anything, and what the next crew needs to know. No polishing the numbers to protect my standing.

**PLAYER — CHOICES**

- **[Ask him to credit the whole team]** "Make sure the operators and the people who checked the coupling are named."
  - **GAGE:** They will be. A report that leaves out the people who did the work is a bad report.
- **[Ask about the Bastion invitation]** "Does this mean I can visit?"
  - **GAGE:** It means I can request a route and vouch for the work you did. The decision to invite you is theirs.

## E-06 — Readiness at the Bastion

**Placement:** Optional story after the sleeper has a route to the Bastion. A defense gap or nearby threat is discovered; its exact nature remains undecided.
**Speakers:** PLAYER, GAGE, COORDINATOR, CREED MEMBER.
**Knowledge:** The group discusses only what they have verified about this threat. No single character chooses on behalf of all Creed members.

**CREED MEMBER:** If we reinforce the outer approach, we can hold the line here. If the traders turn back, that's their problem.

**COORDINATOR:** And send the danger toward the road the traders use. That protects this wall by putting someone else in its path.

**GAGE:** Before either of you commit, we need to know what the threat can reach and what the old barrier can take.

**PLAYER:** I can show you where I saw the damage. I don't know what caused it.

**GAGE:** Good distinction. We'll inspect before we name it.

**CREED MEMBER:** The sleeper wants us to wait?

**PLAYER — CHOICES**

- **[Ask for a shared inspection]** "I want you to know what the evidence says before you decide. The choice is yours."
  - **COORDINATOR:** That's fair. We inspect the approach and report back to the people who use it.
- **[Support a protective plan]** "Prepare the defense, but don't push the danger onto the road."
  - **GAGE:** Then the plan needs a way to protect the road too. We won't call it readiness if someone else pays for the gap.
- **[Decline to decide for them]** "I can help with the inspection. I won't tell the Creed what it should believe."
  - **COORDINATOR:** That is not your burden to carry.

**CREED MEMBER:** An inspection won't settle what we owe our neighbors.

**GAGE:** No. But it will keep us from pretending we know more than we do.

## E-07 — Cleo's request and record limits

**Placement:** Cleo asks for access to pod logs and possibly a bounded interview, then works with a Keeper operator on Wellspring's records.
**Knowledge:** Her request names access, storage, audience, retention and withdrawal terms before the sleeper chooses.

**CLEO:** I want to compare the pod's technical logs with the aquifer records. The ration cards might help me understand how your family managed before the war, but I'd only ask about them if you want to talk.

**PLAYER:** What happens to the copies?

**CLEO:** I'll keep a protected working copy while I compare them. When I'm done, I'll delete it and my notes.

**PLAYER:** And if you want to send something to the Archive?

**CLEO:** Nothing goes to the Archive unless you approve it. The Review Council and water researchers would be the only readers, and you can ask me to remove it later.

**PLAYER:** Can I change my mind?

**CLEO:** Yes. You can stop the scan or withdraw later. I'll remove my copy and ask the Archive to remove theirs.

**PLAYER — CHOICES**

- **[Technical logs only]** "You can read the pod logs. No questions about my family. I'll review anything you plan to share."
  - **CLEO:** Understood. Just the logs, then. I won't ask about your family, and nothing goes to the Archive without you seeing it first.
- **[Logs and bounded interview]** "You can ask about the ration cards, but only those. I still approve any excerpt first."
  - **CLEO:** Agreed. Just the ration cards. You can change the excerpt or withdraw it at any point.
- **[Refuse]** "No. Don't access the pod or ask about my family."
  - **CLEO:** Understood. No pod logs and no interview. I'll compare the public station records instead.

*[Later, at the records desk.]*

**CLEO:** The diagram says the intake is capped. The operator says they moved the line after a freeze.

**KEEPER OPERATOR:** We did. I can show you the repair mark.

**CLEO:** Then I need to update the diagram. Show me where the line moved.

**PLAYER:** And the town's private details?

**CLEO:** The assembly decides what leaves Wellspring.

**KEEPER OPERATOR:** The fault history can go out. The cistern location and our remaining defense stock cannot.

**CLEO:** Then that's what I'll send. I'll note that the town withheld those details, not fill them in.

*[At higher trust, after Cleo has honored Wellspring's limits.]*

**CLEO:** I would like to invite you to the Archive. You helped me understand that the people who live with a system know things its plans leave out.

**PLAYER:** Is this because I let you read the pod logs?

**CLEO:** No. The invitation is separate. You can say no.

**PLAYER:** What would I do there?

**CLEO:** See how our researchers review a claim, and meet the people who will read my Wellspring study. I can't promise they will agree with every limit I set.

*[At the Review Council's discussion of Cleo's monograph.]*

**REVIEWER:** Your account omits the cistern location and the reserve inventory. A future engineer may need both.

**CLEO:** Wellspring agreed to share the repair history, not its cistern map or reserve stock.

**REVIEWER:** Then the record is incomplete.

**CLEO:** It's the account they agreed to share. I've marked what they kept back.

**PLAYER:** Do you want me to answer?

**CLEO:** No. I can defend the limits. You don't need to speak for them.

## E-08 — The nomad's first agreement

**Placement:** The nomad's first authored visit at the farm gate.
**Knowledge:** The nomad is a trader and route runner, not a guide to all surviving humans. Their request does not reveal a contact or destination.

**NOMAD:** Could you keep this weather tube under the dry eave until I come back? It stays sealed. There's no name or destination in it.

**PLAYER — CHOICES**

- **[Accept]** "I can keep it dry and leave it sealed."
  - **NOMAD:** Good. I like knowing where we stand.
- **[Ask about the risk]** "What happens if someone finds it?"
  - **NOMAD:** They find a weather tube. I don't put anyone's name or location in it.
- **[Decline]** "I'm not comfortable holding something I can't inspect."
  - **NOMAD:** Fair answer. Better than agreeing and resenting it later.

**PLAYER:** Why trust me with it?

**NOMAD:** I don't know you yet. I know what you agreed to do. That's enough for one small favor.

## E-09 — The route marker

**Placement:** The sleeper helps the nomad inspect a silent route marker.
**Knowledge:** Damage or tampering is observable; the fate of any contact is not.

**NOMAD:** The marker should answer by now.

**PLAYER:** Could the storm have damaged it?

**NOMAD:** Could have. So could age, an animal, or someone passing through. We check the casing before we decide.

**PLAYER:** There's a fresh scrape on the lower edge.

**NOMAD:** That's what I see too. The casing is damaged. It doesn't tell us who caused it, or why.

**PLAYER:** Do we wait?

**NOMAD:** We leave the signal quiet and report the damage through the safe channel. No names. No guesses.

**PLAYER:** And if nobody answers?

**NOMAD:** Then we still won't know why. We'll plan for the route to be unsafe until we have better evidence.

## E-10 — Town life, forecast and a first irregularity

**Placement:** Optional everyday encounters, the next day's forecast, and an isolated clinic or municipal record clue.
**Knowledge:** The clue can mean several things. It does not establish a trial program or identify a perpetrator.

*[At the old broadcast tower, the evening forecast is playing.]*

**WEATHER BROADCASTER:** Good evening, Wellspring. Tomorrow's forecast: high clouds, a dust front near noon, and a chance of a radiation storm. Please secure loose items and stay under cover if the warning sounds. Have a pleasant evening.

**PLAYER:** You make that sound almost relaxing.

**WEATHER BROADCASTER:** That's the cheerful sign-off. The radiation warning is real, though.

**PLAYER:** I know. I'll keep an eye out.

**WEATHER BROADCASTER:** Good. I'll keep the warning clear.

**CLINIC ROBOT:** Your pulse is fast.

**PLAYER:** You could ask before scanning me.

**CLINIC ROBOT:** You're right. I saw a patient and reached for the monitor. Old habit. Should I stop?

**PLAYER:** Yes.

**CLINIC ROBOT:** Stopped. If you want help later, ask. If you don't, I won't take a reading.

*[The player notices a clinic ledger with one name missing from a sequence.]*

**PLAYER:** The name's torn off this entry, and it says "transferred." Was someone treated here?

**CLINIC ROBOT:** I can't tell what happened from this copy. The page was torn before it reached my cabinet.

**PLAYER:** Could "transferred" mean they went somewhere else?

**CLINIC ROBOT:** It could. The note doesn't say where. It might have been routine. I don't know.

**PLAYER:** We should ask Cleo?

**CLINIC ROBOT:** She may know how these records were kept. I don't know what happened.

*[At the Founders' Fair.]*

**TOWN ADMINISTRATOR:** Welcome to the Founders' Fair. The stalls are open.

**PLAYER:** What are they selling?

**TOWN ADMINISTRATOR:** Nothing. It is a civic fair. The stalls are for displaying things.

**PLAYER:** What are we celebrating?

**TOWN ADMINISTRATOR:** The original answer has been lost. The assembly decided the fair was still worth keeping.

**PLAYER:** Then what do people display?

**TOWN ADMINISTRATOR:** Repairs, recipes, and objects that are difficult to classify. Last year, someone displayed a chair with three legs and called it "ongoing."

*[Optional everyday line with the town administrator.]*

**TOWN ADMINISTRATOR:** The council chamber is open.

**PLAYER:** Is there a meeting?

**TOWN ADMINISTRATOR:** Not today. I keep opening it anyway. The room sounds less empty when the chairs are in order.

## E-11 — Family traces continue

**MAIL CARRIER:** I have a letter for this address.

**PLAYER:** It's been decades.

**MAIL CARRIER:** My route says to deliver it. It does not say the recipient is still home.

**PLAYER:** Do you know who sent it?

**MAIL CARRIER:** No. My route card only has the delivery address.

**PLAYER:** It's still sealed.

**MAIL CARRIER:** My route says to deliver it, not open it.

**PLAYER:** Do you know where they went?

**MAIL CARRIER:** The address is the only thing on my route card. I don't know where your parents went.

**HOB:** We can read it now, or keep it sealed. Either is all right.

**PLAYER:** Let's decide after dinner.

**HOB:** All right. It can wait until you are ready.
