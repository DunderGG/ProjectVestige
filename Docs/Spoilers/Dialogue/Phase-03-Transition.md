# Phase 3 - Transition: A pattern emerges

> **Spoilers.** These drafts let independent clues accumulate without resolving more than the characters can prove.

## T-01 - Independent clues

**Placement:** The sleeper compares a clinic entry with a separate civic shipment record.
**Knowledge:** The records are suspicious together, but still incomplete. Cleo knows only what the player shows her.

**PLAYER:** The clinic copy has a name missing. This shipping log has a sealed medical crate sent to the old institute.

**CLEO:** Do they mention each other?

**PLAYER:** No. Different places. Different records.

**CLEO:** Then let's keep them separate until we have more.

**PLAYER:** The clinic entry just says "transferred."

**CLEO:** No destination. It could be routine.

**PLAYER:** Do you think someone meant to hide something?

**CLEO:** Maybe. But this copy doesn't tell us who hid it or how.

**PLAYER - CHOICES**

- **[Ask Cleo to preserve the uncertainty]** "Keep the original wording, but mark it unverified."
  - **CLEO:** Yes. Unverified, not explained away.
- **[Ask to look for another source]** "Can we find a second record before we draw a line between them?"
  - **CLEO:** We can ask the clinic operator what they know and search the institute index. Neither is a guarantee.
- **[Stop investigating for now]** "I need a break from this."
  - **CLEO:** Of course. The documents will still be here when you return.

## T-02 - The copied page

**Placement:** The nomad discovers that a route note has been copied. The old signal may be unsafe, but the nomad does not know who copied it or who has seen it.
**Knowledge:** The page reveals a pattern, not a contact's identity or location. Do not have the nomad share names to make the player useful.

**NOMAD:** This page has enough of the marker to make the next run less safe.

**PLAYER:** Can someone use it to find the people on your route?

**NOMAD:** Not from this alone. But it gives somebody a place to start. I won't wait until it gives them the rest.

**PLAYER:** We could warn everyone.

**NOMAD:** I'll warn the next stops through the usual channel. No names on the copied page.

**PLAYER - CHOICES**

- **[Offer to help change the markers]** "We can replace the signal and warn the stops that use it."
  - **NOMAD:** Yes. A few trusted stops can share the work. I don't have to carry every leg myself.
- **[Suggest closing the route]** "Close it now. We can decide later whether it's safe."
  - **NOMAD:** That may be right for one leg. Closing the whole circuit without warning would leave people waiting for supplies. Let's separate those decisions.
- **[Ask who is at risk]** "Tell me the names. I could help more if I knew."
  - **NOMAD:** Out on the salt flats, sleeper, a loose tongue gets someone's water cache looted before sundown. You want to help? Help me change the trail signal. Leave the names where they belong—in heads that know how to keep quiet.

**PLAYER:** What do you need from me?

**NOMAD:** Help change the public signal at the farm stop. The rest stays between the people who use those routes.

## T-03 - The farm as a limited stop

**Placement:** The nomad, Hob, the sleeper and a Wellspring representative discuss a possible rest and trade stop. A later confirmation follows the assembly's own discussion.
**Knowledge:** No names, locations or contact list are shared. The farm's residents, the assembly and the nomad each decide for themselves. This meeting can produce only a proposal; it cannot activate the stop.

**NOMAD:** I want to ask whether this farm could be a stop on the route. Rest, water if you have it, and trade. Nothing more unless everyone agrees later.

**HOB:** The farm is not only mine to offer. I live here, and the sleeper lives here.

**PLAYER:** What would visitors be expected to tell us?

**NOMAD:** Nothing about where they came from or where they're going. A stop should not demand a map as its price.

**WELLSPRING REPRESENTATIVE:** The farm's call is separate from the town's. I can take a proposal to the assembly, but they'll decide for themselves after talking through the risks.

**PLAYER - CHOICES**

- **[Discuss safety first]** "What happens if someone gets caught in a flash freeze or a radiation squall?"
  - **NOMAD:** Smart thinking. We can write down what a safe stop would need. Blankets and water are ideas for the list, not supplies anyone has promised.
- **[Discuss privacy first]** "Visitors shouldn't have to tell us where they came from or where they're going."
  - **NOMAD:** Right. No names, no map, no questions they don't want to answer. Trade stays a fair swap, not an entry fee.
- **[Decline for now]** "I can't offer the farm as a trail stop."
  - **NOMAD:** Fair enough! Better to say no plainly than say yes and jump at every footprint in the mud. No hard feelings.
  - **Outcome:** The proposal ends here. No supplies are set aside, no marker is raised, and the route does not use the farm as a stop.

After discussing the terms, each person answers separately. The sleeper's choice speaks only for them:

**PLAYER - CHOICES**

- **[Agree to the proposal]** "I'm willing to offer a limited rest and trade stop, if Hob agrees and Wellspring makes its own decision."
- **[Decline]** "I don't want the farm to host a route stop."

**HOB - IF THE SLEEPER AGREES, HOB'S OWN ANSWER IS STILL SEPARATE:**

**HOB:** I would be glad to share the farm as a rest and trade stop, if that is what you want too. I won't offer your home for you.

**HOB - IF HOB DECLINES:**

**HOB:** I know the farm could help, but I don't want to make it a route stop. Please leave the marker down.

**HOB - IF THE SLEEPER DECLINES:**

**HOB:** Then the farm is not a stop. A warm stove is only hospitality when the people who live here choose it.

Hob's answer is an independent authored yes-or-no branch, never inferred from the sleeper's choice. If either the sleeper or Hob declines, the nomad accepts the answer and the proposal ends. No supplies are set aside, no marker is raised, and the route does not use the farm as a stop. If both agree, the representative takes the proposed terms to the assembly; that referral is not town approval.

**FOLLOW-UP - AFTER THE ASSEMBLY HAS DECIDED**

**WELLSPRING REPRESENTATIVE - IF THE ASSEMBLY APPROVES:** The assembly agrees to a limited public rest and trade stop, under the terms we discussed. No route details or private destinations are part of it.

**WELLSPRING REPRESENTATIVE - IF THE ASSEMBLY DECLINES:** The assembly isn't willing to make the town part of this stop. That's their decision. The farm's choice is separate, but this joint route proposal cannot go ahead without the town's agreement.

The nomad then decides whether the approved terms work for the route:

- **If the assembly declines:** The nomad thanks the representative and does not activate a town-facing stop. No supplies are set aside and no marker is raised.
- **If the assembly approves and the sleeper and Hob both agreed:** The nomad may accept the terms: "Then, if you both still mean it, I'll add this stop to the route. Nobody owes me a bed, a story or a map." Only after all four parties have agreed may the group choose which supplies to set aside and raise the marker by the gatepost.
- **If any party withdraws or declines before activation:** The nomad accepts the change. The stop remains inactive; no supplies are committed and the marker stays down.

**HOB:** If you borrow a neighbor's kettle, you don't rummage through their cupboards while the water boils. The nomad's friends are their own folk; our work is keeping the gate mended and the stove warm.
## T-04 - What the clues do and do not say

**Placement:** After at least two independent clues have been found. The group discusses whether to continue toward the institute.
**Speakers:** PLAYER, CLEO, GAGE, WELLSPRING RESIDENT.
**Knowledge:** Gage and the resident only know the evidence presented in this scene. The clue set suggests human subjects may have been moved or studied; it does not identify a procedure or responsible person.

**WELLSPRING RESIDENT:** If that institute is active, I don't want strangers following a trail back to our door.

**PLAYER:** We don't know that it is active.

**WELLSPRING RESIDENT:** We don't know that it isn't. An unmapped cellar out in the dust is like an unexploded mortar shell. You don't tap it with a hammer just to see if the powder's dry.

**CLEO:** Ink on old clinic vouchers gives us cause to ask questions. But an old requisition slip doesn't give us leave to pry open Wellspring's root cellars or sketch arrows pointing straight to your cisterns.

**GAGE:** A blinking signal in an abandoned bunker doesn't mean you charge in with a wrench. We scout the perimeter, test the seismic shoring, and if the concrete looks rotten, we pull back.

**PLAYER:** Could these be ordinary medical transfers?

**CLEO:** They could. The missing name and the shipment are concerning, but they don't prove what happened to anyone.

**PLAYER - CHOICES**

- **[Suggest a careful search]** "Let's look for another source, without bringing anyone from town."
  - **WELLSPRING RESIDENT:** If the assembly agrees to that limit, I can live with it.
- **[Prioritize the town's safety]** "We pause until we know what the institute could expose."
  - **GAGE:** Sound engineering. Checking your retreat line before you breach the bulkhead isn't cowardice; it's keeping your crew alive.
- **[Ask the resident what would help]** "What conditions would make you feel safer?"
  - **WELLSPRING RESIDENT:** No public route, no town names in a report, and a way to stop if the risk changes.

## T-05 - Optional survivor encounters

**Placement:** Use only if an optional survivor path is selected. These are adaptable placeholders; do not combine them into a single convenient guide or make one survivor speak for others.

### A shelter survivor

**Knowledge:** This person can speak only for their own experience. Their age, name, community and specific history remain to be developed.

**SHELTER SURVIVOR:** You're looking for the people who stayed underground?

**PLAYER:** No. I came because someone said you might be willing to talk.

**SHELTER SURVIVOR:** I might be willing to talk about me. Don't ask me to explain every person who lived there.

**PLAYER - CHOICES**

- **[Respect the limit]** "Then I'll ask what you want to tell me."
  - **SHELTER SURVIVOR:** Better. Start with why you're here.
- **[Ask about safety]** "Is there anything you need before we talk?"
  - **SHELTER SURVIVOR:** A clear way to leave. Keep that open, and we'll see.
- **[Offer to leave]** "We can stop. You don't owe me your story."
  - **SHELTER SURVIVOR:** I know. I'm not done talking yet. Just don't rush me.

### A second sleeper

**Knowledge:** The characters share the disorientation of waking into a changed world. Their pod, past and beliefs are distinct and undecided.

**SECOND SLEEPER:** You knew the world before?

**PLAYER:** A little. I don't know this one yet.

**SECOND SLEEPER:** Good. I thought I was the only one who couldn't recognize anything.

**PLAYER:** We don't have to compare everything today.

**SECOND SLEEPER:** Thank you. I don't have answers ready just because I woke up.

**PLAYER:** Do you need anything?

**SECOND SLEEPER:** Water, maybe. And a little time.

## T-06 - The question

**Placement:** A late transition scene where the player decides whether to stay with the evidence or choose a broader, riskier search.
**Knowledge:** The town's safety limits and the possibility of a larger pattern are both real.

**PLAYER:** I can keep chasing the records, or I can stop and ask what the town is willing to carry.

**CLEO:** We need both. We cannot call the pattern proven without a second source, and we cannot pretend a risk does not exist because it is inconvenient.

**GAGE:** The route is the boundary. If it is unsafe, no one goes beyond it.

**PLAYER - CHOICES**

- **[Keep the search narrow]** "We keep the search narrow. One more source to confirm the trail, then we see what the town can handle."
  - **CLEO:** Methodical and disciplined. One more source, verified under proper light, before we lay the pages on the town table.
- **[Pause and ask the town]** "We stop here. We tell the town what we've found, and let them decide whether to brace for trouble."
  - **GAGE:** Sound call. If the town's timbers aren't stout enough to brace the tunnel, you don't keep digging deeper.
- **[Push for a broader search]** "We keep looking. The pattern is already large enough that I don't want to pretend it's small."
  - **CLEO:** Then we set strict survey markers. We aren't chasing ghosts across the wasteland on a raw hunch.

**PLAYER:** Then we do this carefully.

**CLEO:** Yes. With dry ink and steady hands.
