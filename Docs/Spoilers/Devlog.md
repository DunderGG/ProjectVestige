# Devlog in-world pieces

> **Spoilers.** This names the voice behind the devlog's postcards and what each piece commits to canon. The devlog itself never does.

Every devlog post, published at the separate devlog site, ends with a short, clearly fictional piece from inside the world. @DunderGG decided this on 2026-11-27 ([Lore.md, section 13](Lore.md#13-decision-log)), first for some posts and the same evening for every post, with the voice changing from post to post.

## The postcard voice

The postcards are written by Morrow, the Authority's intermediary ([character sheet](Characters/AuthorityIntermediary.md)), at a time before they learn anything about what the Authority is doing. They are never named in public, and the reader is never meant to work out who writes.

## Spoiler rules

These hold for every piece, whatever the voice.

- **No faction.** Never name the Authority or use its vocabulary (assignment, program, service roles, the Continuance as a mandate, its seal).
- **Settled facts only.** A piece may hint at a fact about the person only when it is settled in this folder, and at the world no deeper than the early chapter: what the player can know in Phases 1 and 2 of [StoryTimeline.md](StoryTimeline.md) before "First irregularities" (ordinary life, weather, haze, farming, towns). Never the war's causes, the trials or the family's fate.
- **Never the body.** No hint of the mechanical body, the unchanging face or the conversion.
- **No identity.** No name, pronoun, place or past.
- **No invitation.** No call to action, no "come find me", no address, nothing the reader is asked to do.
- **No knowing wink.** Nothing that tells the reader a secret is there to find.

## Form

- 3 to 6 short lines, about 70 words.
- Opens with a header: a short in-world description of what the piece is ("A postcard with no address, held at the post office for collection."). No "From the world of the game" label; the separate card under the sign-off marks it as fiction.
- Ends with a stamp line. On a postcard or a transmission: a 24-hour time and the weather ("18:20. Haze thinning."). On a notice (an ad, notice or sign): the printed thing's own small line, such as a catalogue page or a print code ("Spring catalogue, page 12."), never a time, since a printed thing wasn't written at a moment.
- Echoes its post: the piece picks up the post's subject, mood or a detail of it, in the world's terms.

## Frequency

Every post (@DunderGG, 2026-11-27, replacing "about one in four, never two in a row"). Never announced or labelled as a series. The voice changes from post to post, so the same voice doesn't end two posts in a row.

## Other voices

All under the same spoiler rules. The devlog site draws each with its own look, set by the piece's `kind`: `postcard`, `notice` or `transmission`.

- **Old ads, notices and signs** (`notice`): printed things still around from before the war, or put up since. The brand behind a printed piece has its voice and guardrails in [Brands.md](Brands.md); a brand not yet listed there gets its entry before the piece is written.
- **Characters the game already shows** (Hob, the farm's machines): only once a player has met them in the build, and never naming or introducing anyone a player hasn't met.
- **Intercepted transmissions from Morrow** (`transmission`; approved by @DunderGG on 2026-11-27): occasional, a few lines, clearly fiction, hinting only at lore that won't change and checked against this folder. The speaker is never named and the transmission never reveals who they speak for or that they speak for anyone; all the postcard rules above apply.

## Who writes and approves

The devlog editor never writes these pieces. The writer (`vestige-writer`) writes each one, and @DunderGG approves each piece before it is published.

Before publishing, check:

1. Every spoiler rule above.
2. Lore consistency: each fact hinted is settled in this folder and matches the decision log.
3. Length (3 to 6 lines, about 70 words) and the stamp line.
4. The previous post's piece used a different voice.
5. @DunderGG's approval of this piece.

Then add the piece's row to the table below.

The public-side text in the devlog repository (its `CLAUDE.md` and `PUBLISHING.md`) is spoiler-free: it says such pieces exist and who writes and approves them, and names no character or faction.
