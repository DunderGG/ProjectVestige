# Alice

> **Spoilers.** This is a provisional character concept and relationship outline. Alice is a woman from before the war who woke from a separate cryo pod. She is in the planned release roster and is a potential human romance option; her detailed history, public identity and full romance-arc scope remain open.

Alice is an adult human from before the war who woke from a separate cryo pod and has since begun building a life of her own. Her independent project is a creative or community practice with value beyond survival. She is a potential human romance option in the release roster, but romance is not the reason she exists, the solution to her survival, or the condition for her project to continue.

## 1. Character Identity

- **Name:** Alice. Whether she used this name before the war or chose it after waking is open.
- **Species and origin:** Human; a woman from before the war who woke from a cryo pod separate from the player's. The pod's maker, location, condition, and the circumstances and date of awakening remain open.
- **Age:** An adult. Exact age and the amount of time spent awake remain open.
- **Gender and pronouns:** Woman; she/her.
- **Role:** A self-directed survivor in the planned release roster and a potential romance option. Whether she receives one of the three full romance arcs remains open. She is not a representative of all humans, a source of essential progression, or a farmhand by default.
- **Appearance and field gear:** Open. Her design should reflect the life she has made since waking, not simply repeat the sleeper's cryo-pod appearance.
- **Mannerisms and dialogue voice:** Warm, candid and focused on the present. She speaks conversationally and expressively, with concrete wording. When an old custom or service she assumes still exists has changed, she can react with curiosity and adjust; dry, self-aware humor may soften those moments. Avoid archaic diction, constant nostalgia, or making every conversation a history lesson. No in-character dialogue has been drafted.

## 2. Worldview and Personal Conflict

- **Standing:** An individual human survivor from before the war. Her existence does not establish a nearby human settlement or resolve how many people survived worldwide.
- **View of the sleeper:** The shared experience of waking into a changed world may create understanding, but does not make Alice and the sleeper automatic friends or romantic partners. Alice has her own history and boundaries.
- **View of robots:** Not settled. Her views should grow from her own encounters, rather than restating the sleeper's perspective or making her a spokesperson for human-robot relations.
- **Want:** To sustain a recurring creative or community practice that gives people a reason to gather beyond immediate survival. The medium, location, participants and specific outcome remain open.
- **Contradiction:** A possible starting conflict is that she judges her own project by whether it is practically useful, even while wanting to make room for something whose value is shared experience. This is a writing direction to confirm, not final characterization.
- **Independent agency:** The sleeper may contribute materials, practical help or a perspective, but does not own the project, choose its purpose, or make it succeed on Alice's behalf. Alice and willing participants choose what happens to it.
- **Relationships outside the sleeper:** To be developed. Her life must include concerns and connections that continue on the friendship path and are not merely waiting for the sleeper to arrive.

## 3. Routine and Availability

- The place where Alice lives and is first encountered is undecided. She should already be awake and making her own way when the sleeper meets her; the meeting is not a rescue from a sealed pod.
- Her visits and story events should be predictable enough to follow without requiring the player to catch a random appearance.
- Severe weather can delay travel or gatherings, but should not permanently cancel a relationship event or punish the player for missing a day.
- A detailed daily schedule should wait until the character's location and the relevant maps are decided.

## 4. Community Project and Gifts

### Community Project

The provisional project is a recurring, voluntary gathering where people can make or share something together. Its exact form—such as a performance, craft, or another creative practice—is open. It should welcome human and robot contributions without suggesting that creativity belongs to humans alone. Sharing a story, making an object, or attending should never require a participant to disclose private history.

The gathering is optional character content, not a town service, human hub, or requirement for farm or story progression. It can continue and change whether the sleeper chooses friendship or romance.

### Item Preferences and Gifting

Specific likes, dislikes and gift dialogue are undecided until the project's form is chosen. Gifts should remain optional social gestures: they do not earn access to private history, consent, romance, or the community project.

## 5. Relationship Progression and Narrative Arc

The arc should establish trust through the character's actions and shared choices, with a meaningful friendship outcome at every stage. Story milestones—not gift optimization—carry the character's project forward. Romance is an explicit, mutual possibility, not the reward for completing the project.

```text
[Stage 1: The Other Sleeper] --> [Stage 2: A Place to Gather] --> [Stage 3: What Is Worth Making]
                                                                                |
                   [Stage 5: A Home by Choice] <-- [Stage 4: Friendship or Romance]
```

### Stage 1: The Other Sleeper

- Alice has already left her pod and established a routine before meeting the player.
- Her first encounter introduces her through her present-day choices and concerns, not as a cryo-pod rescue or a mirror whose only purpose is to explain the player.
- Helping her is optional; a respectful refusal or a different approach can still begin a friendship.

### Stage 2: A Place to Gather

- The sleeper learns about Alice's ongoing creative or community project.
- The player may contribute practical help or attend, but Alice sets the project's purpose and the other participants decide what they want to share.
- The first visible result belongs to the group, not just to the player-character.

### Stage 3: What Is Worth Making

- A practical constraint—such as scarce materials, weather, or competing responsibilities—forces Alice to reconsider how to sustain the project.
- The conflict is not that creative work is foolish or that survival needs do not matter. Alice and the participants weigh what they can safely do and choose a workable path.
- The sleeper can offer help, raise a concern, or decline. They do not decide whether Alice abandons or continues the project.

### Stage 4: Friendship or Romance

- Only after mutual trust is established does Alice have a clear, explicit opportunity to express romantic interest.
- The sleeper can choose romance or friendship. Either choice preserves Alice's core story and community project.
- Alice may decline or set a boundary; gifts, pod history, rescue, or project completion do not substitute for consent.
- Dating scenes should reveal what this character wants from a relationship, rather than repeat Gage's or Cleo's scenes with a human in their place.

### Stage 5: A Home by Choice

- Long-term commitment, exclusivity, marriage terminology and breakup behavior follow the shared romance rules in [LoreAudit.md](../LoreAudit.md#l-31--romance-commitment-and-intimacy-are-not-defined-beyond-the-relationship-outline); they are not settled by this character sheet.
- If committed, Alice decides separately with the player whether to move to the farm. Commitment does not require relocation.
- Her project and other relationships continue regardless of romance or residence.

## 6. Partnership, Residence and Farm Presence

- Alice is not a farm worker and grants no farm-performance bonuses.
- If she chooses to live at the farm, her presence adds personal space, conversation and shared domestic scenes. It does not automate chores or change crop output, sale value, machine performance, or progression.
- If she keeps a separate home, the relationship continues through authored visits and scenes; living apart is not a lesser relationship.
- Alice's ongoing project is hers to continue, change, pause or end. The sleeper does not become its manager.

## 7. Story and Progression Boundaries

- Alice's project can add a small, optional community event or gathering once its location and scope are designed.
- No romance stage is required for the player's family story, town restoration, faction access, human population revelations, core tools, or farm progression.
- Alice's presence must not imply that every human survivor is romanceable or that she represents humanity as a whole.
- Do not connect this character to the living exhibit's abuse as a way to make the sleeper a rescuer or to accelerate romance.

## 8. Content and Implementation Notes

- This is a story and character proposal, not an implemented character asset. Do not invent a content ID, schedule system, or new romance framework in this document.
- Alice's status as a potential romance option is decided. Her full romance arc and relationship-specific milestones remain open; do not infer other characters' romance eligibility from pronouns, appearance, or human status.
- Keep the friendship and romance routes separate from any future recruited-worker or automation systems.
- The current direction for affectionate, non-explicit romance and multiple dating relationships is recorded provisionally in [LoreAudit.md](../LoreAudit.md); commitment rules still need a shared decision.

## 9. Key Event Outlines

These are outline beats only. Dialogue, exact triggers and calendar windows are not drafted.

1. **First meeting:** Alice is already active in the world and pursuing her own project when the sleeper encounters her.
2. **First gathering:** The player sees the project working through the contributions and choices of several people.
3. **Practical conflict:** Participants decide how to balance the project with an immediate constraint; the sleeper can help but does not choose for Alice or the group.
4. **Relationship choice:** After trust develops, the character offers an explicit friendship-or-romance choice. The project and core story do not depend on romance.
5. **Commitment and residence:** A later commitment choice follows the shared relationship rules. The player and Alice separately decide whether to share a home.

## 10. Seasonal and Festival Content

The gathering may appear at a seasonal event if Alice and the participants choose to take part. It should not become an essential festival service or a hidden deadline for relationship progression. Specific seasonal activities and dialogue are open until the project's medium is decided.
