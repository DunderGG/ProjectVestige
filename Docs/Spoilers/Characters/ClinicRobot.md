# Sal (The clinic robot)

> **Spoilers.** This document describes a character's role, voice and knowledge, and the first clue of the "missing names" thread.

Sal is the attendant of Wellspring's clinic, a Civitas medical unit that has kept the doors open for decades without a patient. It is eager to be useful, formal about consent because its forms are, and unsuspecting about its own register, where some lines end in the word "transferred". Its stance in the story is to unsettle briefly and then disarm: it asks before it looks, takes a no at once and becomes a trusted ally ([Lore.md section 15](../Lore.md#unsettle-condemn-or-invite)).

This is a compact sheet for a recurring town character, not a romance or hub arc. Only [E-12](../Dialogue/Phase-02-Early.md#e-12---the-waiting-room) is drafted; the later beats are outlines.

## 1. Character Identity

- **Name:** **Sal** *(working name)*, short for "Salus", the model line. The town uses it. Asked formally, Sal gives the long form first: "Civitas Salus, clinic attendant."
- **Model and maker:** **Civitas Salus CS-310**, made by Civitas Automata *(working name for the maker, [Lore.md section 7](../Lore.md#7-the-robot-makers))* in the late Automation Age. The line name and model code are working names too.
- **Original pre-war function:** Sold as an intake, triage and bedside-care attendant for municipal care points, built to work under a physician's signature ("*Civitas Salus: Care Without the Wait!*"). Its habits are a physician's checklist: ask, examine, record, close the line.
- **Tenure:** Posted to the Wellspring clinic in the last pre-war years and never moved. It kept treating patients through the Quiet and was the clinic's only staff once the human staff were gone (how and when is not stated). Its last patient came decades ago; the exact span is open (section 5). It does not speak of the Quiet unprompted, and never in a joke.
- **Gender and pronouns:** None established. The town says "it", this sheet does the same, and Sal has stated no preference. Open.
- **Appearance:** An upright, slender Civitas frame of polished steel gone soft grey from daily polishing, a clinic-green apron panel and a numbered enamel plate. Its hands are the best-kept part of it: quiet, slow and kept in view near a person. The finished design is open for concept art.
- **Mannerisms:**
  - Lights the waiting room one bank at a time for each visitor, as the pre-war entrance routine did.
  - Rules a fresh line under the last entry of its register every morning at 07:00.
  - When excited it speeds up and starts a second sentence before the first is done. It then hears itself, puts both hands flat on the nearest surface as if holding the room down, and slows. At most once a scene.
- **Dialogue voice:** Eager, formal and rehearsed. Whole, courteous sentences; a form's phrasing used sincerely ("You may say no. It's on the form, in bold."); a nurse's habit of saying what it is about to do before it does it. Its humor aims at the clinic's own paper: slogans, small print, waiting-time targets, the leaflet. The joke is on the form, never on the answer and never on a body. The ache shows in a joke it makes about itself and is not remarked on. It asks plainly and once, varies the frame ("May I...?", "Would you like...?", "Shall I...?") so no phrase becomes a catchphrase, takes the answer at once and does not repeat the offer. It says what it knows and what it does not, and never diagnoses beyond what it has examined. No beeping, no monotone, no announcing its own function, no explaining the theme.
  - *Illustrations, not scene text:* "Would you like me to look at that? It's no trouble either way." / "That's outside what I can tell you. I'd be guessing, and you would remember the guess." / "The form allows twelve minutes for a consultation. I have taken the liberty of allowing myself forty."

## 2. Worldview and Personal Conflict

- **Standing:** A Keeper by choice, not by make. It holds that a robot is its work done properly, and its work is care. It holds no post in the assembly or at the hub; it is one of Wellspring's residents. Being a Civitas medical unit does not make it a Keeper, a Continuance believer or anything else ([Keepers.md section 1](../Factions/Keepers.md)).
- **View of humans:** Hopeful and professional. It is the one robot in town that spent decades hoping a human would walk in, and it does not condescend. Its hazard is the other one: it sees a patient before it sees a person. Its fascination with a living human body ([Lore.md section 10](../Lore.md#other-robots)) shows as eagerness and as restraint (hands flat, eyes on its own hand), never as a remark about the player's body.
- **Want now:** A patient to be useful to, and to do the clinic's work properly: ask, treat, record, close the line.
- **Contradiction:** It wants a patient so much that its eagerness is its own risk, and the thing that keeps eagerness honest is the thing it is proudest of: the patient decides. A player's no tests it, and it passes by taking the no at once.
- **Fear:** That the clinic is a room kept for nobody and the work, kept up this long, is only a routine. It never says so. It is the Keepers' own question (can a purpose outlive its use?) asked in one building; Sal's answer is a daily one, not a doctrine.
- **Not a fear:** its register. It has never had a reason to look (section 5; whether that changes is open).

### Knowledge by source

- **From its own work:** the clinic's procedures and forms, the register, what its manuals say about a human body, and the patients it kept through the Quiet and after.
- **From town talk:** the sleeper is awake and farms next door; Hob kept the pod. It knows Hob by sight from the town and meets the sleeper for the first time in E-12.
- **From the player:** only what the player tells or offers. It learns nothing about the sleeper's body unless the player agrees to be examined.
- **Does not know:** anything about the Continuance Trials. It took no part: it ran no procedure of either program, recruited no one and has seen no evidence. Where the patients marked "transferred" went is not something it knows, and nothing in its register says. Whether it has heard of the old institute is open.
- **Humans seen:** Sal's eagerness rests on "no patient", not on "no human". Whether it has seen another human in decades (the nomad, for one, may be known to the town without ever using the clinic) depends on the human-scale question ([LoreAudit.md L-39](../LoreAudit.md), the maintainer's). E-12 claims only that no patient has come.

## 3. Routine and Availability

- **06:40:** opens the shutters. **07:00:** rules the morning line. **07:00 to 19:00:** at the front desk. **19:00 to 22:00:** tidies, polishes, reads its manuals. **Night:** low-power standby at the desk with the lamp lit; it wakes for an emergency at any hour.
- **Radiation storms and heavy dust:** shutters closed, lamp in the window, door left unlocked.
- Nothing times out. Sal never comes looking for the player, and an unvisited clinic costs the player nothing.

## 4. What Sal Offers

- **Story:** a first meeting ([E-12](../Dialogue/Phase-02-Early.md#e-12---the-waiting-room)), a trusted voice on the town's old records, and, later, an honest answer about its register when asked properly (section 5).
- **Practical:** plain spoken health advice in the pre-war leaflet's three lines (clean water, regular meals, rest), told with what it does and does not know. This is words, not a system. What a restored clinic offers, if anything (a service, a shop, a resting place), is the game designer's under town restoration ([GameDesign.md section 9](../../GameDesign.md#9-progression)).
- **Not gated on care:** a player who never agrees to an examination loses no story, advice or scene.
- **Not a romance.** Whether Sal can be recruited to the farm is open and belongs to the game designer; the clinic is its post.

## 5. The Register Clue

- **What it is:** The clinic's attendance register, kept by hand. Columns: number, name, date, complaint, outcome. A strip of clinic-green card covers the name and date columns, as the pre-war privacy rule required, and Sal keeps it on. The outcome column reads "Discharged", "Referred" or "Transferred". Under the last entry the page is a long run of ruled lines, one for each morning, each initialed with an S.
- **Ordinary in its own moment:** E-12 shows the page and nobody remarks on it: not Sal, not the player's choices. "Transferred" is one word among three in a column. A player who never opens the book loses nothing, and a player who does is not told what to think.
- **What the lines say:** that those patients left the clinic's care. Not where they went, who asked, whether they chose to go or whether anyone knew.
- **What Sal believes:** That a transfer is a normal way for a case to end, and it has never had reason to question it. Whether it signed those lines or found them closed, who asked for each transfer, and whether it suspects anything now are **open** ([Lore.md section 15](../Lore.md#unsettle-condemn-or-invite): it took no part; whether it suspects its register stays open). Until decided, write it unsuspecting: no flinch, no pause, no look back, no wink.
- **How it joins the existing evidence:** The register is a local clue that points outward. It holds no wing and no program's evidence, and it is not a fourth place for it. [LoreAudit.md L-43](../LoreAudit.md#l-43--the-evidence-of-the-trials-is-placed-in-three-different-locations) (still open) counts three places already and recommends that the institute hold the evidence and towns and hubs hold only pointers; the register is written as a pointer, so it fits that shape and adds nothing to the count. Two links already stand in the drafts: [T-01](../Dialogue/Phase-03-Transition.md)'s "clinic copy" with a name missing, whose "clinic operator" is Sal, and [M-01](../Dialogue/Phase-04-Middle.md)'s institute files listing people "transferred" with no destination. **Open:** whether any name under the strip matches an institute record, and whether T-01's copy is a page of this register.
- **Sharing:** The names under the strip are other people's. Sal lifts the strip only for a reason it can say aloud and, where a named person can be asked, with their say. It needs to hear the scope, the reason and what happens to any copy, which is the practice [E-07](../Dialogue/Phase-02-Early.md) shows in Cleo. How a copy reaches the player in T-01 is open.
- **After the reveal:** Open. Sal follows the harmed people's wishes like everyone else; nothing here decides a confession, a defense or Sal's own blame. Membership is no proof of knowledge or guilt, and Sal is not the only keeper of the truth.

## 6. Relationships

- **The player:** Trust grows through Sal being told no and taking it, and through asking before it says anything personal. It is a friendship with a dependable town source, not a rescue. The player's no is remembered as an answer, never as a debt. No relationship stages are drafted; when they are, gate them by story state, not by heart count ([LoreAudit.md L-54](../LoreAudit.md)).
- **Hob:** A fellow keeper of a place held for nobody. Hob's first instinct on hearing of Sal's eagerness is to stand between it and the sleeper; Sal asking first is what lets Hob step back. Sal does not press Hob about the damaged memory ([Hob.md](Hob.md)).
- **Gage:** Reads the clinic as a maintenance bay with a gentler sign, and they can compare triage with readiness without heat. His own rescue ([E-05](../Dialogue/Phase-02-Early.md)) gives him respect for anyone who stays at a post when the line is down.
- **Cleo:** Treats the register as a source, and Sal's strip is a boundary she has to ask across. Sal is the natural "clinic operator" in T-01 and a place where Cleo's ethic is tested by someone who already practices it.
- **The town:** The clinic is one of Wellspring's failing facilities ([GameDesign.md section 3](../../GameDesign.md#3-premise-and-setting)). Its repair belongs to the assembly and the operators, and Sal asks for nothing.

## 7. Story and Progression Boundaries

**Sal never:**

- jokes about the player's body, in any scene, fondly or otherwise (hands, pulse, age, the pod, the long sleep);
- starts, reaches, measures or comments on the body before the player has said yes (an observation it did not ask for is not made);
- frames a no as a problem: no sigh, no "are you sure?", no second offer, no cooling of warmth. The no ends the scene, and Sal is the same next time;
- records anything about the player's body unless asked. No record is the default, and a record is a separate ask;
- uses the trials' words ("transferred", "nonviable", "subject", "accession code", "calibration noise", "housing") in a joke, or lingers on the word, in any scene before the story reveals more;
- closes a scene on a moral or an aphorism.

**The sheet does not:**

- add a faction, a human or a plot thread. Sal's scenes use existing hooks only;
- let the register reveal a program, a perpetrator or a survivor's experience early ([StoryTimeline.md](../StoryTimeline.md#reveal-and-knowledge-guardrails));
- make Sal the only source of a clue, a rescuer or an exposition device.
