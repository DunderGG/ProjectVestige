# The second sleeper

> **Spoilers.** This is a provisional character concept and relationship outline. The character's name, identity, voice, history and final romance eligibility remain undecided.

The second sleeper is a possible human romance character: an adult who woke from a separate cryo pod and has since begun building a life of their own. Their independent project is a creative or community practice with value beyond survival. The sleeper may become a partner, but romance is not the reason this person exists, the solution to their survival, or the condition for their project to continue.

## 1. Character Identity

- **Name:** Undecided. Until a name is chosen, refer to them as **the second sleeper**.
- **Species and origin:** Human; awakened from a cryo pod separate from the player's. The pod's maker, location, condition, and the circumstances and date of awakening remain open.
- **Age:** An adult. Exact age and the amount of time spent awake remain open.
- **Gender and pronouns:** Undecided. The use of they/them in this document is a temporary writing convention, not a decision about identity or attraction.
- **Role:** A self-directed survivor with a possible romance arc. They are not a representative of all humans, a source of essential progression, or a farmhand by default.
- **Appearance and field gear:** Open. Their design should reflect the life they have made since waking, not simply repeat the sleeper's cryo-pod appearance.
- **Mannerisms and dialogue voice:** Reserved for the maintainer to decide. Do not infer their voice from being human, their creative project, or their relationship to the sleeper. No in-character dialogue has been drafted.

## 2. Worldview and Personal Conflict

- **Standing:** An individual human survivor. Their existence does not establish a nearby human settlement or resolve how many people survived worldwide.
- **View of the sleeper:** The shared experience of waking into a changed world may create understanding, but does not make them automatic friends or romantic partners. They have their own history and boundaries.
- **View of robots:** Not settled. Their views should grow from their own encounters, rather than restating the sleeper's perspective or making them a spokesperson for human-robot relations.
- **Want:** To sustain a recurring creative or community practice that gives people a reason to gather beyond immediate survival. The medium, location, participants and specific outcome remain open.
- **Contradiction:** A possible starting conflict is that they judge their own project by whether it is practically useful, even while wanting to make room for something whose value is shared experience. This is a writing direction to confirm, not final characterization.
- **Independent agency:** The sleeper may contribute materials, practical help or a perspective, but does not own the project, choose its purpose, or make it succeed on the character's behalf. The character and willing participants choose what happens to it.
- **Relationships outside the sleeper:** To be developed. Their life must include concerns and connections that continue on the friendship path and are not merely waiting for the sleeper to arrive.

## 3. Routine and Availability

- The place where the character lives and is first encountered is undecided. They should already be awake and making their own way when the sleeper meets them; the meeting is not a rescue from a sealed pod.
- Their visits and story events should be predictable enough to follow without requiring the player to catch a random appearance.
- Severe weather can delay travel or gatherings, but should not permanently cancel a relationship event or punish the player for missing a day.
- A detailed daily schedule should wait until the character's location and the relevant maps are decided.

## 4. Community Project and Gifts

### Community Project

The provisional project is a recurring, voluntary gathering where people can make or share something together. Its exact form—such as a performance, craft, or another creative practice—is open. It should welcome human and robot contributions without suggesting that creativity belongs to humans alone. Sharing a story, making an object, or attending should never require a participant to disclose private history.

The gathering is optional character content, not a town service, human hub, or requirement for farm or story progression. It can continue and change whether the sleeper chooses friendship or romance.

### Item Preferences and Gifting

Specific likes, dislikes and gift dialogue are undecided until the project's form and the character's voice are chosen. Gifts should remain optional social gestures: they do not earn access to private history, consent, romance, or the community project.

## 5. Relationship Progression and Narrative Arc

The arc should establish trust through the character's actions and shared choices, with a meaningful friendship outcome at every stage. Story milestones—not gift optimization—carry the character's project forward. Romance is an explicit, mutual possibility, not the reward for completing the project.

```text
[Stage 1: The Other Sleeper] --> [Stage 2: A Place to Gather] --> [Stage 3: What Is Worth Making]
                                                                                |
                   [Stage 5: A Home by Choice] <-- [Stage 4: Friendship or Romance]
```

### Stage 1: The Other Sleeper

- The character has already left their pod and established a routine before meeting the player.
- Their first encounter introduces them through their present-day choices and concerns, not as a cryo-pod rescue or a mirror whose only purpose is to explain the player.
- Helping them is optional; a respectful refusal or a different approach can still begin a friendship.

### Stage 2: A Place to Gather

- The sleeper learns about the character's ongoing creative or community project.
- The player may contribute practical help or attend, but the character sets the project's purpose and the other participants decide what they want to share.
- The first visible result belongs to the group, not just to the player-character.

### Stage 3: What Is Worth Making

- A practical constraint—such as scarce materials, weather, or competing responsibilities—forces the character to reconsider how to sustain the project.
- The conflict is not that creative work is foolish or that survival needs do not matter. The character and participants weigh what they can safely do and choose a workable path.
- The sleeper can offer help, raise a concern, or decline. They do not decide whether the character abandons or continues the project.

### Stage 4: Friendship or Romance

- Only after mutual trust is established does the character have a clear, explicit opportunity to express romantic interest.
- The sleeper can choose romance or friendship. Either choice preserves the character's core story and community project.
- The character may decline or set a boundary; gifts, pod history, rescue, or project completion do not substitute for consent.
- Dating scenes should reveal what this character wants from a relationship, rather than repeat Gage's or Cleo's scenes with a human in their place.

### Stage 5: A Home by Choice

- Long-term commitment, exclusivity, marriage terminology and breakup behavior follow the shared romance rules in [LoreAudit.md](../LoreAudit.md#l-31--romance-commitment-and-intimacy-are-not-defined-beyond-the-relationship-outline); they are not settled by this character sheet.
- If committed, the character decides separately with the player whether to move to the farm. Commitment does not require relocation.
- Their project and other relationships continue regardless of romance or residence.

## 6. Partnership, Residence and Farm Presence

- The character is not a farm worker and grants no farm-performance bonuses.
- If they choose to live at the farm, their presence adds personal space, conversation and shared domestic scenes. It does not automate chores or change crop output, sale value, machine performance, or progression.
- If they keep a separate home, the relationship continues through authored visits and scenes; living apart is not a lesser relationship.
- The character's ongoing project is theirs to continue, change, pause or end. The sleeper does not become its manager.

## 7. Story and Progression Boundaries

- The character's project can add a small, optional community event or gathering once its location and scope are designed.
- No romance stage is required for the player's family story, town restoration, faction access, human population revelations, core tools, or farm progression.
- The character's presence must not imply that every human survivor is romanceable or that this one person represents humanity as a whole.
- Do not connect this character to the living exhibit's abuse as a way to make the sleeper a rescuer or to accelerate romance.

## 8. Content and Implementation Notes

- This is a story and character proposal, not an implemented character asset. Do not invent a content ID, schedule system, or new romance framework in this document.
- When character content is implemented, eligibility and story events should be authored per character. Do not infer romance eligibility from pronouns, appearance, or human status.
- Keep the friendship and romance routes separate from any future recruited-worker or automation systems.
- The current direction for affectionate, non-explicit romance and multiple dating relationships is recorded provisionally in [LoreAudit.md](../LoreAudit.md); commitment rules still need a shared decision.

## 9. Key Event Outlines

These are outline beats only. Dialogue, exact triggers, calendar windows and the character's voice are not drafted.

1. **First meeting:** The character is already active in the world and pursuing their own project when the sleeper encounters them.
2. **First gathering:** The player sees the project working through the contributions and choices of several people.
3. **Practical conflict:** Participants decide how to balance the project with an immediate constraint; the sleeper can help but does not choose for them.
4. **Relationship choice:** After trust develops, the character offers an explicit friendship-or-romance choice. The project and core story do not depend on romance.
5. **Commitment and residence:** A later commitment choice follows the shared relationship rules. The player and character separately decide whether to share a home.

## 10. Seasonal and Festival Content

The gathering may appear at a seasonal event if the character and participants choose to take part. It should not become an essential festival service or a hidden deadline for relationship progression. Specific seasonal activities and dialogue are open until the project's medium and character voice are decided.
