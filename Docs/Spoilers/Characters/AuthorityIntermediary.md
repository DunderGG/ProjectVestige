# Morrow

> **Spoilers.** This is a provisional character concept for Morrow, a human-origin person whose current body is entirely mechanical beneath a convincing human exterior. The Authority hides Morrow's connection to it and uses them to recruit human victims. Their pronouns, history, exact transformation and evolving understanding of the program remain open.

The Authority continually manipulates Morrow's information and assignments to serve its changing needs. How much Morrow understands, what choices they have, and how they respond can evolve over time. Their covert role does not define the whole person or settle their boundaries, loyalties and future.

## 1. Character Identity

- **Name:** Morrow. Whether this is their personal name, a chosen nickname or a public identity remains open.
- **Species and origin:** Human in origin. Morrow's current body is entirely mechanical, with no organic interior. They accepted the conversion believing it would address a serious survival need, but did not agree to become a recruiter. The need, procedure and life before the Authority found them remain open.
- **Age, gender and pronouns:** They are an adult and retain a youthful adult appearance. Their exact age, gender and pronouns remain open.
- **Role:** A covert Authority recruiter. Morrow passes as human and hides their connection to the Authority while drawing other humans into its program as victims. The exact methods and what Morrow understands at each point are shaped by the Authority's ongoing manipulation and remain open.
- **Body and presentation:** Morrow is indistinguishable from an ordinary human in appearance and ordinary interaction, with a youthful adult exterior that changes little over time. Under that human exterior, the body is entirely mechanical. The exterior can be maintained or replaced without implying biological agelessness. How close technical examination can detect the machinery, how the body functions, and how Morrow experiences it remain open. This is not Reed's social concealment. Do not assume a copied mind, effortless maintenance or a pain-free transformation.
- **Relationship to the Continuance Trials:** This person belongs to the Authority's separate program. They are not the Index's living exhibit and their alteration is not an Iron Creed frame conversion.
- **Dialogue voice:** Measured, attentive and quietly reassuring. Morrow listens closely, asks grounded follow-up questions, avoids promises they cannot control, and may use occasional understated humor. Their cadence and wording should sound fully human; uncertainty can emerge through careful rephrasing, not mechanical tells. Do not make every conversation a recruiter pitch or about their body or past. No dialogue has been drafted.

## 2. Worldview and Personal Conflict

- **Standing:** A distinct human survivor whose body has been altered for the Authority's purposes. Their existence does not establish a hidden human network or show that the Authority can find every survivor.
- **Knowledge of the program:** At first, Morrow believes they are guiding people toward safety or help. The Authority hides the program's coercive purpose and continually changes the information and rationale it gives them. What evidence changes Morrow's understanding, and when, remains open; do not make permanent ignorance or complete complicity the default.
- **Relationship to the assigned role:** Morrow is meant to recruit victims, but their degree of cooperation, refusal or negotiation can change as their understanding changes. The Authority manipulates them; it does not remotely control their personality or erase their choices.
- **How the role began:** The Authority presented Morrow's conversion as a way to address a serious survival need; the recruitment role was not part of what Morrow agreed to. The Authority later assigned that work and continues to manipulate the information around it.
- **Core conflict:** The Authority treats their altered body and assigned work as proof that a person can be made useful to machine rule. The character's own view of their body, work and future must be authored separately; neither gratitude nor hatred is automatic.
- **Independent want:** Morrow wants to build a life with at least one part that is not assigned by the Authority: time, a relationship or a choice that is theirs for its own sake. This does not automatically mean escape, rebellion or abandoning the Authority.
- **View of the Authority:** Open. They may distinguish particular people from the system, distrust its founders, rely on some resources it provides, or hold conflicting views. Do not reduce them to a simple defector or loyal agent.
- **Agency:** They decide what to disclose about themselves, whether to contact other humans, what information to share and what response they want. The player cannot consent on their behalf or make them a source of names and locations.
- **Relationship with the sleeper:** Trust is not owed because both are human. The character can decline questions or help, and their story should remain understandable if they choose privacy or no further contact.

## 3. Routine and Availability

- Their location, public identity, work and routine are undecided. Do not create a new city map or Authority hub just to accommodate them.
- Introduce them only in the later, optional Authority thread, after the Creed and Index trials have had their separate middle-story reveal. They are not an early clue, a boss encounter or a prerequisite for understanding either story.
- Morrow's human appearance should not be an identity puzzle the player solves through visual tells. The later reveal concerns their mechanical nature and concealed Authority role, not an obvious mismatch.
- Their stable youthful adult appearance is a clue about the machine exterior, not proof of biological agelessness.
- Their recruitment work should be limited by specific leads, risks and other people's choices. They are not a directory to survivors, a replacement for the nomad's private contact circuit, or proof of the Authority's reach everywhere.

## 4. The Intermediary Role

The Authority's intended use is to recruit human victims into its program. Morrow's affiliation and purpose are hidden; they can approach targets under a human identity. Morrow initially believes they are guiding people toward safety or help, while the Authority hides the coercive purpose. The exact method, the promises Morrow is told to make, and how the Authority alters its account of each assignment are open. Do not assume every approach succeeds or that Morrow controls what happens after a victim is recruited.

The Authority's program uses manipulated tests and imposed service roles to justify machine rule. This character is one person affected by that program, not proof that its conclusions are true. Their testimony can provide a limited perspective; it cannot establish every founder's order, every subject's experience or the whole scope of the organization.

### Contact, consent and risk

- A lead about a human is not permission to approach, identify or disclose them.
- Other humans can refuse contact or decline to join any program. The intermediary's role does not override that choice.
- Do not expose a survivor's location, identity or route as an automatic result of befriending the intermediary.
- If they knowingly helped the Authority, assess their responsibility through what they knew, what choices they had and what they did. Do not absolve them automatically, or make coercion proof that they lost all agency.
- They may choose to continue, refuse or redefine their role. None of those outcomes should be selected for them by the sleeper.

## 5. Story Progression and Personal Arc

This outline is provisional. It centers the character's choices, not a redemption, recruitment or escape outcome.

```text
[Stage 1: A Human Stranger] --> [Stage 2: The Hidden Assignment] --> [Stage 3: Changing Accounts]
                                                                            |
                    [Stage 5: A Future They Choose] <-- [Stage 4: Boundaries and Contact]
```

### Stage 1: A Human Stranger

- The player first meets Morrow as an apparently ordinary human in the later, optional Authority thread.
- Their human presentation is convincing; do not foreshadow the truth with generic robotic mannerisms or uncanny physical tells.
- The scene establishes a capable person with a life beyond the Authority's assignment, not a spectacle or a helpless victim waiting to be rescued.

### Stage 2: The Hidden Assignment

- Independent evidence connects Morrow's recruitment work to the Authority's human program.
- The evidence establishes only what its sources can support. It does not settle what Morrow knew, whether they consented, or what they did in a particular contact.
- The player can learn about the program without forcing the character to testify.

### Stage 3: Changing Accounts

- As the Authority shifts its needs, it changes what Morrow is told about targets and the purpose of assignments.
- If Morrow chooses to speak, they can describe their experience and the limits of what they know at that time.
- Their account may be partial or shaped by manipulation. Corroboration should not turn them into an unreliable-person twist.
- They are not required to reveal other subjects, identify founders or solve the Authority mystery.

### Stage 4: Boundaries and Contact

- The character decides whether they want to approach other humans, share a lead, pause contact or refuse the Authority's task.
- Any contact is handled with the affected person's consent and safety in mind; one person's choice does not authorize disclosure about another.
- The sleeper can offer support, ask what the character wants, or step back. The sleeper does not choose the character's next role.

### Stage 5: A Future They Choose

- The character's later life remains open. They may remain connected to the Authority, leave its service, negotiate a different role or choose another path.
- No single ending proves the Authority has been defeated or every subject has been found.
- Disclosure, friendship, accountability and any future relationship are distinct choices. The character need not reveal everything or reconcile with anyone for their story to have a meaningful outcome.

## 6. Relationships and Romance

- No romance eligibility or relationship type is decided. Do not make romance the reward for discovering the character's humanity or supporting them against the Authority.
- A friendship path should be complete if one is developed. Boundaries and privacy remain valid outcomes.
- Do not connect this character to Reed's social concealment, make Reed the intermediary, or revise Reed's character sheet through this concept.
- Do not assume a personal relationship with the Index's living exhibit, the nomad, Alice or the sleeper's sibling.

## 7. Story and Progression Boundaries

- This is a distinct possible human survivor, not a commitment that they join the initial release roster. The existing target of four to six human survivors still needs a roster decision.
- Their appearance is optional and comes after the middle-story reveal of the Iron Creed and Index programs.
- They do not unlock the Authority's founders, expose every cell, identify every human or resolve the public factions' response.
- Do not use their body as horror spectacle, a gameplay upgrade, a sign that they are less human, or shorthand for perfect obedience.
- Do not invent a transformation system, character asset ID, schedule, map or new relationship framework here.

## 8. Open Decisions

- Whether Morrow is their personal name, a chosen nickname or a public identity; their gender, pronouns, exact age and public identity.
- Their life before the program, the survival need Morrow believed the conversion would address, the details of the procedure and how the Authority found them.
- The exterior's construction, mechanical capabilities and limits, care needs and relationship to Morrow's sense of self.
- How Morrow's human identity and consciousness persist in a fully mechanical body; do not assume a copy or transfer.
- How long the exterior has retained its youthful appearance.
- What evidence changes Morrow's understanding of the Authority's goals, when they recognize the harm, and what choices they make as that understanding changes.
- How Morrow's wish for an unassigned life takes shape, which relationships matter to them, and what they choose to do next.
- The method and limits of their contact work, and what risks it creates for them and for other humans.
- Their location, routine, first appearance and place in the later optional story thread.
- Their relationship type, if any, with the sleeper and other characters.
- Whether they are included in the initial four-to-six human cast and which other optional survivor concepts fit the release roster.
