# Reed

> **Spoilers.** This is a provisional character concept for a man in the planned release roster and a romance option. His pronouns and public identity remain undecided.

The disguised survivor is a human man who has spent years living in a robot city, concealing his humanity through clothing, mannerisms and an adopted public identity. He has built a life there before meeting the player. His concealment is social, not a full robot costume or an unexplained robotic body. He is romanceable, but romance remains an explicit, mutual choice and is not a reward for learning his secret.

## 1. Character Identity

- **Name:** Reed. Whether this is his birth name, a nickname or the public identity he uses in the city remains open.
- **Species and origin:** Human. He has lived in a robot city for years; how he arrived and where he lived before remain open.
- **Age:** An adult. Exact age and the amount of time spent in the city remain open.
- **Gender:** Man.
- **Pronouns:** Undecided. This sheet uses he/him as a temporary writing convention, not a decision about pronouns.
- **Role:** A self-directed city resident and romance option in the planned release roster. His work, home and place in the city remain open; he is not a guide to all humans, a source of essential progression, or a rescue target.
- **Appearance and clothing:** He dresses in locally ordinary, practical clothing that supports his public identity. The specific style and any distinguishing personal details remain open. He does not wear a full robot costume.
- **Mannerisms and public identity:** His presentation reflects habits he has practiced to blend into city life. Avoid treating robots as a single mannerism or making him imitate a caricature of a machine. What identity he uses and how he maintains it remain open.
- **Dialogue voice:** Personable, attentive and quietly direct. Use a natural, unhurried cadence and everyday, concrete wording. Gentle, situational humor is welcome; let him express affection or discomfort plainly when it matters. He can set a boundary or decline a question simply, without becoming cryptic or shutting down the conversation. Avoid robot catchphrases, conspicuous "human slip-ups," clue-dropping and making every exchange about concealment. Let his eventual work and interests inform his vocabulary, not assumptions about humans. No in-character dialogue has been drafted.

## 2. Worldview and Personal Conflict

- **Standing:** An individual human living in a robot city. His existence does not establish a hidden human community or show that humans are common there.
- **Want:** To keep a life he has made in the city and decide for himself how he is known. His specific work, interests and ambitions need to be established before the concept is finalized; concealment alone is not a complete character goal.
- **Possible contradiction:** The habits that protect his privacy can make ordinary closeness tiring. This is a direction to explore, not a fixed personality trait or a reason he must eventually reveal himself.
- **View of robots:** Not settled. His experience should be specific to people and institutions he knows, not a universal judgment about robots.
- **View of the sleeper:** He does not owe the sleeper his history or a confession. Trust may grow through ordinary, reciprocal interactions and respect for boundaries.
- **Independent agency:** The player does not expose him, decide whether he stays in the city, or determine what his public identity means. His life and relationships continue on the friendship path.
- **Relationships outside the sleeper:** He already has a routine and connections in the city. Who knows him, and what those relationships mean, remain open.

## 3. Routine and Availability

- He should be encountered through story and exploration while already going about his own life, not in captivity, distress or need of rescue.
- His work, usual location, home and daily schedule are undecided. Define these only after the city setting and his role there are established.
- Relationship events should be authored and findable; privacy should not depend on the player missing a random appearance or failing to identify clues.
- Do not assume a new city map or faction hub for this character. His setting and scope must fit the planned locations or be considered separately.

## 4. Concealment, Identity and Gifts

### Concealment

Clothing, mannerisms and an adopted public identity are the selected broad approach to blending in. The practical details—how his identity works, what makes the concealment plausible, who knows or suspects, and what risks it creates—remain open.

The player should not be asked to solve his identity as a puzzle. A discovery, if any, is not a prize for noticing clues, and disclosure is not owed as payment for friendship, gifts or romance. He chooses what to share, with whom and when. Keeping his humanity private can remain a valid outcome.

### Item Preferences and Gifting

Specific likes, dislikes and gift dialogue are undecided. Establish preferences from his chosen work and interests, not from assumptions about his secret. Gifts are optional gestures; they do not earn access to private information, consent, romance or his city life.

## 5. Relationship Progression and Narrative Arc

The outline below is provisional and focuses on trust and self-determination. Friendship should provide a complete relationship path. Romance is an explicit, mutual possibility; neither his disclosure nor the player's understanding of his concealment earns or confirms a relationship.

```text
[Stage 1: A Familiar Face] --> [Stage 2: Ordinary Trust] --> [Stage 3: A Boundary]
                                                                  |
                   [Stage 5: A Life on His Terms] <-- [Stage 4: What to Share]
```

### Stage 1: A Familiar Face

- The player meets him while he is carrying out an ordinary part of his established routine.
- The encounter does not frame him as a mystery to solve, an unusual specimen or an emergency.
- His public identity is accepted at face value; the player is not prompted to investigate him.

### Stage 2: Ordinary Trust

- Repeated interactions show him as a person with interests, work and relationships beyond concealment.
- Trust grows through reciprocal, visible choices rather than gifts or accumulating clues.
- The player may accept a boundary or decline an offer without losing access to essential story progress.

### Stage 3: A Boundary

- A conversation or situation gives the player a chance to ask for more personal information than he wants to share.
- He can decline plainly. The player can respect that boundary, ask what he is comfortable discussing, or end the conversation.
- If the player oversteps, the story should make the harm understandable and allow a sincere repair; do not use a hidden permanent penalty or make the survivor's friendship a puzzle reward.

### Stage 4: What to Share

- If he chooses, he may tell the player privately that he is human and explain some part of his public identity.
- The disclosure is his choice, not a forced reveal, public unmasking or conclusion the player earns.
- Choosing not to disclose does not invalidate the friendship or block the survivor's personal story.
- Because he is a romance option, the arc should include a clear, mutual opportunity to choose romance, separate from disclosure. Its scene and timing remain undecided.

### Stage 5: A Life on His Terms

- He decides what his relationships and public identity look like going forward. He need not make a public announcement or leave the city for the arc to resolve.
- Friendship remains meaningful whether or not he shares more of his history.
- Any commitment, exclusivity, breakup and residence rules follow the shared romance design; this sheet does not settle them.

## 6. Partnership, Residence and Farm Presence

- He is not a farm worker and grants no farm-performance bonuses.
- His wishes about residence are undecided. Do not assume that a relationship requires relocation or that living at the farm is safer or better for him.
- If he ever chooses to visit or live at the farm, his presence adds character and domestic content only; it does not automate chores or change crop output, sale value, machine performance or progression.
- A relationship must not require him to abandon the city life he has chosen.

## 7. Story and Progression Boundaries

- His identity is not a key to a human settlement, faction, city system or essential progression.
- The player does not out him, decide when he discloses, or use romance to change his public status.
- Avoid making him a secret-agent archetype, an informant, or a representative of all humans unless those directions are separately chosen and justified.
- Do not connect this character to the living exhibit's abuse as a way to make the sleeper a rescuer or to accelerate romance.
- His story should be distinct from the second sleeper's creative or community project, the nomad's travel and trade circuit, and the engineer's technical role.

## 9. Key Event Outlines

These are outline beats only. Dialogue, exact triggers, location, the survivor's occupation and voice are not drafted.

1. **First meeting:** The player encounters him in his ordinary city routine; no emergency or identity puzzle is involved.
2. **Everyday connection:** He shares a small part of his interests or work, independent of the player's help.
3. **Boundary conversation:** He declines a personal question; respecting the boundary preserves the friendship path.
4. **Optional disclosure:** If chosen, he shares his humanity privately and on his terms. The friendship does not require this event.
5. **Relationship choice:** A clear mutual invitation to romance is separate from disclosure and from any decision about residence.
